# Lower64 / 12D: two exact structural barriers for prospective 842

No new lower bound. The known 841 is Takhanov–Assylbekov–Yun [arXiv:2606.18984v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18984v2), with the structural explanation [Takhanov, arXiv:2609.09179](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09179v1). The known 841 does not fix the canonical 816 exactly. This work concerns specific quaternionic frames and does not rule out 842.

## Common exact geometry

Let C=24 Hurwitz directions; D=τC=24 signed pair-coordinate directions. Each is three orthonormal bases up to sign (C_r,D_r for r=0,1,2). Across distinct bases within one type every absolute dot is 1/2; C/D dots are zero or 1/√2. Takhanov's two canonical 384-point mixed families have block direction types D,C,C and C,D,D with respective weights (1/√2,1/2,1/2), and (1/2,1/√2,1/2), all eight signs and quaternionic XOR relation. Each source is 3 × 16 × 8. The two first axial C cells are kept; E3 may be the axial C or D cell, both give 840 with canonical mixed stock. Math below uses that underlying factor basis explicitly, not approximate 841 coordinates.

## A. Exact 840 ceiling with one canonical mixed family frozen, even without antipodal symmetry

Orient E3=C when freezing M1, flexing M2; orient E3=D when freezing M2, flexing M1. The alphabet model includes every oriented vector of either (i) a weight permutation of (1/√2,1/2,1/2) using arbitrary C∪D block directions, or (ii) a permutation of (1/√2,1/√2,0) with C∪D for both nonzero blocks, always with all three axial cells and the frozen mixed family present. Full orientations are permitted and points from the other family can be arbitrarily omitted/replaced. Axis constraints restrict directions in weight-1/√2 blocks to the opposite alphabet. The portable exact checker enumerates every permitted donor; after requiring compatibility with the frozen family, in each orientation there are 1,344 distinct directed points. Dots have denominator 16 with numerator a+b√2; signs tested in integers, no threshold tolerance.

There are 384 matched flexible slots indexed by canonical unsigned triple and signed orientation. Each slot has exactly two alphabet states: keep its original weight assignment, or shift the heavy weight to third; these states conflict with one another. The 768 first-type admitted points are exactly these states. Both states of distinct slots actually coexist across all possibilities; within an unsigned triple, flipping at least one orientation gives maximum 1/√2−1/4<1/2 for mixed states. The other 576 alphabet points are (b/√2,c/√2) with zero in the original light body block, weights on the flexible family's old-heavy and third blocks, and fixed signed pure directions b,c in the two permitted 24-cells. Each such point blocks both states of two slots if b,c are in corresponding basis classes, or 16 slots if they aren't (global exact census). Here is a short charging proof, independent of global census details.

Fix signed b. At most eight mutually compatible two-block donors can share b, since their unit c-directions must be pairwise nonpositive (orthoplex bound in R4). In the original signed b-class, the 16 slots use every signed c in the corresponding basis twice, once per sign of the first light coordinate. A new two-block point whose c projects positively on one of these signed c coordinates by >1−1/√2 conflicts with both states of the two corresponding slots: its dot with the original is 1/2 + (dot c)/(2√2), and with the new state is 1/(2√2) + (dot c)/2. If one admitted donor's c comes from a different basis, its four positive signed projections are all 1/2, giving ≥8 blocked slots in this very b-class, sufficient to charge the whole b-group. Otherwise each donor hits its own signed c with dot1 and disjoint blocked slots. The signed b-classes are disjoint, so adding any subset of compatible two-block points cannot more than replace the flexible slots they render unusable. This proves 840 inside the stated full alphabet model (not continuous body directions or both mixed families varied).

## B. A structural failure of two-family E8 orthoplex co-design for the third-heavy D donor with C,C lights

The intended improvement is 840→844: keep all three axial C cells, retain two full 384-point mixed families but allow their light frames to change jointly, then add one four-point orbit with heavy direction d∈D in block three (weight 1/√2) and two arbitrary direction letters in C with weights 1/2. Within each retained family keep the old-heavy block (one for family1, two for family2) and all signed D heavy directions at weight 1/√2. Each projective heavy line is assigned a full orthonormal light basis (8 axes in its complementary 8D), with independent signs of heavy and whole light, for 32 per heavy line. This orthogonality is necessary for its saturated signed-heavy class by Rankin. Candidate E8 light lines are either a pure block axis of weight1 in the normalized light, or two equally weighted letters in C∪D, each amplitude 1/√2; each light 8-frame is taken from an E8-style uniform alphabet glue so that within that 8-frame the third-block letter types are all C or all D on the mixed lines. Orientation, glue permutation, and actual light basis may change. The following covers all 144 prepared d∈D0 direction cases and in fact every d in D, all a_e,b_e∈C. It does not cover arbitrary continuously rotated mixed directions or mixed C/D letters within one light frame's third block.

1. Divide all physical vectors by their common two-factor scale: normalized light v_i=(b_i,c_i), opposing w_j=(a'_j,c'_j); opposed old heavy directions are u_1,u_2. Their actual angle condition, after maximizing over the four independent signs and multiplying by 2, is `p_i+q_j+|<c_i,c'_j>|≤1` with p_i=|<u_2,b_i>| and q_j=|<u_1,a'_j>|. For every full opposing 8-frame, sum_j q_j²=1, so max q_j≥1/√8. A nonzero D direction in another heavy's light coordinate can be matched by that opponent's available D heavy line, giving p_i≥1/√2, impossible as their sum exceeds 1. Pure axes on that non-third block, if using C, are already forbidden by the fixed axial C cell; those using D were just excluded. Therefore the only nonzero directions on that block are balanced mixed C, squared norm1/2 per mixed light row. Trace over the full light orthonormal basis equals 4 in that block, so all 8 rows must be mixed (no pure third line either).
2. Fix any old light frame and an opposing heavy u_2∈D. Since its light b_i directions are C with amplitude 1/√2, p_i is 0 or 1/2, exactly four highs by Bessel. Opponent analogously has four q-high rows. High-vs-high forces mutual orthogonality of the third projections. Each of the four-vector third covariance matrices has trace2 and is ≤I; their ranges orthogonal, each rank at least2, so each is a rank-two orthogonal projector. Consequently the full 8D rank-four projector onto that high subset is block diagonal: if its third principal block is F=F², the third block of the full projector identity forces the squared norm of the off-diagonal block zero. These full projectors for all opposing D heavies commute because they project onto subsets of one light orthonormal basis. Their block compressions therefore commute as well, and every b_i is a simultaneous eigenvector with eigenvalue pattern given by its C/D overlap signature. The 12 projective C directions have distinct signatures over the 12 D directions (axes see both signs on a pair containing the axis; sign halves see one per pair and the pattern determines their projective signs). Different signature directions are orthogonal. Thus b_i all belong to one C basis; each appears twice by Bessel. Their paired c_i must be the same third line with opposite relative sign, and third lines for different b choices are orthogonal. So every frame reduces to a matching permutation between one C basis and one third basis.
3. At each of the three old D heavy basis classes there are two strict heavy choices with overlap 1/2 with the donor's normalized opposite C light letter; pick one strict frame in each. The third donor compatibility leaves budget 1/2 for the two remaining normalized block contributions in each row. If the frame's non-third C basis differs from that donor's light basis, its four dots with the donor's corresponding light coefficient are all 1/4. But the matched third basis contains a line with abs dot ≥1/2 with the donor's unit heavy d, yielding ≥1/(2√2)>1/4 in that term. Thus the non-third C basis of every strict frame is exactly the donor's light basis. If the strict third basis is D, then it cannot be d's D basis (one contribution is 1/√2>1/2); if it is a different D basis all contributions are 1/(2√2), and the row matching the donor's non-third C letter, which already spends 1/2, fails. So all strict third bases are C. Against one another, strict frames in different old D heavy basis classes have normalized light dot budget ≤1/2. Two different third C bases have every absolute unit dot 1/2; for matching same b line they give 1/2+1/4>1/2 when optimizing independent relative signs, so the three strict frames must use the same third C basis. The special b index matching the donor's C light has to map to a third label orthogonal to d; only two of the four labels are orthogonal. The images must be distinct across distinct heavy basis classes, otherwise their light lines coincide and dot1. Three frames and two allowed labels is impossible.

Separately, each original family by itself had a feasible collection of three disjoint donor-compatible E8 frames across twelve glue types for each of the 144 representatives, so the obstruction genuinely uses cross-family saturation rather than denying individual frame capacity. Scope: no rank-7 old classes, arbitrary light rotations, mixed C/D within a third light frame, altered heavy weights, or off-alphabet donor. An A8 regular-simplex 9-line light class could increase one signed heavy occupancy from 16 to 18 at heavy squared weight 3/7; its simplest E8 star placement fails axial caps. For next revisit, go off standard light-frame assumptions rather than another old alphabet search.

No native jobs this round. Hosted discrete diagnostic: all 28,224 C/D third-heavy four-point donor shapes against canonical mixed stock have ≥2 old four-point blockers; the 288 cheapest replace one canonical 8-orbit with two matching phases, neutral only. The local attempted 844 by replacing whole 32-point second-heavy class with a 28-point real rank-seven light frame plus 8 donor points failed numerically; in the exact C/D alphabet all 48 cases have just 9 legal light lines and orthogonal capacity 6. This numeral is not a proof against real off-alphabet rank7.

## C. Stronger late result: permit arbitrary mixing of C and D inside each third four-frame

The commuting-projector reduction B.1–B.2 did not use uniform third alphabet type. Keep the saturated old heavy classes, arbitrary allowed tetrahedral/octahedral light letters, balanced nonzero light weights, and proposed third-heavy (C,C,D) four-orbit. Rotate each physical quaternion block independently by left multiplication by a member of C. This permutes C and D (and their frame classes), preserves the fixed axial C cells, maps the donor's two C light letters to C0 coordinate 0, and maps its D heavy d to D0 label0. Thus all prepared representatives and all 12 D heavy lines are equivalent in this co-design model.

Each of the three D heavy frame classes contains a strict heavy direction. It sees the donor's opposite light with overlap 1/2 under B.3's normalization; its non-third light C basis must therefore be C0 by the same trace/Bessel argument. Write a matched frame as an ordered array X=(X0,X1,X2,X3) of its third projective letters, aligned to non-third C0 coordinates 0 through 3. It cannot contain d (one third contribution would then exceed 1/2), and X0 must be orthogonal to d (its non-third term already spends the full remaining 1/2). Two such X in different D heavy classes must have X_i ⟂ X'_i for each i, as the matching non-third letters already spend half of the allowed light inner product. These conditions are necessary even when every third frame contains both C and D letters.

The exact 24-line C∪D orthogonality graph has precisely 24 four-cliques (3 all C, 3 all D, 18 mixed two-and-two). There are 144 allowed ordered rows X. Columnwise orthogonality leaves 72 unordered triples for the three strict source classes, hence 432 triples when assigned to classes r=0,1,2. For strict class r the two non-third coordinates that overlap its own D heavy direction are {0,k_r}, with (k0,k1,k2)=(1,2,3). If the three first-side rows are X_r, an opposite strict class s with row Y requires spans of {X_r[0],X_r[k_s]} and {Y[0],Y[k_r]} to be orthogonal for all r, by the high-vs-high cross condition. Exact graph check finds no Y for any s and any of the 432 ordered triples. This proves B's negative result without assuming uniform third alphabet or a fixed shared E8 pool. Remaining assumptions: saturated full D heavy sets, weight 1/√2 on heavies, orthonormal complete old light frames chosen from pure lines or two equal nonzero C/D letters, and donor of exactly the stated third-heavy D / two C light type. No implication for arbitrary weights, off-alphabet directions, or incomplete heavy groups.
