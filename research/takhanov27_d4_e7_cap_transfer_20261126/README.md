# Lower49: an orientation-independent E7 test, and continuous transfers across the eight paired D4 carriers

**Scope and comparator.** Start from R. Takhanov’s 210,108-point 27D configuration, raw norm squared 128 and maximum off-diagonal inner product 64. Input is the bundled exact source and reconstructed arrays in the sibling Lower48 packet; the checker also accepts Takhanov’s raw version1/certificates tree at 77f0860bf93a745140e6e25481d3f60773b11441 and validates the source blob and certificate hash. A new lower bound requires at least 210,109. There is no such new bound in this packet. The two claims below are exact, different obstructions, with different admissible sets.

**Recovery of a coupled carrier.** The degree-two graph on the 13,248 missing Leech roots, joining the two endpoints of each distinct inserted midpoint body, has sixteen 8-cycles with diametrically opposite endpoints. In each, close four consecutive roots under the integral reflections in them. Each closure is a real 24-root D4 subsystem of the original Leech shell; the sixteen cycles pair into eight distinct D4 carriers, with *two* cycles per carrier. Thus simultaneous work on one carrier really can share the physical deletions of two different cycles. For each of these eight spaces let V be its four-dimensional real body span plus the full 3D tail. The old source has 68 rows entirely in V, not 126. This is a meaningful positive local gain gate: a full E7 would notionally insert 58 more points than the in-space population. The exterior, not an isolated small code, decides whether that gain is real.

**Theorem A (all orientations of a full exceptional-root transplant).** A full 126-point E7 root system in any of these eight real seven-dimensional V, in *any* rotation in O(7) and without fixing the existing pure-tail poles, costs at least 132 actual old rows. No full E7 transplant alone can beat the incumbent. A strict conflict is dot product greater than 64; coincident old and new rows are deliberately counted once on both sides.

*Mechanism: boundary directions must be equiangular.* For E7 roots of squared length 2, the polar of their convex hull, intersected with the closed dominant chamber, is the simplex with vertices 0 and ω_i/m_i, where ω_i are fundamental weights and m_i the highest-root coefficients. Indeed for a dominant x the highest root has maximal product with x, and the coordinates in the fundamental-weight cone are nonnegative. The diagram in the checker is a length-six path 0–1–2–3–4–5 with 6 attached to 2. The highest-root coefficients are (2,3,4,3,2,1,2), and the inverse Cartan diagonal is (2,6,12,15/2,4,3/2,7/2). Dividing by m_i² gives a unique maximum of 3/2 at i=5. Strict convexity of squared Euclidean norm excludes every nonvertex from attaining that maximum. Weyl symmetry now implies that normalized E7 support in every unit direction is at least 1/√3. The equality directions, up to scale, are in the Weyl orbit of ω_5. For any two distinct nonopposite orbit points u,v, their scalar product is 1/2 or −1/2: orbit differences belong to the root lattice and pair integrally with weights, so u·v is congruent to 3/2 modulo 1 and is strictly between −3/2 and 3/2. Their lines therefore have common absolute normalized inner product 1/3.

Project the entire real old code into V. The exact source gives 68 projections of squared length 128 and 96 of squared length 96. Every length-√128 projection conflicts by the strict support inequality (support >64). At length √96 the bound is exactly 64, so the only escape from paying the source row is an equality direction as above. The 96 rows project onto 24 lines with multiplicity four each. Join two of these 24 lines when the absolute dot product of representatives of length √96 equals 32. For each of the eight true carriers the checker independently prints and validates an eight-coloring. Thus at most eight lines of any carrier can be in the equality set at once: a clique takes at most one vertex of each color. At most 32 of the 96 old rows escape. At least 68+96−32=132 distinct old rows are charged. This covers all O(7), not just a finite list of symmetric orientations. It does not by itself exclude proper subsets of rotated E7.

**Theorem B (continuous short-head surgery with simultaneous Leech refunds).** In all eight D4 carriers together take the union H of heads h=(r+s)/2 for orthogonal roots r,s of a single D4. H has 160 distinct actual vectors with norm squared 64. Allow all of the following at once, with arbitrary outside deletions:
1. On each h∈H, add arbitrary norm-eight tails t∈R³ with any orientation, any number of sites, and any coordination *across* carriers, subject only to the kissing condition.
2. Restore any removed roots of the whole 196,560-point equatorial Leech shell, not just the local D4 cells.
3. At any of the six signed axial tails ±8e_i allow an arbitrary norm-eight body anywhere in R²⁴, not just H.
4. Replace the 12 native pure-tail poles with arbitrary pure-tail points of norm √128.
No combination gives net positive. In particular this excludes full or partial E7 in the D4⊕A1³ orientation which preserves all 24 D4 roots and uses spinor tails (±4,±4,±4√2); E7 partial subsets in completely arbitrary O(7) remain open. The E7 D4⊕A1³ split has 24 pure D4 roots; 6 pure-tail A1³ roots; 32 mixed vector weights above the two conventional tail axes; and 64 mixed spin weights. The spin and vector bodies all belong to H (the usual D4 vector and two spinor octets). Globally, H can never conflict with an equatorial Leech root: a short head is the half-sum of two orthogonal Leech roots, has dot ≤64 against every other root, and gives exactly 64 against either endpoint. The obstruction therefore comes from the *actual lift*, not from the base Leech shell.

*Mechanism: turn each continuous donor into a label with an unavoidable, distinct old blocker.* Consider a nonaxial t of norm eight and write a≥b≥c for its absolute coordinates, b>0. Since (a+b)²−||t||²=2ab−c²>0, the signed sum of its largest two coordinates exceeds eight. In other words, t lies in the open spherical cap with t·τ>32 for a signed native midpoint tail τ (two entries ±4, one zero). This cap has angular radius 45 degrees. Two tails in the same cap have positive dot product, so cannot occur at the same h since ||h||²=64. Use (h,τ) as a label; at most one actual site uses it. Every incumbent midpoint q with q_tail=τ and h·q_body≥32 blocks this label regardless of the remaining coordinates of t.

A second, necessary set of blocked rows handles refunds without making any unsupported generic-position assumption. For each of the 160 heads the exact source gives a pair of coordinate axes J={i,j} such that in each of the four signed midpoint tail classes on J some midpoint has body product ≥64. Choose one such pair, deterministically. When the primary cap uses both axes of J, nothing more is needed. Otherwise it includes one axis i∈J and excludes j∈J; take the auxiliary midpoint tail v_i=τ_i and v_j=τ_i if i<j, or v_j=−τ_i if i>j, with the third entry zero. Since |t_i|≥|t_j|, t·v>0 except possibly in a tie. The primary cap can always be chosen so the auxiliary product is strictly positive: if the primary pair can be J, use J. If the two axes of J are tied for second place, either can complete the primary pair with the remaining, larger axis. Among those two assignments exactly one uses an auxiliary sign agreeing with the sign of the excluded coordinate (the choices prescribe opposite relative signs), so choose it. If there is no tie between the selected and excluded J coordinates, positivity is strict immediately. Thus old q in this auxiliary tail class with h·q_body≥64 strictly blocks the donor even in degenerate orientations.

The 160×12 label vertices connect only to these guaranteed real old midpoints. Append one vertex for each of the 13,248 deleted roots. Each such root has four actual old signed midpoint rows above its two incident edges (dot 96 with the root). On this joint bipartite graph the replay exhibits and validates a matching of size 15,168 into the 26,496 actual midpoint rows, so every donor label or restored missing root can charge its own distinct old row. Already-retained equatorial points charge themselves if explicitly removed and reinserted. Axial tails use a disjoint argument: at any fixed signed axial tail the 48 old bodies form a full orthoplex in R²⁴. A compatible signed-tail reconfiguration cannot add more there than the old axial rows it blocks, since surviving old bodies plus the replacement have pairwise nonpositive products and cardinality ≤48. The latter inequality follows from rank(G)≥n/2 for unit vectors with off-diagonal products s≤0: tr(G²)≤2n−1ᵀG1≤2n. The same survivor/addition count for pure tails uses the classical three-dimensional kissing theorem τ(3)=12. These two charging pools are disjoint from each other, from the retained equatorial rows, and from the midpoint matching. The conclusion permits arbitrary unrelated deletions (which only worsen net gain).

**Where positive potential remains.** The finite cap-label method is a test for a transferable construction, not an impossibility result for Takhanov's entire 27D code. It does not cover nonaxial mixed donors with bodies outside the specific 160 heads, donors with different body/tail radii, changes of the body/tail decomposition in which mixed donors are no longer of the stated form, or adding other donor types that reuse the same charged midpoints. Those are the meaningful escape routes, not a larger sampling of the excluded 160 real tail spheres.

**Outward radial extension, still simultaneous with Theorem B.** In addition, allow any number of new sites (c h,t) for h∈H, arbitrary individual real c>1 with total norm squared 128, and arbitrary tails. On a fixed h, two such sites have t·t′≤64−64cc′<0. A mutually strictly obtuse set in R³ has at most 4 vectors (if there are at least two, none is zero). For completeness any affine dependence among five vectors would give a convex combination of a positive group equal to a convex combination of a disjoint negative group; its squared norm would equal the average of strictly negative cross products, impossible. A single empty-tail site causes no issue. For every one of the 160 h there are 46 equatorial Leech roots r with h·r=64; 152 heads retain 38 of them in the source and the remaining eight retain 22. These old roots strictly block every c>1 on that ray. The replay matches four slots on each of the 160 rays into 640 *distinct* retained equatorial sources. This fourth charging pool is disjoint from the previous midpoint, axial and pure-pole pools. If a retained equatorial row is used as one of these blockers it cannot also be reinserted alongside the outward donor, so there is no double counting. Consequently simply shifting radii outward on these heads does not evade the obstruction. Moving inward (body length <8, nonaxial tails) remains open; changing body directions remains open.
