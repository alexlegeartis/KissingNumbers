## 1. Multi-parent one-hot splitting: sharp +39

Start with the 589,824 signs from the nine 0/unit prefix fibres of shortened QR48 on eight marked sites, all 3,120 signed D40 roots, and Smart's 4,267 octads (≤128 compatible sign rows per octad). A daughter relative to a deleted parent sign s has its 39 ordinary signs unchanged; on hot coordinate i multiply its sign by −a and normalize, allowing independent a≥0. With all D40 roots retained, the signed root on hot plus any ordinary yields 2(a+1)^2≤39+a², or a≤a*=sqrt(41)−2. With two different hot coordinates on one parent, the mutual normalized dot is (38−a−b)/sqrt((39+a²)(39+b²)), strictly decreasing in both in that interval, and equal to 1/2 only when a=b=a*. Two such daughters on the same hot of one parent are impossible (arc length <pi/3). Thus every multi-child parent has forced amplitude a*.

At an incident octad B a hard daughter forbids the signing matching its signs on B: its raw dot is a*+7>2(a*+1) because a*<5. (The parity opposite that bad signing is precisely the parity of the unsplit parent s restricted to B.) If two parent restrictions on B have different parity and both choose some fixed hot i in B, then at least one forbidden signing has each parity. Every set of 128 compatible signings on B must be *one entire parity class*: independent 128-subsets of connected 8-regular bipartite cube Q8 are precisely its two color classes (count edges to see the complement also independent). Therefore a contested B loses at least one row, even allowing arbitrary sign recoding.

Smart's incidence data give a much stronger separation than global rank: for each hot coordinate i the 852–855 incident octads contain at least sixteen disjoint binary bases of the 39-dimensional even-weight space. Hence any parent difference except zero and all ones is odd on at least sixteen octads containing i. No two accepted QR parents are complements: if their body difference were all ones, adding the all-ones mother word would give a nonzero QR word supported on the eight punctures (or their prefix difference would be 11111111, impossible for two accepted prefixes); dmin=12 excludes it.

Consequences:

* If every octad retains 128 signings (its own parity chosen freely), each hot can be used by at most one multi-child parent. Thus total improvement over the 1,139,120 three-layer baseline is at most 39, regardless of number of split parents. One deleted central row and the published 40 daughters attain it.
* Even allowing arbitrary deletions AND independent re-signing of all octad rows, the same ceiling holds whenever each hot is used by at most four multi-child parents (in particular, for up to four multi-parent splits). To prove it let m_i count such parents. For a pair sharing hot i at least sixteen incident B have odd restriction difference. A contested B at that i counts at most floor(m_i²/4) opposite-parity pairs. If m_i≥2, the number of contested incidences at i is thus ≥16 binom(m_i,2)/floor(m_i²/4). Each distinct contested octad accounts for ≤8 incidences; for m_i=2,3,4 the charge per i after dividing by 8 is 2,3,3 respectively, in each case ≥m_i−1. Thus total octad loss pays for every duplicated hot. Ignoring parents producing ≤1 daughter (their net is nonpositive), total gain ≤ number of used hot coordinates − number of multi-child parents ≤39.

## 2. A nonlinear obstruction to restoring an antipodal pair via QR dodecad shortening

A plausible escape is to make the binary donor bank antipodal. Mark any QR48 weight-12 support D, set aside four sites E⊂D as head and puncture M=D\\E (eight coordinates); T is the complement of D (36 coordinates). For each of the 589,824 mother words with prefix 0/e_i on M retain its ordinary 36 sign tail on T and give it an *arbitrary*, person-by-person real head z in R^4 with ||z||=2. Resulting squared norm is 40. This includes nonlinear and nonbinary head choices. Let v be the QR word 1+D, i.e. ones on T only.

On any retained noncentral pair differing by a dodecad supported at exactly 2 marks in M, all four of E and 6 of T, the body dot is 24; maintaining the kissing angle forces head dot ≤−4, hence z' = −z by equality in Cauchy. There are exactly 28 such mother differences, an undirected multigraph on the eight unit prefixes with a difference word as edge label. Traversing an edge translates a codeword by its label; a closed walk realizes the XOR of the edge labels and multiplies the head by (−1)^length. The fundamental cycle voltages span a group containing v. Translating by v has even path length: every edge flips all 4 original E bits and v has zeros on E. Thus for EVERY noncentral w, necessarily z(w+v)=z(w), not its antipode. This conclusion requires no linearity, sign coding or symmetry at all from the reassignment.

The verification below regenerates QR48, its 17,296 dodecads, three orbits of sizes 8,648,4,324,4,324 under x↦x+1 and x↦−1/x of P¹(F47), and every 495 four-subset in each orbit representative. For every one of the 1,485 representative splittings the prefix graph is connected, cycle rank in message space is 13 or 14, and v belongs. In each there are exactly 480 weight-12 differences with 1 mark in M, 3 head bits in E and 8 body bits in T, whose 8-sets cover every active axis. The group permutations preserve the mother code (translations on the cyclic construction, inversion verifies on the generators).

In particular the result excludes global antipodalization of all matched dense rows. It also rules out making a central pair w0,w0+v become opposite while splitting both at any shared body coordinate i by the old one-hot mechanism, keeping all noncentral rows. Indeed pick one of the 480 differences u with i in its eight body bits, and use the matched noncentral rows w0+u, w0+u+v. They have opposite bodies but the SAME arbitrary head. For the two proposed opposite daughters take opposite heads ±h with ||h||=2 and body words of length 36, each replacing the hot sign by −a*, where a*=sqrt(41)−2. The raw body product with the matched near row is 20+(a*+1)>sqrt(10[39+a*²]); the two head contributions are opposite, so at least one offending raw product is still this large. Exact check: (21+a*)²−10(39+a*²)>0. A smaller a does not arise for multi-child regular splits, per part 1 when the ordinary entries are signs (the displayed exclusion concerns the specified a* and norm-2 antipodal four-coordinate head). This is NOT a theorem about varying the head radii, omitting some of the forcing rows, or using different body amplitudes/marks.
