# 47D: an exact cyclotomic shell join and a partial projection obstruction

Lower81, 2026-11-27, 10:30 UTC. **No new kissing-number lower bound.** The strict target was 23,766,961 against the **Boyvalenkov–Cherkashin** 23,766,960-point hyperplane section of a 48D extremal even unimodular lattice ([equation (3) and its aftermath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05121), 2023 preprint / Results in Mathematics 80, 2025). The current project README attributes it to them, not us. The strategy is different from the earlier P48 D4/E6 whole-fibre angular tours and cyclic lattice neighbors: use the order-47 cyclotomic projection of P48q, then reassign all complete body-shell groups arbitrary scalar heights simultaneously.

## Continuous join theorem, exact scope

Define a complete body group in K*=π(P48q), for the rank-46 body K, as all vectors in one coset of K having one common squared radius r. Each selected group is placed with a constant real scalar head and normalized pointwise (all points in a group automatically share one normalization). One may use either sign or both, and include either pure pole. Impose the standard sufficient distance rule: within each group certify with bare min(K)=6, and between groups certify with the distance inferred from shortest normal representatives and exact emptiness of the norm-six/eight fibres, as described below. **Every configuration under these rules has at most 23,766,960 points, allowing arbitrary real scalar heights, even independently by group.** If the 9,372,270-point central body group is excluded, the same rule gives at most 20,919,607. Both are upper bounds on the stated certificate mechanism; not on arbitrary actual configurations with the same bodies if their genuine cross distances are stronger, not on partial fibres, and not on the unrestricted kissing number. Only the first count is asserted attainable, by the older known section.

## Exact geometry and census

[Nebe, Theorem 5.6 / fixed-lattice table](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/papers/DIM48.pdf) gives an order-47 isometry of P48q with fixed lattice F of Gram ((6,1),(1,8)). Use the equivalent extended ternary QR coordinates where the order-47 cycle permutes the finite places and the constant word lies in the code. Write unscaled coordinates in units 1/√3. The odd code lattice M=A3(C) has raw integral coordinates with residue in C, and its even sublattice M0 has even coordinate sum (equivalently even norm). The min-six even neighbor is M0 ∪ (u+M0), with
  u=(1/2 at every finite place, −5/2 at infinity),
  v=(1/2 at every finite place, 7/2 at infinity).
In these formulas dividing raw coordinates by √3 is implicit. The half-constant vector is in the other even neighbor (norm four); replacing one entry by −5/2 adds an odd norm-three frame vector, so the u-shift gives precisely the distinct min-six even neighbor. This uses the canonical ternary QR construction and existence of the min-six neighbor from Nebe, §3.1–3.2. One can also check directly that the half-constant vector and u both define order-two integral even extensions of M0, and that they differ by a vector in M ∖ M0. The displayed u,v are fixed; their Gram determinant is 47, hence they generate the cited fixed lattice. Each vector with one finite −5/2 and all other coordinates +1/2 is a minimal lattice vector because it differs from u by a signed difference of two norm-three frame rails in M0.

For x in the parent lattice let (s,t)=(x·u,x·v). Its normal projection has squared length Q(s,t)/47, where Q(s,t)=8s²−2st+6t². The body's coset index is j=8s−t modulo 47, because the kernel of this map on the normal integral pairing coordinates is exactly the column span of the Gram matrix. The body radius is parent norm minus Q/47. The fixed lattice is primitive, and unimodularity identifies the projected body lattice with K*, K=P48q∩F-perp. For a fixed j and one body radius, all normal labels giving that same body coset are alternate parameterizations, not extra points.

The min-six shell is an antipodal spherical 11-design, with 52,416,000 points. Remove ±u, and use joint moments of s,t through total degree 10, computed by Wick pairings and the spherical denominator 48·50···. The remaining integrality support has |s|≤3 and |t|≤4, and every (s,t) must satisfy |x·w|≤||w||²/2 for w in F from the minimum (only ±u have exceptional norm-six coincidence). Use the central sign symmetry, with the origin carrying half its true count. Exact elimination leaves free cells X=N6(2,4), Y=N6(3,3), Z=N6(3,4). By the order-47 isometry, after subtracting ±u, every count is a multiple of 47: another fixed minimal vector would be another norm-six vector in F, which does not exist. Nonnegativity of cells (1,−4) and (3,−2), of counts 705−4X−15Z and 1645−6Y−35Z, bounds the finite enumeration. Precisely two profiles remain, (X,Y,Z)/47=(0,0,1) or (3,5,0). The 47 explicit finite −5/2 rail vectors above all have (s,t)=(3,4), proving the first profile. This identifies the entire shell census without enumerating its vectors.

Next the norm-eight shell (39,007,332,000 points) has exactly known joint moments through total degree 18. If H is homogeneous harmonic of degree d>0, its P48q weighted theta lies in Δ³ M_(d−12), by modularity in weight 24+d and the absence of norms two and four. The q^4/q^3 coefficient ratios for d=2,4,…,10,12,14,16,18 are respectively zero, −72, zero, 168, −576 (in the zero cases the theta is identically zero; in the other cases use Δ³, Δ³E4 or Δ³E6). The usual Eisenstein normalizations give E4=1+240q+..., E6=1−504q+..., Δ³=q³−72q⁴+.... For a homogeneous degree-d polynomial P in the pairings, its top harmonic is
  H = ∑_i c_i ||x||^(2i) Laplacian^i(P),
  c_0=1, c_i/c_(i−1)=−1/(4 i (d−i+23)).
Here Laplacian on P(s,t) is 6∂_ss+2∂_st+8∂_tt. Thus the known norm-six moments and lower norm-eight moments determine each new norm-eight moment. The shell count follows from the ordinary theta form E4^6−1440E4³Δ+125280Δ² (see [Elkies](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~elkies/aws09.pdf)). Apart from ±v at (±1,±8), the norm-eight pairing support is |s|≤4, |t|≤5 by the minimum-six inequality. Tensor Lagrange interpolation of degrees eight and ten finds every count, all exact integers and multiples of 47 after the two axis exceptions. In particular each of the (0,±4) fibres has 7,945,350 norm-eight points. The full signed norm-eight table has 91 nonzero cells including ±v.

For each difference class j, enumerate its shortest normal representatives, with minimum Q=μ_j. The largest μ_j is exactly **144**, so every positive body radius r≤6 can be represented by a parent lattice vector of norm r+μ_j/47<10. There are no smaller nonzero parent norms below six. Thus the norm-six and norm-eight tables give every complete nonzero body group with r≤6, even those first described by much longer normal representatives. Since the fixed ordinary body K contains the central shell, its minimum is six. Using just that minimum for within-group certification restricts r+h²≤6, so no complete group with larger r occurs under this certificate.

For two distinct body vectors, pick the shortest normal representative of their difference class. Their difference lifts to a nonzero parent vector with squared length d_body²+μ_j/47. Use D_j=6−μ_j/47, strengthened to 8−μ_j/47 (or 10−μ_j/47) if some shortest representative has no norm-six fibre (or neither norm-six nor norm-eight). Both exact tables determine the necessary emptiness. If two attempted groups use the same body class and radius, allow coincident body vectors across them and use D=0 for the paired test. A pure head is compared directly. Within each group the lattice difference on K gives squared distance at least six.

For body radii r,r' and signed heads h,k the stipulated compatibility test is
  r+r'−D+2hk ≤ √((r+h²)(r'+k²)).
For each available body group use two sign options (one for the central zero head) and split the absolute head range [0,√(6−r)] into four equal intervals on the amplitude, not on the square. The left side minus the right is separately concave in the two unsigned amplitudes, so it can be nonpositive somewhere in any interval rectangle only if it is nonpositive at one of the four corners. These corner tests have exact two-radical rational comparisons. Distinct intervals of the same oriented option are incompatible, giving a weighted clique upper bound on every continuous choice. With the central group included, first constrain every axis amplitude by comparison with that group; equality of the center maximum with the threshold pins an amplitude at the top, so such intervals collapse. Keeping or omitting the center yields 95 and 418 nodes and the displayed caps. The clique bound is exact arithmetic: successive weighted independent sets in the compatibility graph cover residual vertex weights, then binary branch on a selected vertex; no optimizer status is trusted. The baseline witness uses the ordinary s=0 section, i.e. nine cells and the endpoint heights signed by t. The maximum for the normalized, fixed total norm six subcase is already the same, with 107 oriented options including poles.

## A separate partial-fibre obstruction for any extremal 48D parent

Fix any norm-six normal u; take its standard 23,766,960-point section A0. Select any subset of projected, normalized minimal-shell vectors whose original height x·u is strictly positive, not necessarily complete fibres, and permit arbitrary old-section deletions; a gain is impossible. Same for negative height. Height six projects to zero; rule it out. For x of height s=1,2,3, its old blockers are exactly the section vertices y with x·y=3, since its projected radius is slightly smaller than six and the other integer dots are at most two. Each such donor blocks at least 15,520 / 7,936 / 1,656 different old points. On one side an old vertex y can be hit by at most 9,720 / 576 / 96 corresponding donors. Divide each donor's unit deletion price uniformly over its old blockers. The load at any old point is at most 314063/414966<1, which even proves a strict margin for any nonempty such trade.

To verify the counts, for a pair of roots u,x with dot b=0,1,2,3, remove ±u and ±x. The remaining 7×7 joint dot support is {-3,…,3}². Antipodality and degree-ten Wick moments determine all cells up to the one missing (6,6) moment; tensor interpolation makes them affine in the (3,3) cell. Nonnegativity and integrality leave parameter intervals [0,96], [49,57], {368}, {1656}. The lower (0,3) counts for b=1,2,3 and maximum (s,3) counts for b=0 give the quoted budgets. For arbitrary two-sided selection the same price argument is no longer conclusive; it would require observing heavy true same-neighborhood overlap while avoiding cross-side contact edges. The complementary two-sided problem remains open.

## Why change architecture next

The two 7.945-million outer bodies had the right scale to matter; their class contact costs, not their population, prevented a join. A serious next step is a norm-eight **partial** body shell selected by true directed contact differences (not requiring absence of all short differences in the periodic ambient set), or a different body projection with an additional tag direction. The exact 47-class census and difference domains above allow either approach to name the actual blocked families. No result here certifies a gap for non-affine group selections or arbitrary physical deformations.

Multiplicity check: every positive projected body radius satisfies r ≥ 6 − 144/47 = 138/47 > 3/2, by lifting its shortest normal representative to a nonzero parent vector. Consequently two copies of the same complete body group with heads of the same sign, at any allowed magnitudes, are impossible: the two normalized lifts of a single shared body make angle at most arctan √((6−r)/r) < 60°. So at most one sheet per orientation is lossless. There are only two pure poles.
