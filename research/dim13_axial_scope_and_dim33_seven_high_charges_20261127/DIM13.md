# Lower100 low-dimensional pivot: no sparse-real axial exchange in 13D with both old binary caps

There is no new lower bound. The incumbent is 1154, from V. Zinov'ev and T. Ericson, [Theorem 1 (1999)](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/ppi457): a 51-support signed-quartet equator (816), an ordinary length-12 binary d=4 code of size 144 on each cap, all 24 signed axial rows on each cap, and two poles. Unit axial vectors are (±(√3/2)e_i, ±1/2); ordinary binary cap magnitudes are 1/4, with the same 144 sign patterns at both heights in the literal old witness. Literal masks used by the replay are from [Cohn's spherical code archive](https://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/153312) through the read-only numerical extract `ze99_1154_numeric.txt` at [Rafael Amichis Luengo's reproduction](https://github.com/tretoef-estrella/kissing-number-13-zinoviev-ericson-reproduction/blob/main/ze99_1154_numeric.txt), Git blob 6705498d28e8222da2191aea7a6a5a317e255547. ## Exact theorem

Keep the entire old signed-quartet equator, both 144-point binary caps, and both poles. Remove any subset of the 48 signed axial rows. Allow arbitrary real replacements, non-antipodal and with completely independent heights. **If every new ray has at most nine nonzero ordinary coordinates, none of these trades improves 1154.** Also, for any support size, every replacement with maximum absolute ordinary magnitude >1/2 is injectively charged to a required-deleted old axial row. Thus an improving replacement while keeping equator, binary caps and poles must include a genuinely diffuse new ray with at least ten ordinary positions and all magnitudes ≤1/2. The theorem is not an upper bound on all 13D codes; relaxing or moving the equator, coded caps or poles remains outside scope.

Write each new unit ray as (x,t). Poles require |t|≤1/2, hence ordinary squared norm at least 3/4. A complete old signed quartet Q requires Σ_{i∈Q}|x_i|≤1. For the actual 51 quartets every pair occurs 4 or 5 times, the uncovered triples consist of two disjoint octahedral surfaces, and every fixed head position has exactly four missing pair-links, a single 4-cycle on four other positions (the other seven are isolated leave vertices); the code checks the needed finite invariants. The following high-leaf argument uses just the head link.

### Head-link norm lemma, no support restriction

Let one ordinary head attain m=max_i|x_i|>1/2, m<1. Scale the other 11 amplitudes to w_i=|x_i|/(1−m). Each of the 17 link triples has sum at most 1. Every covered pair consequently has sum≤1; these are all residual pairs except the edges of the C4 leave. Degrees in the **triple** link are 4 on that C4 and 5 on the other seven. With L=Σw and D its part on the four degree-4 sites, 5L−D≤17.

Put s=max w_i≤1. If s≤1/2, then D≤2, so Σw_i²≤sL≤19/10. If s>1/2 occurs at an isolated leave vertex, all others ≤1−s; D≤4(1−s), whence Σw_i²≤s²+(1−s)(21−9s)/5≤19/10 by convexity on [1/2,1]. If it occurs at a C4 vertex, the two leave-neighbor amplitudes u,v obey u+v≤1 (their mutual pair is covered), and the opposite C4 vertex is ≤1−s. Therefore D≤2,L≤19/5, and all sites other than the chosen maximum and its two neighbors are ≤1−s. The convex function f(z)=z²−(1−s)z obeys f(u)+f(v)≤2s²−s on the polygon 0≤u,v≤s,u+v≤1 by its vertices. Consequently Σw_i²≤s²+(1−s)(19/5−s)+(2s²−s)=4s²−29s/5+19/5≤2, again by the endpoints.

It follows that ordinary norm² is ≤m²+2(1−m)², incompatible with ≥3/4 for 1/2<m<5/6. Thus m≥5/6 (m=1 is trivial). The ray blocks the old signed axial row of its maximal coordinate's sign and its hemisphere sign (choose either for t=0), since their dot is ≥(√3/2)(5/6)>1/2. Two new rays with the same signed axial label have dot at least (5/6)^2−2(1/6)^2=23/36>1/2 by residual Cauchy, since their heights' product is nonnegative. So this owner is an actual injection.

### Diffuse rays through nine positions, finitely many exact polyhedra

If m≤1/2 and support≤9, pad ordinary magnitudes with zeros to size 8 (when ≤8) or leave size 9, and sort a≥b≥c≥d≥e≥f≥g≥h (≥i), all nonnegative. The three possible largest-covered-triple strata have respectively the following necessary cuts:
1. abc is covered, so a+b+c≤1.
2. abc is leave while abd is covered, so a+b+d≤1.
3. both abc and abd are leave. Then c,d are the opposite pair completing the same a,b leave edge, and acd plus abe are covered, giving a+c+d≤1, a+b+e≤1.

The seven largest positions contain at least two actual old quartets in every seven-set. The second-smallest possible 4-position sum of sorted seven is c+e+f+g, so this must be ≤1. An 8-support contains at least 5 old quartets and a 9-support at least 12; these counts are independently checked on all underlying sets. For each dimension the family of 4-subsets whose sum is ≤1 is upward-closed in componentwise ranks (replace indices with larger, hence smaller weights). Existence of ≥k forces an upward-closed subfamily of exactly k (iteratively remove an extremal element). Its finitely many maximal-weight generators give the needed equations. There are five such ideals for k=5 on eight positions and 48 for k=12 on nine. For nine positions the largest pair lies in at least one old quartet wholly within them: its original codegree is ≥4 and each excluded site kills at most one quartet through that pair, by its triple degree ≤1. Hence impose a+b+h+i≤1.

The portable exact checker evaluates the squared norm maximum for the resulting 5×3 and 48×3 bounded rational polytopes. It changes variables to nonnegative successive jumps x_j−x_{j+1}, so each vertex with k positive jumps is pinned by k independent original constraints and zeros elsewhere. It exhaustively selects the positive slots and active constraints, uses fraction-free exact integer elimination with divisions verified and rechecks all inequalities before norm evaluation. Every maximum is ≤3/4; at equality, the only vertex is three initial 1/2's and all else zero. Strict convexity of squared norm ensures these are the only equality rays even away from vertices. Any such ray has |t|=1/2 and absolute ordinary L1=3/2, but the literal 144 binary sign code projects surjectively on every triple. Align the three signs in the same hemisphere's old binary cap to get dot (1/4)(3/2)+1/4=5/8>1/2. Hence no diffuse ray through nine positions is feasible, completing the theorem.
