# 71D Γ72: all 9,434,880 equal-residue two-block candidates blocked, and a complete-bank trade obstruction

**No record change.** The actual construction at [KissingNumbers main c4bc5ab7](https://github.com/alexlegeartis/KissingNumbers/tree/c4bc5ab73ec9c057e6701d964f18e98af2cede04/verifications/improved/dim71-gamma72-single-block-refill) has 2,623,656,288 points. A named historical external comparator is the 331,737,984-point 64D construction of [Edel–Rains–Sloane, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0207291), embedded into 71D; this is not a new worldwide census. The carrier is [Nebe's Γ72](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2862v3); the independent [Kominers norm-ten counts, Proposition 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08734) verify 2,595,532,800 old (10,4) points before any attempted change. The old package's own refill only used a single occupied block. This result tests two *other* occupied blocks without claiming an upper bound for 71D.

## Actual old stock and exact candidate class

Write \(P=\Lambda_{24}/2\Lambda_{24}=A\oplus B\) for the two complementary totally singular halves in the verified three-Leech-block model. The squared norm on the triple is half the sum of the three Leech squared norms. A triple is in Γ72 when its three A labels agree and its B labels sum to zero. The actual normal is \(x=(2\lambda,0,0)\), with \(\lambda\) the fixed norm-four Leech vector from the live package; \(\|x\|^2=8\). Write \(a=A(\lambda)\ne0\), \(c=B(\lambda)\ne0\). A lift of label \((s,j)\) has Γ72 squared norm \(s\), product \(j\) with \(x\), and orthogonal projection of squared norm \(s-j^2/8\). Take the entire live shells \((8,3),(8,-3),(8,4),(10,4)\) except the 4,600 old points removed from \((8,4)\), all of form \((2\mu,0,0)\). The translated representation of \((10,5)\) by \((8,-3)\) has identical projections because the translate is by \(x\). The 81,952 first-block-only additions are maintained.

For each nonzero \(b\in B\), let \(F_b\) be the 48 signed norm-eight vectors of the Leech class \((0,b)\). They are the signs of 24 orthogonal axes. Completeness follows from the [pinned Leech residue calculation](https://github.com/alexlegeartis/KissingNumbers/tree/c4bc5ab73ec9c057e6701d964f18e98af2cede04/verifications/improved/dim73-82-gamma72-depth6-lattice) and is redundantly checked by producing 24 distinct axes in every nonzero B label: distinct norm-eight vectors in the same Leech parity class are orthogonal or antipodal by the minimum applied to their half-sum and half-difference. The candidate bank is
\[
Y_b=\{(0,u,v):u,v\in F_b\}.
\]
For fixed \(b\) it has 2,304 rays, altogether \(4,095\times2,304=9,434,880\). These distinct vectors have Γ72 norm squared eight and are orthogonal to \(x\), hence are mutually compatible by the lattice minimum. They are also orthogonal to all the first-block-only additions. The theorem is that *none* of these rays can be appended to the retained live stock.

## First cut: 2,071 complete banks from a frame identity

For every \(\Lambda_{24}\) norm-eight frame, \(\sum_{\mathrm{axis}}\langle\lambda,w\rangle^2=8\|\lambda\|^2=32\). The magnitudes are integers at most four (apply the Leech minimum to \(\lambda\pm w\)); parity is constant on all directions of \(F_b\) and is the polarization pairing of \(a\) with \(b\). There are 2,048 odd frames. Each has 23 entries of absolute value one and one of absolute value three. If any \(w\in F_b\) has \(|\langle\lambda,w\rangle|=3\), the points \((w,u,0)\) and \((w,0,v)\) have norm squared eight, belong to the retained \((8,\pm3)\) shells and have dot product four with the candidate \(y=(0,u,v)\). Their projected threshold \(\sqrt{8(8-9/8)}/2\) is strictly less than four. A single such old point suffices.

For even frames, \(\langle\lambda,w\rangle=4\) exactly when \(\lambda-w\) is another norm-four vector in the A label \(a\). Each nonzero A label has 24 antipodal norm-four axes; their dots are even, and a distinct pair cannot have absolute dot two because such a sum or difference would be a forbidden norm-four vector of A label zero. Thus these axes are orthogonal. Excluding the \(\lambda\)-axis gives 23 distinct nonzero B differences; each supplies two axes of dot magnitude four. Such an old \((w,u,0)\) or \((w,0,v)\) is in the retained \((8,4)\) stock, because it occupies two blocks and is not one of the original first-block-only deletions. Its projected threshold \(\sqrt{8\cdot6}/2\) is again less than four.

This covers 2,071 banks. The 2,024 remaining frames have 16 zero dots and eight of magnitude two against \(\lambda\). The per-frame classifications are also checked literally.

## Second and third cuts: fixed first block λ and an exact two-slot test

Let \(y=(0,u,v)\) and take a Γ72 minimal displacement \(w=(\lambda,r,s)\) with \(A(r)=A(s)=a\), \(B(r)+B(s)=c\), and Leech squared norms of \((r,s)\) summing to 12. Choose signs so the two local products against \(u,v\) are nonpositive. If their absolute values in the unscaled Leech inner product add to \(d=6\) or \(8\), then \(y\cdot w=-d/2\) in the Γ72 metric. The lift \(z=y+w\) has \((s_z,j_z)=(16-d,4)\) and dot \(y\cdot z=8-d/2\). For \(d=6\) that is five against threshold four for \((10,4)\); for \(d=8\) it is four against threshold \(\sqrt{12}\) for \((8,4)\). Both are strict. Both survive the actual small-shell deletions: the first block is \(\lambda\), not a doubled norm-four vector, and the nonzero second/third profile is checked by the norm choices. The finite gate is therefore sufficient regardless of what other valid blocker types there may be.

* **6+6.** The norm-six Leech classes in fixed nonzero A label \(a\) occupy exactly the 2,048 anisotropic B labels (the other half is singular). A norm-six class has a unique antipodal pair, by applying the minimum to its half-sum and half-difference. The replay constructs all 2,048 axes from pairs of norm-four vectors with appropriate A/B sums and Leech inner product of magnitude one. The shift \(b'\mapsto b'+c\) yields 1,024 unordered pairs of axes; check both placements. The resulting masks rule out 1,789 of the 2,024 whole residual banks. Of the remaining banks, 220 contribute four ordered unsigned axis pairs each and 15 contribute 16, so 1,120 unsigned pairs or 4,480 signed candidates remain after this cut.
* **4+8 and 8+4.** For each of the 24 norm-four axes in A label \(a\), the corresponding norm-eight class is \((a,c+B(r))\). The replay constructs its 24 axes from orthogonal norm-four sums and verifies all these class sizes. For both ordered placements of the resulting 576 axis pairs, the same elementary dot-sum test rules out every one of the 4,480 remaining candidates.

For mask efficiency the second cut represents norm-six vectors by signs in the norm-four A frame. All their local products with the norm-four axes are odd and their squares sum to 24, so the magnitudes are all one. For B-only norm-eight axes, the local magnitude profile in that same frame is either one 3 and 23 ones, eight 2s and 16 zeros, or two 4s and 22 zeros. For the even case, any local product four forces a second magnitude-four axis: subtract the matching signed norm-four vector to obtain another norm-four vector with the same A label; the trace then gives the stated alternatives. The checker verifies all of these profiles explicitly and uses exact popcounts, equivalent to the ordinary coordinate product in an orthogonal basis. No optimization or tolerances enter.

## Joint trade theorem, even allowing arbitrary shared old deletions

For each complete bank \(Y_b\), consider *only* old blockers supplied by the preceding fixed-\(\lambda\) 6+6 displacement family. Count separately those with \(d=6\) (norm-ten) and \(d=8\) (norm-eight). These counts are **distinct old points per bank**, not just incidences with repeats. Indeed, given old \(z=(\lambda,z_2,z_3)\) and a bank label \(b\), the two norm-six residue labels of \(r=z_2-u,s=z_3-v\) are fixed, so the norm-six axes are fixed. Both signs are unique, because for either tail coordinate the equation \(\|z_i-r_i\|^2=8\) forces \(\langle z_i,r_i\rangle=(\|z_i\|^2-2)/2>0\); the old coordinate is nonzero of norm at least four. Thus \(z\) can block at most one of the candidate rays in any one fixed \(Y_b\) by this displacement mechanism.

The same exact masks count the distinct old points: for each of the 1,024 unordered norm-six pairs, sum the compatible unsigned candidate axis pairs for which the magnitudes sum to 6 or 8, then multiply by two for the two placements and four for the independent signs of the candidate. All counted nonzero candidate local products admit precisely the negative signs on the displacement; \(d\ge6\) and the absolute local maximum is five. The checker yields:
* At least **52,224** total retained distinct old blockers for *every* \(Y_b\).
* At least **49,152** are in the dominant norm-ten shell alone, for every \(Y_b\).
* For every one of the 2,048 odd banks, precisely 76,032 norm-ten and 576 norm-eight blockers in this mechanism, total 76,608.
* Minimum 52,224 and maximum 76,608 over all 4,095 bank totals.

For a selection of \(m>0\) complete banks, any old blocker can be counted at most \(m\) times; therefore their union has at least 52,224 old points regardless of shared deletions, strictly more than \(2,304m\) if \(m\le22\). Using the dominant norm-ten slice alone gives strictly more than \(2,304m\) if \(m\le21\); if all selected banks are odd, the full bound is strict through \(m=33\). For a mixed selection with \(m_o\) odd and \(m_e\) even, the same proof gives \(\lceil(76608m_o+52224m_e)/(m_o+m_e)\rceil\) old deletions. In particular, the failure of a joint trade is not inferred from the much cheaper many-to-one blockers of the first cut.

### Scope and replay

This does not exclude arbitrary partial banks, larger mixed selections, other two-block positions, different B residues in the two positions, or a different overall carrier. It gives no new lower bound. The [standalone checker](verify.mjs) uses only Node and three exact input files from the pinned repository; it reconstructs the complete 98,280 norm-four unsigned directions in the published Leech frame from norm and parity, rebuilds the B-only frames and relevant norm-six/eight classes, and performs exact integer dot/mask checks and trade counts. No external downloads, searches or fitting required. Run at repository root as `node research/dim71-bonly-two-block-trade-20261127/verify.mjs .`.
