## Proof framework: an entire BW fibre is a nonpositive residual code

Use Takhanov's octad split I={0,2,4,5,6,10,11,23}, complement J, and five tails. Raw norm is 128 and raw dot cutoff 64. The 4,320 standard J bodies b of squared norm64 each have 16 full Leech head directions (an antipodal orthogonal 8-frame); distinct standard b have dot≤32, directly rechecked on all pairs. At one b, subtract its common norm64 contribution: allowable 13-dimensional residual vectors r=(I-head,tail) have squared norm64 and mutual dot≤0. Thus the full fibre bound is 26 by the orthoplex bound in dimension 13. Actual full local occupancy histogram is 16:28, 17:4, 20:32, 24:4010, 25:224, 26:22. Each 24-ray fibre contains twelve exact antipodal axes and has precisely one vacant residual basis direction, which is always a physical tail coordinate. There are 48 additional nonstandard J bodies each with one auxiliary outside the 4,320, not silently classified as standard.

Among the 2,416 pure-J auxiliaries, 320 have axial tail ±8 e_j and 2,096 have four ±4 entries. The 320 axial points divide into ten signed tail colours. For each colour its 32 J heads form an exact signed 16-frame. Any replacement confined to that tail colour still has at most32 by the orthoplex bound (same tail consumes all raw cutoff); all ten colours are saturated. Therefore merely adding axial rays, without changing some other shape, cannot improve the configuration.

### One-axis reframe is exactly closed

Keep 22 of the 24 local antipodal rows and inquire whether replacing the remaining antipodal pair can yield three or four rays, without changing any other fibre. The 22 kept rows force new residuals into the orthogonal circle of the selected old axis x and the vacancy y. The two already saturated signed axial colours project cap walls y≤(64−p_+)/64 and y≥−(64−p_−)/64, where p_± are the maximum J products with those old signed-colour frames. Actual unordered (p_+,p_−) counts are (32,48):374; (32,32):2701; (16,32):600; (16,16):335. Except in the last class, the sum of squares of the two cap magnitudes is <1. Each of the two allowed circle lobes then has angular width arcsin(c_+)+arcsin(c_−)<π/2, so there can be at most two points with nonpositive pairwise dot. In every one of the 335 remaining fibres and each of its twelve old axis choices (4,020 planes), actual retained exterior normals give the four exact additional walls ±x±y≤1: their dot with b is32 and their products with the two residual basis vectors are each ±32 independently. The only unit circle points satisfying all four are the coordinate axes; the earlier caps exclude the vacancy axis. Thus all single-pair rebuilds are closed for arbitrary real angles, not merely sign/Kempe reassignment.

### Explicit higher coupling: Hadamard four-axis candidates

Transform THREE old pairs plus the vacant axis by a 4×4 Hadamard. This replaces six old points by eight when feasible. Use both even and odd sign parities, all eight signed sum vectors divided by 2. In the cube-only 24-ray branch two old Leech head axes plus a cube tail axis generate the genuinely new midpoint tail shape (±4,±2,±2,±2,±2). In the ordinary switched branch all feasible old triplets passing the conservative equator gate and pole gate were checked. The equator gate uses the convex hull of the original signed Leech frame at the same b, hence clears the whole Leech shell by averaging even endpoints: a head in their convex hull is safe against every old non-endpoint, and the concrete candidate gate uses all head signed sums with ℓ1 bound ≤1 in that frame. For candidates using axes whose associated shell endpoints are retained rather than removed, the required direct equator check is imposed by the cap at the same b: remaining local pairs are orthogonal to the moved axes, and for a different b any original frame endpoint is a distinct Leech point, so the convex-hull argument is valid. The pole gate is at most two uses of each nonvacant tail coordinate, and axial max≤32 on the vacancy's signed colours. An exact parity-sum calculation on every actual old off-equator row counts the blockers. All **46,592** eligible candidates on 208 cube-only fibres and **453,248** on 3,426 ordinary fibres hit at least five fixed off-equator rows, so even a single +2 packet cannot pay its own blockers. This does not claim exhaustion of arbitrary three-pair deformations or coordinated neighbouring packets.

## Exact continuous append and paid chambers

For each of 64 underfilled fibres of occupancy16–20, a pure tail of squared norm64 at its J body is compatible with all old equator automatically: b is the average of two opposite head axes at that fibre. Each nonzero tail normal q contributes one inequality q·t≤64−max(b·J_x) over actual old rows with this tail. There are 171 distinct signed tail normals. All 64 exact polytopes have maximum squared norm ≤48 (histogram 36:3,38:22,40:7,48:32), so this full continuous pure-tail interface is closed, not just the old axis/cube catalogue.

For 32 of those underfilled fibres all 16 head endpoints are retained, so the opposite eight pairs force the head component of any new r to be zero, making this an exact all-residual append obstruction. For the other 32, annihilate each retained antipodal residual pair and use an exact integer basis for the remaining kernel of dimension three or five. Restrict the actual old normals to this kernel, keeping only strongest copies; include every retained Leech equator projection. Dropping equator temporarily yields 23 apparent candidates above radius64, with squared maxima 72 or80, not numerical tolerance. On restoring it every one of the 32 full mixed residual polars has exact squared max≤48. Thus no new point anywhere at any of the 64 underfilled fixed b even with arbitrary real 13D residual and all old rows held.

Polytope maxima use exact incremental homogeneous vertex clipping. Begin with a containing coordinate box (free physical coordinates of a norm8 residual satisfy |x_i|≤8), then clip each actual halfspace. A pair of already-known vertices is an edge iff its common active constraints determine a face containing exactly those two vertices (tested directly against all known vertices). Each new vertex is a rational crossing on one such edge; the induction also handles lower-dimensional faces by retaining all boundary vertices. Use homogeneous integer coordinates, gcd reductions, and test the positive definite squared norm via exact ratios. A convex quadratic reaches its maximum at a vertex; when its max equals the target radius, strict convexity rules out nonvertices of that norm. The checker does not rely on a numerical polytope vertex vendor or a grid for these statements.

### Eleven genuine neutral swaps and their limits

Eleven partially switched fibres (indices in lexicographically sorted standard J bodies) are especially close. ## Moving-body follow-up

For each neutral pair, pay its two owner rows and let the replacement move in the four-dimensional span of the J-body, head midpoint, head difference, and D5 tail direction. The basis Gram is diagonal with entries 64, 32, 32, 32. Project all 232,228 retained 29D rows, including the Leech equator padded with zero tails, to obtain the exact wall system. Each polytope has maximal squared norm 128, exactly four possible rays on the sphere, and no compatible triangle. Moving the body in these eleven specified spans therefore still cannot turn the corresponding two-to-two exchange into a gain. This does not rule out unrestricted joint moves across the full construction.
