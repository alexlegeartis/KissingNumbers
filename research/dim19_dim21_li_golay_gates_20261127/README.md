## 19D: a 48-to-24 charging theorem for robust jointly redesigned heads and shoulders

Keep Li's twelve pure tail roots. Apart from those, choose any subset of Li's existing 12,460 points to retain. Consider adding any finite collection of rays with the following properties:

1. Each head sign is one of the 256 zero-tetrad sign heads that Li omitted. After positive rescaling individually, its 16 head magnitudes belong to \([39/40,41/40]\). The sign may repeat among the proposed rays.
2. Its three tail coordinates are arbitrary real numbers. The added rays are mutually compatible and compatible with the retained points.

Then the total cannot exceed 12,460. More specifically, at least **two** old *shoulders* must be removed per added ray, irrespective of which other old points are dropped. This does not cover strongly nonflat heads, other sign classes, simultaneous insertion of a different new family, or removal of a pure tail root.

**Structure.** Write the two blocks as \(\mathbb R^{16}\oplus\mathbb R^3\), in Li's 19D isometry. Norm-squared-8 shoulders have six head entries \(\pm1\) and tail \(v\), where \(v\) is one of the twelve vectors \((0,\pm1,\pm1)\) and permutations. For each \(v\) there are sixteen six-coordinate supports; for each such support all 32 even head sign patterns occur. The head signs are even because the original octad signs are odd and the two signs in the marked tetrad are opposite. Li's marked tetrad is \(T=\{1,4,7,9\}\) in one-based coordinates. With \(R\) the 3-by-4 sign matrix in §4, the twelve \(R(e_i-e_j)/2\) are precisely the twelve distinct stated tail directions, and the pure tail roots have tails \(2v\). The 96 supports are the octads of \(E^\perp\) that meet \(T\) twice, with \(T\) removed. The 256 omitted heads are the signs of the restrictions of \(z\in E\) with \(z_T=0\); \(z_T=T\) projects to the same head because \(T\in E\). Orthogonality makes each restriction to a shoulder support even. The literal finite check from the 12 tabulated generators of \(E\) establishes that every shoulder restriction has rank 5: each of the 32 even patterns occurs in exactly eight of the 256 heads. This is the only code-specific check needed below.

**At least 48 conflicts per insertion.** Put \(A=117/20\) and \(B=41/10\). Scale a new ray as in item 1, write it \((H,t)\), and put \(r=\|t\|\). Against the pure tail roots the maximum dot product is at least \(2r\), since the sum of the two largest absolute tail coordinates is at least \(r\). Compatibility with these norm-squared-8 roots gives \(2r\le\sqrt{2(\|H\|^2+r^2)}\), hence \(r\le\|H\|\le B\). On every shoulder support there is a unique old sign pattern which fully aligns with \(H\); its head dot product is at least \(A\). Designate only the fully aligned rows with the tail criterion below as conflicts. For \(r=0\) all orientations of those rows conflict since \(A>\sqrt2 B\). For \(r>0\), any \(v\) satisfying \(v\cdot(t/r)>47/82\) gives sixteen strict conflicts. Indeed,
\[
f(r)=\frac{\sqrt{2(B^2+r^2)}-A}{r}
\]
is increasing on \(0<r\le B\), since the sign of its derivative is that of \(A-\sqrt2 B^2/\sqrt{B^2+r^2}\), which is positive, and \(f(B)=2-A/B=47/82\). Thus \(A+v\cdot t\) exceeds even the upper bound \(\sqrt{2(B^2+r^2)}\) for the true dot threshold.

There are always three such \(v\). Let \(a\ge b\ge c\ge0\) be the sorted absolute coordinates of \(t/r\). Two distinct orientations give \(a+b\) and \(a+c\), each at least \(1/\sqrt2\) because \(a^2+b^2+c^2\le2(a+c)^2\). For a third distinct direction choose the larger \(s=\max(b+c,a-c)\). As \(a\le s+c\), \(b\le s-c\), and \(c\le s/2\), we have \(1\le (s+c)^2+(s-c)^2+c^2\le 11s^2/4\). So the third value is at least \(2/\sqrt{11}>47/82\). Consequently three disjoint oriented sixteen-support banks contribute at least 48 fully aligned blockers.

**At most 24 charges per old shoulder.** Fix an omitted head sign. Any allowed magnitudes have mean at least \(39/40\) and variance at most \(1/40^2\). Their head direction thus makes an angle at most \(\arctan(1/39)\) with the flat direction of that sign, and two such heads have cosine \(\ge\kappa=760/761\). If one of the proposed rays with that sign has zero tail, it excludes all other rays with that sign: their cosine is at least \(\kappa/\sqrt2>1/2\). Otherwise let \(a',b'\) denote their two tail-to-head norm ratios in \((0,1]\). The normalized tail directions have dot product at most
\[
\frac{\tfrac12\sqrt{(1+a'^2)(1+b'^2)}-\kappa}{a'b'}.
\]
When the numerator is nonpositive this is at most zero. Otherwise \(a'^2,b'^2>2\kappa^2-1\), so it is at most \((1-\kappa)/(2\kappa^2-1)<1/500\). For a fixed old shoulder with tail \(v\), all designated nonzero tail directions \(u\) obey \(e\cdot u>c\), where \(e=v/\sqrt2\), \(c=47/(82\sqrt2)\), and \(c^2>1/500\). Their projections into the plane \(e^\perp\) have strictly negative pairwise dot products, because \((u-(e\cdot u)e)\cdot(u'-(e\cdot u')e)\le 1/500-c^2<0\). At most three nonzero planar vectors can be pairwise strictly obtuse: four directions around a circle would have an adjacent angular gap of at most \(90^\circ\). If a projection vanishes, \(u=e\) and it alone exceeds the allowed tail dot with each other direction in the cap. If a zero-tail ray is charged instead, it was already isolated within its sign. Thus at most three rays of any fixed matching head sign charge this shoulder. Each old shoulder has eight matching signs, so it receives at most \(8\cdot3=24\) charges. Counting the incidences yields \(48\,\#\text{new}\le24\,\#\text{removed shoulders}\), proving the stronger two-for-one claim.

The advance over the previous fixed-body flat-head obstruction is this strict two-for-one gap under **joint** shoulder removals, with \(\pm2.5\%\) head amplitude variation and unconstrained tails. To escape, change sign syndrome or permit substantially nonflat heads or a different pure-tail shell, not just free more shoulders. An attractive structural route is to group prospective donors by a nonzero tetrad syndrome and reuse the existing 112 phase cosets while redesigning the root/shoulder charges together. This is prospective, not a candidate.

## 21D: line-pivot pair swaps and a uniform-amplitude no-go theorem

Use Li's punctured Golay code \(D\) of size 4096, its 21 distance-five words \(\mathcal L\) forming \(PG(2,4)\), and all 210 odd-signed base octads \(\mathcal O\). The roots plus octads account for 27,720 points; Li obtains 3,840 more using line tags. The proposed replacement below is a different pair-swap law, not another labeling of Li's weakened lines.

Fix a line at infinity \(L_\infty\). Pair distinct coordinates disjointly. For each pair \((j,k)\), choose a pivot \(i\in L_\infty\), distinct from both and collinear with them. For word \(w\in D\) give these coordinates respective magnitudes \(\alpha_p+q_p\chi_i(w)\), \(\alpha_p-q_p\chi_i(w)\), allowing a fixed sign reversal of \(q_p\) per pair, where \(\chi_i(w)=(-1)^{\mathrm{wt}(w)+w_i}\). Unpaired magnitudes are fixed. All are positive. The norm squared \(N=\sum_j\alpha_j^2+2\sum_pq_p^2\) is constant, with \(\alpha_j=\alpha_k=\alpha_p\) on a pair. The five pivot characters always multiply to one by Golay duality: the lift of \(L_\infty\) is an octad containing the punctured triple, and its dot product with the lift of any \(w\) is zero mod 2. All sixteen even phase patterns occur by the replay.

For the word difference given by a line \(P\), the relative pair phase is \(+1\) if the pivot is on \(P\), \(-1\) otherwise. If it is \(-1\), the terms linear in \(q_p\) cancel coordinate-by-coordinate. If it is \(+1\), \(P\) contains both members or neither by collinearity, and these terms cancel across the pair. The pair's contribution to \(N/2-\mathrm{dot}(P)\) proportional to \(q_p^2\) is \(g(P,p)=1-\sigma(2-2m)\), where \(\sigma\) is the relative phase and \(m=|P\cap\{j,k\}|\). The remaining contribution is \(-\frac12\sum_j\alpha_j^2+2\sum_{j\in P}\alpha_j^2\). For fixed pair \(g\) has counts \((-1,1,3)\mapsto(4,8,9)\), so its sum is 31. Summing all 21 slacks and using that each coordinate lies in five lines gives \(31\sum_pq_p^2\ge\frac12\sum_j\alpha_j^2\) as a necessary condition for compatibility of all \(4096\) tagged rays. Equivalently, at least \(1/32\) of the squared norm must be in pair variation.

**Theorem (uniform amplitudes).** If there are \(k=9\) or \(10\) disjoint pairs, all means are one and all absolute deviations equal \(q\), then the full 4096 cannot be compatible both internally and with the unchanged odd Golay octads. Dense compatibility requires \(q^2\ge 21/(62k)\), so \(q>9/50\). For any phase profile, write the 21 magnitude entries as \(1+qv_j\), where \(v_j\in\{-1,0,1\}\), \(\sum v_j=0\), \(\sum v_j^2=2k\). The octads have incidence \(r=80,\lambda=28\), as checked literally; thus the mean square of \(E_O=\sum_{j\in O}v_j\) over them is \((80-28)2k/210>4\), so some \(|E_O|\ge3\). For this octad \(E_O\) is a linear form with integer coefficients in the five pivot signs. If any coefficient vanishes, the best even-total phase attains the sum of the absolute coefficients. If none vanish it attains at least the sum of their absolute values minus twice the minimum, which is at least 3. Hence an actual phase realizes \(E_O\ge3\).

A head's sign mask has even overlap with this octad: the octad is zero at the punctured coordinates, so its dot product with the lifted Golay word equals that overlap and is even. Relative signs therefore stay odd. For that octad the support function of its odd sign rows is \(\sum_{j\in O}(1+qv_j)-2\min_{j\in O}(1+qv_j)\). If it contains a low coordinate this is at least \(6+5q\). If no low coordinate occurs, at most three are unmatched, so at least five are high. The same bound holds when there is an unmatched coordinate; if all eight are high the result is \(6+6q\). Compatibility needs \((6+5q)^2\le2(21+2kq^2)\), but \( -6+60q+(25-4k)q^2>0\) on \(9/50\le q<1\) for \(k=9,10\). The derivative is positive and its value at \(9/50\) is already positive. Contradiction.

Only uniform amplitude full-code pair swaps with this line-pivot architecture are excluded. Heterogeneous continuous scouting found no feasible assignment; it was not a certificate. As a distinct next move, replace equal q by support-dependent q and control the *octad-versus-moment sum* analytically, or use deliberate repeated finite-neighborhood contacts to change the radius profiles in a non-affine set of omitted words.

## 21D Hermitian finite-neighborhood obstruction (optional next-round handoff)

In the prior 1024-word component's Hermitian model, its two sides are each \(\mathrm{Herm}_3(\mathbb F_4)\). In block form write \((u,z,H)\) with \(u\in\mathbb F_2\), \(z\in\mathbb F_4^2\), and \(K=H+zz^\dagger\in\mathrm{Herm}_2(\mathbb F_4)\). The sixteen affine rank-one line moves give, from a center \((u,z,K)\), the unique neighbor in slice \(z'\):
\[
(u+1,z',K+B(z,z')),\qquad B(z,z')=zz'^\dagger+z'z^\dagger.
\]
The five lines at infinity leave \(u,z\) untouched. For centers in distinct \(z,w\) slices of one fixed \(u\), their affine neighborhoods are disjoint exactly when \(K_z+K_w\) is *outside* \(\operatorname{im} B(z+w,\cdot)\). The image has binary rank three. Its unique nonzero annihilator \(\ell_{z+w}\) is constant on each of the five projective directions, and any four of the five functionals are independent. The replay verifies these literal facts in the \(2\times2\) block.

If the occupied finite \(z\)-slices contained three collinear positions, then all three binary pair differences on their common functional would have to be one, a contradiction. For \(s\) occupied slices such a set is a cap, hence \(s\le6\) because each \(z\) sees the other points in distinct among five directions. At each occupied slice, the \(s-1\) distinct neighbor directions fix \(s-1\) functional values on all its centers (at most 4 independent), so there are at most \(2^{4-\min(s-1,4)}\) centers per slice. For \(s=3,4,5,6\) the entire count is at most 12,8,5,6 respectively. Thus a collision-free family of at least 13 centers in one \(u\)-block uses at most two \(z\)-slices. This only obstructs *perfect* finite-neighborhood separation, not general independent domination or the full phase graph. Deliberate controlled collisions are the natural escape.
