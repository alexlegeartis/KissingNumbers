## I. Full-D4 92D fibre obstruction for arbitrary real added heads

Use the original 22 complete four-coordinate body rows, body norm²88, three row-frame colours, and each fixed two-symbol Z8 prefix giving exactly 8^10 body words. Work in the **published sufficient Gram envelope**, not actual pointwise extrema: within a fibre body dot≤40, for distinct same-colour prefixes body dot≤48−p with p the sum of two prefix frame dots, different colours body dot≤44. Prefix symbols use the signed-axis metric (identical 4, opposite −4, other 0), so distinct same-colour prefix pairs have p≤4. The mixed condition is body cap plus physical head dot ≤half the product of total radii. Within-fibre certificate requires head norm²r≤8. Retain all 24 current scaled D4 big heads, with arbitrary new colours and prefix assignments. Then **at most 32 full fibres** can be certified in this architecture, even with arbitrary variable-length real extra heads. The existing literal32 attains it. This does not bound 92D, alternate large-stock geometries, stronger exact conditional body envelopes, smaller subfibres, or other prefix profiles.

**Real reduction to dual templates.** For any additional head h let r=||h||² and S be the sum of the largest two absolute coordinates. Against all 24 big heads, the maximum raw head dot is 2S. Since even the best prefix cap is 44, necessarily
`44+2S ≤ M(r):=sqrt(24(88+r))`.
For dimension 4, r≤S²: write x₁≥x₂≥...≥0 and maximize x₁²+3x₂² on fixed x₁+x₂=S. With s=sqrt(r), this requires 20s²−176s+176≥0. The polynomial decreases on [0,sqrt8] and is negative at 7/6, so s<7/6. In particular M<139/3, S<7/6, and τ:=(48−M)/2>5/6. A same-colour big/small pair with p≤−4 is impossible. At p=0 its negative head contact requires signed coordinate sum at least τ in absolute units (head dot≤−2τ).

All eligible p=0 big roots of h are contained in one of **24** abstract six-root neighbour sets: those negative at least 2 to a short dual 24-cell direction (±e_i or (±1/2)^4). To see this, if any eligible signed sum uses a *difference* of absolute coordinates reaching τ (including a zero), its larger coordinate is ≥τ and every other coordinate is <7/6−5/6=1/3. No sum of two others can reach τ, so every eligible root is incident to the same opposing signed axis, described by the axial template. Otherwise, both signs of any eligible pair align with the negatives of the respective signs of h; all lie in a single diagonal template. Empty eligibility can use any template.

Two small heads in the same colour need p=4, since p≤0 would require a negative dot more than their length product given both r<(7/6)². Their prefixes therefore lie on one common row or column in the two-symbol array, with the other symbols from distinct axes (not opposite). This follows by taking any two distinct prefixes sharing one coordinate: any third sharing one coordinate with each must have that same fixed coordinate. Thus at most four small heads in each colour.

**Repeated-template exclusion.** If two smalls use the same abstract template and each requires p=0 compatibility against at least four distinct big roots, they cannot coexist. Apply a D4 automorphism taking their shared template to +e_0. Its six targets are 2(−e_0±e_j) (j=1,2,3); four contain both signs on a residual axis, forcing the e_0 component a≥τ>5/6. If a≤7/8, then r≤a²+3(7/6−a)²≤37/36 by convexity on [5/6,7/8], so M<185/4 and τ>7/8, a contradiction. For two such heads their axial components a,b are both >7/8 and <7/6, while each residual norm is <sqrt3·7/24. Now 4ab−a²−b² is increasing in a and b there, hence >2(7/8)², which equals 6·3(7/24)². Cauchy–Schwarz thus gives 4h·k>r+s (using s here for the second squared head norm), but any coexisting pair under the envelope has h·k≤sqrt((88+r)(88+s))/2−44≤(r+s)/4. Contradiction. This proof does not require actual h to be a dual small root or have common length.

**Exact finite quotient.** Fix k small labels in a colour; their canonical prefixes are (0,0),...,(0,k−1), by signed-prefix symmetries. A graph vertex assigns a possible big root to a distinct prefix p, obeying p2=4 versus each small or p2=0 and membership in its template. Two vertices with distinct roots and prefixes are adjacent precisely when big/big head dot≤prefix dot. Take maximum clique with the repeated-template exclusion above (only needed for k=3,4). The signed permutations and a half-Hadamard give 1,152 simultaneous geometric automorphisms; the standalone script checks that their permutations preserve all large-root dots and template incidences. For k=2,3,4 these leave respectively 5,15,59 multiset orbits. Exact maxima of big roots are 12,6,5; k=1 also ≤12: for a putative >6 big group at least one is a p=4 neighbour of the sole small, so normalize its prefix to (0,1) and its big head to one of the three possible dot classes with the axial template. Its three neighbourhood cliques each have size at most 11. In all searches greedy proper colouring supplies only an upper bound and complete exhaustive branching closes every residual; no assumption of attaining the colouring bound. Thus totals by k=1,2,3,4 are ≤13,14,9,9. Any set of 33 retaining all 24 big would need ≥9 smalls across three colours; enumerate k=1..4 with sum≥9 and the totals never exceed32. `node lower101_92d_d4_phase_capacity.cjs` passed exact replay. Practical consequence: a genuinely new 33rd full fibre requires a change to the large D4 stock or a stronger body-separation certificate; merely rephasing the small radius catalogue is insufficient.

## II. All-real 95D obstruction to compressing one row into three head dimensions

Use 23 four-coordinate body rows, norm²92, three colours and one fixed Z8 symbol per full fibre, each size 8^11=8,589,934,592. With the usual sufficient caps, the norms of heads obey r≤4 (same-fibre body dot≤44); different same-colour symbols have p≤0, so their body cap is ≥48; different colours have body cap46. Then at most **13 full fibres** can be certified in 95D, insufficient to beat current even with the inherited 145,004,544 Golay body-only points. This is not a bound on other codes or exact smaller pair maxima.

In one colour, head dots are nonpositive, strictly negative against a head of norm²<4. A colour containing such a short head is at most 4: its negative Gram graph is connected, and an irreducible PSD matrix with off-diagonal nonpositives has nullity at most one (Perron after a diagonal shift), so rank≤3 implies size≤4. An all-norm²4 colour is ≤6; splitting the negative Gram graph into components puts each singular one at nullity one with a strictly positive dependence. Size6 forces three orthogonal antipodal pairs. Size5 forces either two antipodal pairs plus an orthogonal singleton, or a pair plus a rank-two positively dependent triple in the perpendicular plane.

If a colour has 6, use the signed coordinate axes it supplies. Every other head of squared norm r obeys `2 sqrt(r/3) ≤ 2||h||_∞ ≤ sqrt(96(92+r))/2−46 ≤ 1+r/4`, hence r<4/3 (strict from AM–GM when r<4, and r=4 fails). No two such heads can share an outside colour: their head dot ≥−sqrt(rs)>−4/3, while same-colour body≥48 and total cap<46+2/3 force less than −4/3. So total≤8 in this case.
If a pure colour has five and contains two antipodal pairs plus a singleton, any pure head in another colour has first two absolute coordinates ≤1, third coordinate ≤1 and therefore third≤−sqrt2 using norm²4. Two such can have nonpositive dot only at exact equatorial residual antipodes, so there are ≤2 in a different pure colour. If the pure five instead has an antipodal pair and triangle, the coordinate along that pair has absolute ≤1, leaving transverse norm≥sqrt3. All other pure heads must lie in the gaps between triangle directions beyond angle θ=arccos(1/sqrt3) of each boundary. Since the three gaps are <π by positive dependence of the triangle, each allowed interval has width <π−2θ and mutual transverse cosine >1/3. Two heads in one interval would have strictly positive total dot (transverse >1, axial at least −1), so a second pure colour holds ≤3. In either case there cannot be two pure colours of size≥5. This proves 5+4+4=13 as required.

## III. Constructive 102 complete 95D E7 fibres from two deleted rows; below live

Use scaled E7 roots: 56 signed ordered root differences 2(e_i−e_j) and 70 balanced eight-sign heads. Split into coordinate blocks of four and verify an integer arrangement of **102** roots with distinct valid (colour,prefix) pairs.
- Colour 0: 36 balanced labels with two positive signs in each block. Each six-state block is an orthogonal set of three antipodal signed axes (two-subsets modulo complement). Its two prefix coordinates give exactly its Gram. Add the two all-one vs all-minus block choices in the unoccupied prefix axis pair, for 38.
- Colour 1: the 32 complementary types of a unique positive in one block and a unique negative in the other. For either four-site block its four one-positive words are orthogonal and their negatives give the other four symbols, again exact prefix Gram.
- Colour 2: 32 ordered root differences crossing the two coordinate blocks. Keep both directions; the two prefix coordinates exactly encode the signed axial components.

Each has 8^10 words, giving **109,521,666,048** dense (or **109,666,670,592** retaining the previously certified Golay body-only layer with zero head, unchanged ordinary rows and their existing frame/radius mixed bounds).
