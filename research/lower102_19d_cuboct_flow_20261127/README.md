## Setup and exact status

Write the room as R^16 plus R^3, with raw squared radius eight and raw dot threshold four. Keep the equatorial odd Barnes–Wall frame (4,320), the full mixed coordinate and pure-tail roots (192+12), and twelve shoulder banks (12×16×32 = 6,144). A shoulder tail is one of the twelve vectors v=(0,±1,±1) including permutations. At each v choose any bent coset z of RM(1,4) in RM(2,4), use all sixteen of its weight-six supports, and on each support take all 32 signs of one parity, which may depend on the support. The sparse total is 10,668. A proposed dense ray is ((2/3)(−1)^c, (sqrt(8)/3)d) for an arbitrary real unit tail d∈S². Different words may use different tails; no need to occupy a whole affine coset at once.

**Theorems.** If every dense direction is one of the normalized bank directions, at most 1,536 dense rays are possible for *any* compatible choice of the twelve banks (the argument is analytic aside from the small Reed–Muller minimum census). If all eight triples of cuboctahedron vertices forming a triangle have bank labels whose sum is zero, the **same bound holds with arbitrary real directions for all dense rays**. This second condition is equivalent to a binary-valued closed-flow/face-potential model, and does not hold for every cuboctahedral sparse body. Either claim leaves a strict gap of 256 below Li. Neither constrains Li's different radii and marked heads, a body built from partial rather than full banks, nor unclosed bank labels combined with arbitrary tails. This is not a dimension-19 upper bound. See companion standalone verify.cjs (Node, no packages or inputs).

## Algebra connecting sparse and dense

The odd affine octads force c∈RM(2,4), because an odd signed octad exactly aligned with c gives 16/3>4 if its parity agrees. The quotient Q=RM(2,4)/RM(1,4) is six-dimensional, with q1 meaning bent and alternating form B(x,z) the overlap parity. B is nondegenerate; on the six quadratic monomials it pairs complementary pairs of variables. This form polarizes the binary quadratic bent indicator. A nonzero bent difference has minimum weight six; a nonzero other difference has minimum weight four. Each head coset has 32 words. The companion literally builds all evaluation masks.

Distinct bent banks on adjacent (positive dot) cuboctahedral directions must have bent difference: on weight-six supports their maximum intersection is three for bent difference and four otherwise (checked exactly). An intersection of size four has spare sites on both supports to choose any parity, and adds to the tail dot one to violate four. Equal labels on positive directions fail even for opposite parity (same support, head max at least four). On other nonantipodal directions, equal labels necessitate opposite parity at every common support, since equal parity would give head max six and tail dot >−2. For dense compatibility at positive v·d one must match the *opposite* parity to each retained shoulder support: otherwise its head dot four aligns fully and the positive tail product fails. If parity is opposite then the head dot is at most 8/3, and even the largest possible tail product 4/3 is harmless. For nonpositive v·d either parity works. The desired parity is constant across a bent coset's weight-six supports for a fixed head c, because c is orthogonal to RM(1,4). For an upper bound we can assume one desired parity p_e per bank: within each repeated label choose the same arbitrary test support, and set p_e equal to opposite old parity on that support. Every truly admissible ray obeys the resulting equations, and repeated labels in nonantipodal directions still force opposite p_e. Hence arbitrary support-dependent parities are covered.

Writing c modulo RM(1,4) as x, the necessary direction condition is B(x,z_e)=p_e whenever v_e·d>0. A head word cannot repeat even at opposite tails (dot≥56/9>4). Two words at Hamming distance four require d·d'≤1/2, while distinct words at distance at least six always fit. At a given bank direction v, its four positive neighbors need different labels: a repeated label would require equal desired parity because both are positive against d, but the two neighbors cannot be antipodal and hence require opposite p. Also all neighbors have labels distinct from the central bank. Thus any occupied bank direction imposes equations of rank at least three. Rank at least four gives at most 4×32 words. At rank three the eight possible quotient classes are an affine space; its translation space has a nonzero nonbent element. Otherwise any basis a,b,c would have q(a)=q(b)=q(c)=q(a+b)=q(a+c)=q(b+c)=1, hence B(a,b)=B(a,c)=B(b,c)=1 and q(a+b+c)=0. Translation by a fixed weight-four representative pairs the 256 words by forbidden equal-direction edges, giving at most 128. Summing twelve slots proves the first result.

## Closed flow reduces direction choices to 14 generators

View the twelve v_e as edge-midpoints of a cube. Cube triangles of banks are the triples meeting at a cube corner. Its six faces are labelled +X,−X,+Y,−Y,+Z,−Z, and each cube edge has two incident faces a,b, so v_e=e_a+e_b. If all triangles close, labels are differences F_a+F_b of six Q-valued face potentials. Indeed a dual octahedron has its triangular cycle space generated by the eight cube corner triangles; their rank is seven (the only dependency is their total, because every primal edge has two endpoints and the cube is connected), which equals 12−6+1. Face potentials therefore exist by path integration or directly using the 5-dimensional space of gradients.

Every admissible cone consists of weak inequalities v_e·d≤0 for labels whose parity fails. It is generated by its feasible vectors among eight body-diagonals (±1,±1,±1) and the six signed ordinary axes. To see this, after signed coordinate permutations put d=(x,y,z) with x≥y≥|z|. Then d=(x−y)(1,0,0)+((y+z)/2)(1,1,1)+((y−z)/2)(1,1,−1). Each of the six linear forms x±y,x±z,y±z is weakly sign-consistent on the chamber and its three generators. If a boundary form is zero at d, every generator with positive coefficient also kills it. This proves the exact cone claim without angular sampling. A diagonal activates the three edges incident to its corner; an axis activates the four edges bordering its face.

Normalize F_(+X)=0 and F_(+Y)=12 in the replay’s six-bit Reed–Muller coordinates. These faces are adjacent, so F_(+Y) is bent before relabeling. GL(4,2) acting on Boolean variables is transitive on rank-four alternating polar forms (symplectic basis construction), and therefore on bent cosets; it preserves all geometry and desired signs. Label F_(−Y),F_(±Z) must be bent because those faces adjoin +X; each Z-face potential differs bently from each Y-face potential; F_(−X) must differ bently from all four Y/Z potentials. These exact conditions enumerate 3,168 labelled possibilities, no assumption on antipodal face labels. Phases are twelve bits p, filtered for necessary opposite bits on repeated nonantipodal edge labels. The attainable tags are the five bits B(x,F_j) for the nonzero-index faces, an r-dimensional set. The tag difference on each cube edge is its two face-bit xor. A phase can be represented by the minimum in its orbit under xor of an attainable tag difference: translating heads by the corresponding x leaves all pairwise demands unchanged.

## Exact certificate and continuous capacity

With rank r=5 each tag lifts to two quotient classes. Among all 4,096 phase choices, at most 24 of 32 possible tags have any of the fourteen feasible rays, so at most 48 quotient classes ×32 words. For smaller rank ignore cases with at most 48 eligible classes. The literal census remaining after the phase gauge is:

| rank | eligible quotient classes | greedy forced matching size | number of cases |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 64 | 0 | 12 |
| 3 | 64 | 0 | 540 |
| 3 | 64 | 16 | 2,160 |
| 3 | 64 | 32 | 1,200 |
| 4 | 56 | 0 | 3,600 |

A forced matching edge joins two different quotient classes at nonbent difference whose two lists of feasible generating rays have *all* cross cosines strictly above 1/2. All true cone directions then have cross cosine strictly above 1/2, by writing nonnegative sums of normalized generators and the triangle inequality. Adding one fixed weight-four representative gives a perfect matching between the two 32-word cosets; their total is at most 32, irrespective of the separate pointwise chosen directions. Thus the 3,360 cases with greedy matching size at least 16 already have ≤48×32 words. Matching optimality is unnecessary.

For each of the 552 unhandled rank-two/three cases, the checker finds that every nonempty cone has either one diagonal, a pair of antipodal diagonals (a line), or two diagonals corresponding to one edge of the cube. The actual edge set admits assigning its edge midpoints to distinct incident corners. Assign every non-midpoint direction to the nearer admissible diagonal, and assign those midpoint directions using this injection. Around each corner, after sign flips, the directions have two coordinates one and the third in [0,1], or all three one, before normalization. Their cross cosines exceed 1/2 except for the two zero endpoints on different incoming edges. The midpoint assignment puts at most one such endpoint in each corner group. Furthermore each head is eligible at the corresponding diagonal by construction.

In all 3,600 rank-four cases the checker finds that all cone directions lie in two opposed square caps of one fixed signed coordinate. Concretely all generating rays of each class lie in a single cap (dominant coordinate ±1, other coordinates in [−1,1]), or the cone is an antipodal corner line. Any class admissible at the cap’s axis is admissible at some diagonal in the same cap. Split each square cap by the signs of its transverse coordinates, and orient the four edge-midpoint assignments cyclically so each corner gets one. Two directions in the same signed quadrant can be represented with dominant coordinate one and both nonnegative transverse coordinates ≤1. The four corner vectors of this smaller square generate it; their normalized cross cosines are ≥1/2, with equality only at the two different edge-midpoints (transverse (0,1) and (1,0)). The same holds for normalized convex combinations, with equality possible only for that original pair. Orienting midpoints ensures strict >1/2 inside each group. Axis directions can be put in an eligible diagonal group, while for any non-axis ray the three cut edges of its chosen diagonal are already positive (there is at least one nonzero transverse coordinate, and signs of zeros can be chosen either way). Thus all words charged to a corner again meet the three associated parity equations.

In both geometric cases, the rank-two parity slice at a corner contains 512 words (if not empty) and each corner group has minimum Hamming distance six. Here is an independent small exact 160 bound, not a hidden numeric optimizer. The 16-class quotient of each corner slice has five nonzero singular differences, any four independent and all five summing to zero. For each of the 168 possible ordered-by-index bent basis pairs of a rank-two corner plane, the verifier chooses one weight-four representative of each of the five singular differences so their xor is zero (only 4^5 trials per pair). They generate a 16-word linear section and its translates partition the 512-word slice into 32 sets. Each has the five forbidden differences of a Clebsch graph, namely four standard coordinates and their sum. Every independent set there has size at most five: translate a selected vertex to zero, leaving only words of weights two or three. At most three weight-two words fit because complements are forbidden. With one weight-three vertex at most three weight-two words can fit, since each must contain that triple's missing site; with two such triples at most one weight-two word fits, and with at least three none fit. Thus eight groups give at most 8×32×5=1,280. Together with the other cases this proves the continuum ceiling 1,536 for the closed-flow family.

## Mathematical next branch

The exclusion does not mean every direction is forced onto a mesh. It says the *closed* F2^6 face-potential construction cannot pay for the 1,793rd dense ray even with separate arbitrary real tails. A meaningful escape is an **open flow** with nonzero defects at cube corners, or a body with some incomplete banks to remove exact parity obstructions; repeat neither the old 38-coset fixed palette scan nor the same-slot scheme. Another distinct route is a two-radius dense code with amplitudes keeping parity-bad shoulders safe in small positive sectors, and then pricing lost dense neighbors together. Such proposals are not constructions until the actual total and pair conditions are certified.
