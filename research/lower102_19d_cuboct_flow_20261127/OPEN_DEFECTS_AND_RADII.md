# 19D follow-up: two adjacent cuboct defects, and radial changes cannot create new eligible signs

## Adjacent open-flow defects

Use the band-combinatorial notation of the companion. A nonzero defect at a cube corner is the sum in Q of the labels of its three incident edges. Consider *all* bank labelings in which precisely two defects are nonzero, at adjacent corners. They necessarily have the same syndrome, because the sum of all eight defects is zero. Change the label on the connecting edge by that syndrome and every corner closes. In fact the corrected label is the sum of the two other labels at either corner; these are distinct bent labels with bent difference by sparse compatibility. Each corrected pair on that corner is then bent as well. Thus the corrected labels satisfy the phase-independent positive-angle conditions of the closed-flow census. They need not satisfy nonadjacent sparse parity constraints under the original phases; our enumeration does not assume that they do.

Therefore enumerate all one-edge mutations of each of the 3,168 normalized face systems of the closed-flow certificate, using any distinct bent label that still has bent difference with each of the four positive neighbors of the changed bank. Deduplicate label vectors. There are 86,400 different labelled systems after this normalization convention. Some systems describe the same geometry through a different relabeling; duplicates outside this normalization are not needed. Now allow any original twelve phase bits satisfying the necessary opposite requirement for repeated nonantipodal labels; use the same harmless uniformization for per-support parities. A repeated label bank with three nonantipodal banks of that label needs impossible pairwise opposite parity: two of these three neighbors cannot be antipodal to each other, making a parity-odd triangle. This is only a pruning step; all surviving phase candidates still check every required pair. Gauge by the actual image of the sign translation map Q→F₂¹².

A sign coset has *some* valid direction iff its relaxed parity cone contains one of the 14 diagonal/axis generators. An eligible sign coset has at most 32 words. It is therefore enough to treat gauges with more than 56 eligible cosets. There are 52,560 such gauges after checking the necessary repeated-label phases: 11,520 with 60 and 41,040 with 64. Of these, 38,160 have enough disjoint forced-conflict edges to charge them down to 56 sign cosets or fewer. A charge follows exactly the all-generator strict angle test and actual weight-four perfect matching in the main proof. The remaining 14,400 have only diagonal points, antipodal diagonal lines, or cube-edge arcs as their cones, and their active arc midpoints can go to distinct incident corners. The same continuous octant-group argument applies. At each corner the parity slice has either rank two, with ≤160 by the proved Clebsch section, or rank three, with ≤128 by a nonzero nonbent translation as in the same-slot theorem. Thus all such gauges have ≤1,280 words. Overall the relaxed dense population is at most **1,792**, so no more than **12,460** total. No sharpness or tie construction is claimed. The remaining scope is genuine nonlocal defects, such as two nonadjacent corners, four or more defects, or changed shoulder shapes and radii.

## Variable flat head amplitude: empty cones cannot become nonempty

Now do not fix the common head amplitude to 2/3; each new ray may instead have its own amplitude a≥0 and tail length r=√(8−16a²). Keep the entire sparse body only. Every such ray satisfies **a>√(3/8)**, with the following proof independent of every bank label and phase. Write d for its unit tail (a zero tail cannot occur because then a=1/√2 and the equator's octad dot is at least 6a>4). Set M=max_i |d_i| and S=sum of the two largest |d_i|. Pure tail roots give rS≤2 and S≥1, so a≥1/2. Mixed coordinate roots give M≤(2−a)/r. On a bank pointed in the signs of the two largest coordinates the maximal head dot is at least 4a, regardless of parity, hence S≤(4−4a)/r. Also M≥1/√3 and S≥M+√((1−M²)/2). If a≤a*=√(3/8), both upper functions (2−a)/r and (4−4a)/r increase on [1/2,a*], as seen by differentiating. At a* they are less than 983/1000 and 11/10 respectively by squaring. The concave function f(M)=M+√((1−M²)/2) has its minimum on [1/√3,983/1000] at an endpoint, and both endpoint values exceed 11/10. This contradicts the required S bounds. The ordinary head signs must consequently lie in RM(2,4) or they align with an odd equatorial octad and give 8a>4. The retained equator also requires a≤2/3. No sign word can repeat at any two different amplitudes either: the maximal tail norms obey r,r'<√2, and the smallest possible dot for a shared sign is 16aa'−rr'>6−2=4.

A wrong parity bank (on the selected representative support used to define p_e) imposes v_e·d≤(4−6a)/r. This expression decreases on [a*,2/3], hence is smaller than (4−6a*)/√2=2√2−3√3/2<1/√5. Suppose the original strict-radius parity cone for that sign was empty except zero. The origin is then strictly interior to the convex hull of its wrong bank normals, by elementary separation; no proper affine hull or supporting hyperplane through zero can satisfy that assumption. The support function in any unit direction is at least the centered inradius. Every facet through three noncollinear cuboctahedron rows has positive squared distance from the origin at least 1/5: for a,b,c use N=(b−a)×(c−a), h=N·a and check |N|²≤5h² whenever h≠0. Of 220 possible triples, 128 have nonzero h and 24 attain equality, exactly checked; every hull vertex is one of the old integer normals, so the finite certificate covers every hull of a subset. Thus some wrong bank has dot at least 1/√5 in every unit direction, contradiction.

It follows that if a head sign ever fits after changing its common amplitude, its original flat-amplitude cone has at least one nonzero vector. This is merely an eligibility theorem, not a guarantee that old and new angular positions coincide. Forced matchings and the continuous cap arguments from constant amplitude were not reused silently. Nonetheless this gives **no strict improvement even with arbitrary amplitude variation in all closed-flow rank-four/five cases**, because at most 56 quotient classes are eligible and no head sign can repeat. The remaining technically meaningful radial problem in the closed-flow family is rank two/three: a variable radius could redistribute the same head signs enough to evade the previous angular argument, but it cannot immigrate absent signs. The exact amplitude interval and facet margin give a reusable guard for that construction.

## Variable-radius refinement: one genuinely surviving closed-flow case

The eligibility result leaves rank-two/three gauges with 64 eligible quotient classes. In fact all except 180 of the normalized phase gauges are now ruled out even with arbitrary individual flat amplitudes. The additional replay confirms the exact shape of the wrong normals, not an angular sample. This refinement is for closed labels; no variable-radius claim is made for adjacent open defects.

Write δ=(4−6a)/r<1/4, using a>sqrt(3/8) from the preceding proof. For two amplitudes a,b, a distance-four pair needs tail cosine at most T=(4−8ab)/(r_a r_b), and
T²=1/4+32(a−b)²/(r_a² r_b²).
For a≤b the second term decreases in a and increases in b (differentiate, using 16ab<8), so it is maximized at sqrt(3/8) and 2/3. Their difference is less than 11/200 and their squared radii product is 16/9, giving T²<1/4+18(11/200)²<(14/25)². Thus any universal tail cosine bound above 14/25 forbids a Hamming-four pair at *all* permitted radii.

For each of the 3,360 charged rank-three gauges, the checked matching only uses pairs whose old cones are both the identical singleton diagonal direction. The wrong normals perpendicular to such a diagonal must positively span its transverse plane: otherwise a small transverse perturbation would keep all weakly active normals nonpositive, and the strictly negative normals remain negative, contradicting the singleton. The available transverse normals form a hexagon of radius sqrt(2). Any subset positively spanning that plane has gaps at most 120°, so its centered inradius is at least 1/sqrt(2). Hence a relaxed direction has transverse norm less than 1/(2sqrt(2)). It must be on the original sign side: some inactive wrong normal made dot −2/sqrt(3) with the old diagonal (otherwise both signs would be feasible), and has transverse norm sqrt(2/3). On the opposite sign side its dot is greater than (2sqrt(7/8)−1/2)/sqrt(3)>1/4. Therefore all relaxed tails in the same singleton direction have mutual cosine above 3/4. The same coset matching exclusion persists exactly.

In the remaining 552 closed geometric gauges above 48 eligible cosets and without enough cone matching, the revised replay verifies two precise possible wrong-normal sets for every head:
* The old cone consists of an antipodal diagonal line, and **all six** transverse hexagon normals are wrong.
* The old cone is an edge arc of the cube. After coordinate signs and ordering the edge runs between (−1,1,1) and (1,1,1). Its wrong normals impose |y−z|≤δ and |x|≤min(y,z)+δ.
All 12 rank-two gauges have only lines. In rank three, 360 gauges have four disjoint arc edges (parallel within each gauge); the other 180 have eight arc edges, two incident to each corner, and are not covered by the argument below.

For a line the complete transverse hexagon has centered inradius sqrt(3/2), so relaxed directions lie within transverse norm 1/sqrt(24) of one of the two signs. This alone keeps every rank-two charge within an angular class of diameter below the Hamming-four angle. For a rank-three gauge with disjoint arcs assign a relaxed arc direction to the endpoint of its sign of x (any side at zero), and a line direction by its nearer diagonal sign. Every corner has only one possible incident arc type, so use center u=(1,2,2)/3 in the signed ordering appropriate to that edge coordinate. All these directions have dot with u greater than 53/60:
* On the line side, u·w=5/(3sqrt(3)) for the unit original diagonal w. The lower bound (5/(3sqrt(3)))sqrt(23/24)−1/18 is greater than 53/60.
* On the arc side take x≥0 after the chosen sign cut, and enlarge δ to m=1/4. The inequalities force y,z>0: otherwise all three absolute coordinates would be at most m, contradicting unit length. Set k=|y−z|≤m and s=y+z=√(2−2x²−k²). For fixed k, x+2s is concave in x on [0,x_max]. At x=0 it is at least 2√(2−m²). At the endpoint x=(s−k)/2+m, put w=m−k/2. Then x+2s=(5√(3−2w²−3k²/2)−2w)/3, concave in k. At k=0 and k=m it exceeds 53/20 (the smaller is (5√(23/8)−1/2)/3). This proves (x+2y+2z)/3>53/60.
Hence two tails assigned to a common corner have cosine greater than 2(53/60)²−1>14/25. Each originally belongs to the parity slice of that corner, so its group still has Hamming distance ≥6 and the exact 160-word bound. These 372 gauges are capped by 1,280 dense words. Rank four/five were already capped solely by eligibility (≤56 cosets); in rank three gauges with ≤56 there is nothing to check. The only remaining closed-flow variable-radius branch is the precise eight-active-arc rank-three pattern in 180 normalized gauges. There is no claim of a feasible improvement in it. This is the next concrete candidate to try, rather than generic radius scanning.
