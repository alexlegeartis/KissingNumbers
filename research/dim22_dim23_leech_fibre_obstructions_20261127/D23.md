# Lower61, 23D: complementary norm-6 dodecad projection, exact whole-fibre obstruction

27 November 2026. No improvement in the 23D lower bound. Comparator: the 93,150 minimal vectors of laminated Λ23, the 23D section perpendicular to one norm-squared-4 Leech vector. See [Cohn's table](https://cohn.mit.edu/kissing-numbers/) and [Elkies' exact section counts, Nov 4](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~elkies/M272.19/nov04.pdf), which also give 75,900 for a norm-squared-6 normal. The live project README has no competing 23D result. This experiment is not the older projected three-coordinate tail with independently rotated Golay sign orbits; it changes both physical normal directions and uses a mixed norm-4/6 source.

## Scoped result and why the interesting fibres still fail

Fix complementary Golay dodecads D,D' (literal mask 0x006fdd and its complement in the 24 coordinates), and let u,v be 2 on their respective positions in coordinates scaled by 1/√8. Both lie in the Leech lattice and their Gram matrix is 6I. The normal lattice is exactly Zu+Zv: rounding both real coordinates of any alleged extra vector in this plane leaves a Leech vector of squared length ≤3, below the nonzero minimum 4.

Write a=x·u,b=x·v; then a,b are integers. Within a body fibre, distinct vectors in a given squared-radius shell share the same marks. Use the canonical residues a,b in {-3,-2,-1,0,1,2}. Any body of squared norm r²∈(0,4] has a representative of squared norm r²+(a²+b²)/6≤7, hence 4 or 6 (Leech is even, minimum 4). Thus the 40 fibres enumerated are the entire projected-lattice stock in this radial range. They occupy all 36 marks, 5 fibres use shell 6: 1,584 at (-3,-3), and 18,480 at each of (-3,±2),(±2,-3). The 35 other fibres have combined 195,456 body points. Adjacent integer translations of the source normal do not double-count identical body points. There are 270,960 eligible body points before constraints.

Each *complete* fibre can be used at an arbitrary constant real latitude, with arbitrary radial scaling to the unit sphere and with multiple copies when permitted. In all 40 fibres the sample pair witnessed in the certificate has true squared body-distance 4 and hence forces |h|≤√(24−R) for head coordinate h scaled by √6 and R=6r²: embed body p as (p,h/√6)/√((R+h²)/6). Since R≥11, each latitude sign has angular width <π/3; copying any one body ray in two latitudes of the same sign is impossible. Thus two signed copies exhaust the possible complete copies (one total for R=24). Head 0 can be assigned either sign consistently. When a composition uses two copies of a body include the constraint from two copies of the *same point* (unit body-direction dot 1), rather than only the within-fibre contact witness.

For each distinct pair of fibres, take **one** exact witnessed body dot U/12, potentially strictly below the true maximum. If U≤0 ignore this restriction completely in the upper-bound relaxation. If U>0 then the signed-latitude interval test is exact: (U/2+gh)/√((R+g²)(S+h²)) has its minimum over a rectangle at a corner. For fixed h its derivative in g has the sign of hR−gU/2, so its only possible stationary point is a maximum. Apply that in both variables. Choose rational endpoints whose covered magnitude extends outward to the least integer multiple of 1/64 above each real limit; three equally partitioned integer intervals per sign suffice. A pair of boxes is left available only if some rational corner passes ≤1/2. The decisive comparison with g=a/64,h=b/64 is U·64²+2ab≤0, or its square ≤(R·64²+a²)(S·64²+b²). No rounding down or floating cosines are used. Dropping all other contacts means every admissible true configuration injectively selects a clique, not conversely.

The resulting 235-vertex positive-weight graph has exact max clique weight **83,997** (394 DFS nodes). Optional tighter eight-box cover yields **79,641** (625 vertices; 3,834 DFS nodes). This is an upper bound only for arbitrary combinations of **whole** marked radial fibres with r²≤4 and one shared real head dimension, not for partial fibres, point-dependent latitudes, larger projected radii, other normal planes or arbitrary 23D codes. Pure extra poles can contribute at most 2, so the broader scoped bound including them is 83,999 (79,643 under the finer cover). The five norm-six banks total 75,504, so their raw inventory is not what fails. Distinct layers require mutually incompatible scalar latitudes even after weakening all their other mutual constraints.

## Replay

`d23-dodecad-fibre-upper.mjs` is standalone Node ≥22; append --tight for the second value. It checks both whole shell sizes (196,560 and 16,773,120) and their exact binomial mark census, data membership by Golay residues and coordinate-sum congruence, and 820 literal witness row pairs (two 24-nibble positive-offset representations, upper triangle). Multiplying true body coordinates by 3√8 makes integer entries 3x_i−a or 3x_i−b, and their dot divided by 6 is U. Color-class branch-and-bound partitions residual vertices into pairwise incompatible classes, then bounds by one maximum weight per class, exhaustively branching in descending weight. The embedded witness data are **not** claimed to give every pair's true maximizer, only valid obstructions. Both signed cover tasks use small integers (<2^53) and closed outward intervals.

Independent next route: a short norm-four mark orthogonal to a long norm-six mark, then retain the standard Λ23 section exactly before challenging it with extra classes. This avoids starting 9,151 below the target; the 4+6 covering radius squared 5/2 means only one shell-six corner mark can have r²≤4. Its viability requires a coordinated replacement rather than weaker tests of complete dodecad fibres. Another genuinely non-Leech alternative is structured fiber subset exchange in the quaternionic 12D 841 configuration, but prior 13D wing and fixed 12D tests should be checked before re-entering.

## Near-incumbent alternative: one short and one long normal

Take u supported on positions in mask 0x22 (two raw entries +4) and v on D (twelve raw entries +2). The supports are disjoint. Their Gram is diag(4,6); covering radius²=5/2<4 again makes the plane primitive. Use dot marks a mod 4,b mod 6 in {-2,-1,0,1}×{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2}. There are 25 full radial fibres with 0<r²≤4, with R=12r². Exactly one, (a,b)=(-2,-3), comes from norm six (R=42, population 9,072). The a=0 norm-four fibres in their original latitudes (the b=-3 class has two signed copies) recover the full 93,150 lattice section, not just the smaller 75,900 long-normal section.

The same rational-box method with 325 literal marked Leech contact pairs and twelve intervals per sign yields maximum clique weight 93,150 on 579 vertices in 207 search nodes. This is an exact **ceiling and attainment** for the 25 whole fibres, including duplicate latitudes and both pure poles. In the unique exceptional small fibre (-2,-3), R=18, shell 4, its two rays are antipodal and its angular semiwidth is exactly π/3; the program uses the true witnessed contact U=-36, giving |h|≤√54, and tests explicit same-sign different boxes rather than ruling them out. Each such box has width <√18, so cannot host two copies of the same body: angle ≤∆h/√18<1<π/3. For pole vertices, the supporting inequality on the same sign is 3h²≤R, so it is necessary that the endpoint of the box nearest zero passes it. The other 24 fibres have the ordinary √(48−R) head cap (witness U=2R−48, R≥27), and hence each sign width <π/3. Fixture and proof replay: `d23-short-long-fibre-upper.mjs`. In particular the corner fibre contains actual offending pairs against the 35,154 common-equator vectors, despite full within-fibre compatibility.

### Stronger partial/per-point no-gain for the new corner alone

Keep the 93,150 sites at their original positions, but allow arbitrary removals, arbitrary subsets of the 9,072 new projected body rays, arbitrary real head latitude for each selected point, and both pure poles. No replacements by this extra corner alone can improve the fixed baseline.

First, the tempting equator exchange really has one unique equatorial owner per candidate. For a fixed source norm-six x of mark (-2,-3), the norm-four mark (-2,-3) has exactly two z,z' and z+z'=-u-v. Integrality and absence of norm two give x·z,x·z'≤3, with sum 5, so the pair is {2,3}. For the mark-three choice z, x-z is a norm-four ray y of mark (0,0); conversely any equator conflict with x·y=3 has z=x-y in this tiny stock. No two distinct x can give the same y: a repeated owner via the other z' would give y·z=y·z'=-1 in contradiction to y·(z+z')=0. Translating x,z by v shows that positive and negative copies of the same projected body have the same equator owner. This alone permits a superficial 1↦2 story; old off-equator neighbours rule it out.

For a negative-latitude daughter write head coordinate −T/√6 before normalizing, T≥0, body radius squared 7/2 (its whole physical vector is not held to the radius of the old code). For the original negative corner x, count old y with x·y=3 at fixed mark (0,b). Equivalently enumerate z=x-y among norm-four sources at (-2,-3-b) with x·z=3. There are 30 such old contacts per candidate at b=-1, and 12 per candidate at b=-3. In the former signed mark, all non-isolated old rays have degree exactly 12 (22,680 such rows), verified on every old/new incidence by independent integer differences. The positive sign case is obtained by negation: x+v is the corresponding positive parent, and -x-v-u is another canonical negative parent with opposite body, with unchanged contact counts against negated old stock.

If T<√63 (latitude <60°), every one of the 30 b=-1 walls is forbidden: (5/2 + T/6)² − (7/2 + T²/6)>0 by concavity and its two positive endpoints at 0 and √63. There is at most one such daughter per body and sign since their angular separation would be <60°. Counting incidences forces at least 30/12=5/2 times as many old b=-1 deletions as selected low negative daughters; same for the positive sign, in the disjoint b=+1 layer. If T≥√63, every one of the 12 b=-3 walls is forbidden since (3/2+T/2)²−(7/2+T²/6)=−5/4+3T/2+T²/12>0. At most two rays per sign can have latitude at least 60°: the least available mutual dot is −cos(θ₁+θ₂), with equality at 1/2 only for two exactly 60° latitudes and antipodal directions. A pure pole uses the same high sector and would forbid all 324 b=-3 rays. Thus the high sectors lose at least 12 old points for at most 2 new in each occupied sign. The four old blocker stocks are disjoint, proving strict loss for any nonempty extra corner/pole augmentation of a fixed retained subset.

Fixture-free replay: `d23-short-long-corner-expansion.mjs`. It regenerates all necessary norm-four and norm-six rows (including expected Golay point census), confirms the two stock lengths 162 and 2,004, verifies every explicit dot-three incidence and the old degree distribution, and checks the two equator owner vectors sum to -u-v. This does not constrain other partial fibres, simultaneously coordinated motion of old rays, other normal planes, or unrestricted 23D codes.
