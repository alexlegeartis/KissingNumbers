## Geometric obstruction, valid for arbitrary real profiles

Consider a full length-64 DG sign bank in one fixed mother cubic of RM80. At two marked body sites p,q it multiplies the owner's signs by any fixed real profile (x,y), leaving the other 62 magnitudes one, with no tail. The same full sign stock is used in every profile. Its squared norm is N=62+x²+y².

First $N\le96$ is necessary, not an imposed cut: the recorded DG minimum distance 24 is attained by a difference of two distinct quadratic choices plus an affine word. Any coordinate-translation of this binary difference preserves its quadratic part and changes only its affine part, so all translations also occur as DG differences, since all affine words are allowed. The fraction of translations hitting at least one of the two fixed sites is at most 48/64, so a distance-24 difference avoiding both marks exists. Its raw dot within the single profile is N−48, hence N≤96.

Two distinct profiles on the same owner have raw dot $62+u_i\cdot u_j$, where $u_i=(x_i,y_i)$. Thus $u_i\cdot u_j\le48-62=-14$; also $\lVert u_i\rVert^2\le34$. Squaring the sum of m profile vectors gives $0\le34m-14m(m-1)$, so m ≤3. If m=3 then $\lVert u_j+u_k\rVert^2\le40$ and $u_i\cdot(u_j+u_k)\le-28$, giving $r_i=\lVert u_i\rVert\ge28/\sqrt{40}$. For any r in [28/√40,√34], the inequality $14+r>2\sqrt{62+r^2}$ follows from $3r^2-28r+52<0$ (the latter roots are (28±4√10)/6). Consequently if a 16-site sparse sign pattern includes both marks and matches the profile's signs on its support, the raw dot $14+|x_i|+|y_i|$ exceeds the admissible $2\sqrt N$. Every such equality of signs is a genuine angular conflict.

The three profiles use both mark-sign parities: each parity occupies just two opposite quadrants, and two vectors in the same quadrant have nonnegative dot, contrary to the −14 bound. If a profile has a zero coordinate one may assign its sign either way for an exact match; its dot is unchanged. Thus for any sparse sign word of either parity, one of the profiles matches the target parity.

## Exact projection census

At a support B of 16 positions, let π_B restrict the DG word's signs. The chosen mother cubic is even on every original accepted sparse support B and projection of the complete DG bank may itself equal all even 16-bit words. In that case adding the fixed cubic just permutes those words. Multiplying the two marked signs by a chosen geometric profile flips its sign parity as above. Every sign pattern (even or odd) of any sparse bank on B then matches exactly some full-profile donor and is excluded. This is independent of the sign bank's phase or choice of extended-Hamming label, not just a fixed guard obstruction.

The predecessor already proved that each of these original supports is present with 2,048 signs in the ancestor stock. To check a projection use a reduced binary basis of the restrictions of all affine functions on B, then reduce all 1,024 projected quadratic choices to affine-quotient representatives. Exactly $\#\text{representatives}\cdot2^{\text{affine rank}}$ distinct sign restrictions result. Only supports with full affine rank 7 are needed; among 35,392 such B, the histogram of cardinalities is 24,576: 64; 28,672: 2,176; 30,720: 1,792; **32,768: 31,360**. These 31,360 supports are a certified subset, not a claimed exhaustive list over all affine ranks. All 16-bit restricted full words are even because B is an old dual support; the replay independently checks that their restricted affine and quadratic words are even (via dot with all monomials of degree at most two), and each cubic gauge is even as well. The projection then equals the entire even half-space. Counting these good B through each of the 2,016 unordered pairs gives minimum **1,792** and maximum 1,944.

## Count consequence

Suppose m disjoint marked pairs on the length-64 body are upgraded to 3 full profiles each, rather than the existing two. Each attacks at least 1,792 fixed scaffold banks, as these supports contain the two marks. A size-16 support can contain both marks from at most 8 disjoint pairs, so at least $1792m/8=224m$ distinct sparse banks (at 2,048 points each) must be completely retired even if arbitrary phases are allowed. Gain from third profiles is only $131072m$; total loss is at least $327680m$, before any four-site deletions. For one group the stronger compulsory loss is 1,792 banks: $131072-1792\cdot2048=-3538944$. Altering the support alphabet, changing some full DG profiles to thin/owner-dependent banks, using more than two marked body sites, and adding a rail in 65D are not constrained by this statement. The successful separate 65D construction explicitly uses a rail to reduce the previously fatal amplitude sum on sparse supports.
