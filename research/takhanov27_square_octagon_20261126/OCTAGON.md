Thus after an isometry the endpoints are 8(e_i+e_{i+1}) on an orthonormal eight-frame with indices mod 8. For every even i and odd j, the cell r_{ij}=8(e_i+e_j) is in the Leech lattice, by an alternating integer sum of the consecutive cycle roots along an odd-length path. Its norm squared is 128, so it is a true Leech minimal vector. The checker reconstructs and looks up all 16 cells of each carrier in the original sorted Leech shell; these form a product of two four-site simplices and are not hypothetical pool labels.

Here is a positive abstract exchange construction. Choose any four-by-four binary matrix with row/column sums two. In each row, join its two zero-bit and its two one-bit cells horizontally; similarly in each column vertically. There are sixteen new midpoint bodies in total: eight horizontal bodies of the form 8e_i+4(e_j+e_k) and eight vertical ones with the roles reversed. Give each horizontal pair both ± choices of one of the two perpendicular native D3 lines in a chosen coordinate plane, according to its bit, and each vertical pair both signs of the opposite bit's line. Then all 32 spherical points are mutually valid. For two horizontal bodies in the same row, their column pairs are disjoint, so body dot 64 and tail lines perpendicular; in distinct rows the body dot is at most 32, and similarly for vertical bodies. Between horizontal and vertical, the body dot is 32 times the number of shared row/column incidences (zero, one, or two); if it reaches 64, the lines are perpendicular by the matrix rule at their shared cell. Same-body opposite tails have dot 96−32=64. Thus as a local trade the complete network adds 32 against 16 equatorial cells, notionally +8 beyond the native eight-cycle mechanism. This is a genuine algebraic mechanism, not a valid improvement before exterior verification.
