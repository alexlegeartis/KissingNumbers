### Theorem: no sparse or sign-agnostic augmentation, even allowing arbitrary real amplitudes
Keep all 3,120 roots and one *full* parity class (128 signs) on every one of the 4,267 Smart octads; the parity choice can be independent on each support. A further vector scaled to \(\sum x_i^2=8\), with normalized inner products at most \(1/2\) against those old stocks, must have at least **12 nonzero coordinates** and \(\sum_i|x_i|>957/100\). Both necessary statements hold even before imposing a single dense-layer wall. In particular all directions which would automatically coexist with every possible flat dense sign by the \(\ell_1\) bound \(\sum_i|x_i|\le\sqrt{80}\) are excluded, because \(\sqrt{80}<9.57\). This is not a theorem that blocked signs must be removed, or a global 40D upper bound.

Sort magnitudes \(a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_{40}\ge0\). The root stock gives \(a_1+a_2\le2\). If \(F\) is any 5-set inside an old octad, then \(\sum_{i\in F}|x_i|\le4\); indeed maximum over either full parity class on that octad is either the total magnitude or total minus twice the *smallest* of its eight magnitudes, both at least the sum of its six largest magnitudes. Thus this sign-independent deduction also applies when the entire old support lies in the donor and its signs have the other parity; a zero coordinate only helps the parity correction.

Call a set of coordinates *uncovered* if none of its 5-subsets belongs to a Smart octad. A five-subset belongs to at most one octad by \(d\ge8\). Exact inventory from the pinned primary support file: there are 419,056 uncovered 5-sets, 306,005 uncovered 6-sets, 714 uncovered 7-sets, and no uncovered 8-set. For each of the \(714\cdot33=23,562\) pairs (uncovered 7-set \(T\), eighth site \(j\notin T\)), the 4-subsets \(K\subset T\) such that \(K\cup\{j\}\) is covered have common intersection of size at most two. Histogram of common intersection size 0,1,2: (22,074, 1,416, 72). Consequently, for every choice of four top-ranked sites in \(T\), some covered \(K\cup\{j\}\) uses at least two of the top four: otherwise each four-set \(K\) contains all three sites in the complement of those top four.

If the top seven have any covered quintuple, they imply the order-statistic wall \(\sum_{r=3}^7a_r\le4\). Otherwise the rank of the two required top-four sites and the other three ordered members of the covered quintuple gives \(a_3+a_4+a_6+a_7+a_8\le4\). Ties in magnitudes are immaterial, use any deterministic ordering, including zeros if necessary. Now put \(b_i=a_i-a_{i+1}\ge0\). Both displayed tests and root pressure are nonnegative linear forms in \(b\). Add the \(\ell_1\le9.57\) wall only when proving that threshold. At any extreme point there can be at most two (respectively three) positive jumps, since a nonzero feasible perturbation in the null space of the active linear equations would otherwise remain in the orthant and contradict extremality. The positive convex objective \(\sum_i a_i^2\) attains a maximum at a vertex of this compact polytope. Enumerate the jump positions, active rows and all rational coefficients by Cramer's rule, checking every constraint. For support at most 11, both cases have maximum **196/25**. For arbitrary support with \(\ell_1\le957/100\), the two maximum squared norms are **79,769/10,000** and **239,747/30,000**. The latter is approximately 7.9915667, strictly below 8. These are maxima of the two explicitly relaxed order-statistic polytopes, not tight radii of the whole source.

### Theorem: true five-byte half-support fibres have no real tail refill
Split the 40 coordinates into five aligned blocks of eight according to the hexadecimal bytes of Smart's source. Choose any one block and any four sites in it, with a fixed signing on those four sites and zero on the other four within its block. At raw normalization 8 the remaining tail has squared norm 4 in 32 dimensions; inside a common-head fibre it must be a nonpositive code. All old octad supports with exactly those four sites in this block have *pairwise disjoint* remaining tetrads, since otherwise they meet on more than four sites. For fixed head signs, the eight tail signings of either parity on one such tetrad are an antipodal orthogonal frame and span those four coordinates. There are exactly 336 fibres of support occupancy eight and 14 of occupancy seven before multiplying by the \(2^4\) fixed head signings: the true incidence deficit is 1,792 signed rays, not the rough 3,840 that ignores the 16 irregular (4,2,1,1) supports.

For full fibres, all tails are constrained to be perpendicular to a basis, hence no nonzero tail can be added. In the 14 other cases only four physical tail coordinates remain after those perpendicularity constraints. Each is jointly covered with at least three chosen head sites in an actual old octad. The sign-independent wall above (also valid for partial sums of four) forces its magnitude to be at most one. Norm squared four in those four coordinates forces all four magnitudes to be one. The putative eight-support is then excluded by no uncovered 8-set. This is a continuous exact no-refill result inside the stated *fixed* head; it does not bound moves of the head amplitudes or locations or deleting occupied fibres.

### Two-owner flat trades and arbitrary sign recoding
For a prospective new eight-support its owner set consists exactly of the old octads that contain some quintuple of it. If it keeps a complete stock on any such owner, no signing of the new support is legal. Traversing ordered-coordinate subsets, keeping at most two distinct owners, is complete because this set grows monotonically as coordinates are inserted. There are 16 genuinely new supports with one owner, each with a different owner, and 648 with two owners in 252 distinct owner pairs. In every two-owner pool the candidate supports pairwise intersect at least five. This proves that deleting at most two old supports and replacing by *complete* parity classes cannot improve; if there is no two-owner candidate, each one-owner support is assigned to its distinct old owner. The result permits arbitrary parity changes on all retained complete supports. All claims are with no third affected support.

For arbitrary partial sign sets and rephasing on the affected supports, use a stronger 5-face charging lemma. A fixed signed 5-face has body norm squared 5; all extensions on norm 8 and cutoff 4 have tail norm 3 and pairwise tail dot at most \(-1\). The squared norm of their sum is nonnegative, so at most four extensions exist, even in arbitrary tail dimension. Sum over the \(2^5\) head signs for an unsigned 5-face: at most 128 points sharing that face, no matter how many signed supports are used. This closes all one-owner cases. In 244 out of the 252 two-owner pools one can choose a fixed 5-face inside each old parent so that every new support contains one of the two faces. Charge new vectors to one face, and charge each retained point from the two old parents to its own face. Both capacities are 128 before any additional division, proving no net gain even allowing arbitrary partial sign sets on those supports. The eight exceptions are zero-index source pairs (1242,3200), (1243,3202), (1244,3203), (1245,3199), (1246,3201), (1247,3198), (1248,4180), (1249,4179); these eight are **not** claimed closed for unrestricted signed recoding. The simple finite scouting found no witness. Distinct flat support shapes, nonflat weighted recoding of affected owners, and coupled removal/rebuild of more than two owners are not excluded.

**Useful exact structure for the eight exceptions.** In each, all candidates and both old owners share a 3-face. For fixed signing on it, the residual problem has squared norm 5, dot cutoff 1, and the new genuinely two-owner supports use five of only eight active sites. Two such supports share at least two sites; each signed vector occupies eight active sign orthants and no two compatible ones can share one. Thus at most 32 residual points using only those genuinely two-owner supports. In six of the exceptional pools each old parent uses four active sites and one outside; each reserves 16 orthants, and both can coexist at one orthant. Any truly two-owner donor conflicts with either old class at a common orthant. Accordingly even an arbitrary mixture of the genuine dual-owner additions and old parents has size at most 32 per common 3-face signing: count each old parent with half weight per orthant and each new one with full weight, both then use total weight eight. This sub-argument omits the one extra singleton-owner support occurring in each exception. The last two exceptional pairs need another weighting because one parent uses five active sites; neither the raw orthant count nor the greedy tie constitutes a general signed optimum.

### Positively motivated next step
Do not run more uniform small-support searches. The result forces either a real interaction with the dense QR restriction or an actual multi-layer payout. One workable exact mechanism would keep the Smart and root stocks and exploit a *dual QR dodecad* on the new support. Permute the dense QR bank coordinates so a projected 12-position dodecad disjoint from the eight marks is the donor's support. The QR code is self-dual, so every dense bank restriction to this support has even parity. A donor of odd sign parity thus never exactly matches it, and a sufficient dense gate is \(\|x\|_1-2\min_{x_i\ne0}|x_i|\le\sqrt{80}\). Require also the actual octad pressure (full signed support functions), \(a_1+a_2\le2\), norm 8, and the explicit forty central daughters. For the latter, with \(a=\sqrt{41}-2\), a proposed antipodal pair is compatible precisely if \(\left|\sum_jx_j-(a+1)x_i\right|\le2(a+1)\) for all \(i\). Both signs of a 12-supported donor have odd parity together; the pair would contribute +2 if valid. This is an explicit constructive problem with radius/orthant control, **not a found witness**. For a larger gain, seek many such supports simultaneously sharing a QR dodecad and a sparse-wall parallel atlas, or build coupled weighted trades over three or more octad owners.
