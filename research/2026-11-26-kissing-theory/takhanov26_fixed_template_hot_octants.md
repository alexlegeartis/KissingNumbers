# Continuous obstruction for a fixed old template with two capped hot coordinates

This note concerns Takhanov v0.2 at 201,212 and one of its 26-row original chamber templates, not the unrestricted kissing number. It excludes every four-donor family of the following type which tries to keep at least 24 original template rows. It also applies after any signed permutation of the original template. Retained points are not moved.

After selecting two hot coordinates (one regular, one original tail) and fixing their signs, each new donor is a unit vector with hot magnitudes both exactly 1/2, three free signed regular components r and one positive alternate-tail magnitude h with fixed sign. Keep the one-paid-mixed-tetrad condition `|r_i|+h <= 1/2` for the other three regular coordinates. Then `||r||²+h²=1/2` and `1/2 <= 3(1/2-h)²+h²`. The last quadratic is below 1/2 throughout h in [1/10,1/2] by convexity and its endpoint values. Thus h<1/10 and `||z||²>49/25` for z=2r. Every regular coordinate is capped by 1 in z units.

Write each template row as t/d in the pinned integer table. For fixed signed hot coordinates u,v with signs s_u,s_v, keeping this row is exactly the linear constraint
`sum_(i not in {u,v}) t_i z_i <= d - s_u*t_u - s_v*t_v`.
Fix any zero, one or two paid rows and a closed regular sign octant. In that octant the retained inequalities and coordinate caps define a compact polytope. Its squared norm attains a maximum at a vertex (unless empty). Every vertex lies at three independent active planes among the retained row planes, the six cap planes and the three regular sign planes. Enumerate those triples from the full arrangement once per hot choice, and keep only cube vertices with squared norm at least 49/25. Each vertex records its actually violated template rows, so for any chosen deletions it is eligible exactly if its violations are a subset of those deletions. Assign it to every closed octant whose signs agree, allowing both signs at a zero. In particular the computation is a proved relaxation bounding the whole continuous eligible set; it is not a rational-amplitude grid.

The exact integer determinant replay finds a maximum of **three** possible regular octants over all 40 signed hot choices and all 352 deletion subsets of size at most two. For 37 hot choices the maximum is two, and for the other three it is three. Distinct donors in the same regular sign octant cannot coexist: their hot contribution is already 1/2 and the common signed alternate-tail contribution is strictly positive. Hence any such exchange keeping 24 original rows adds at most three donors. A four-donor exchange in this family must instead move at least one of the old retained rows, pay more, or change at least one hot magnitude (or use a different skeleton condition). This does not rule out building a new chamber from scratch, unequal-hot donors, or other mechanisms for sharing skeleton costs.
