# 26D two-chamber route: an unconditional 23-point limit for the 2513 chamber

This closes precisely the conditional 24-point chamber construction from the two-chamber reduction at [the earlier reduction](takhanov26_two_chamber.md). It does not claim Takhanov's 201,212 cannot be beaten. Let a feasible chamber row be (u,r1,r2,r3,v) of norm 1 with each magnitude at most 1/2. Its quartet constraints are 25u+23v+21 Ψ(r) <= M=sqrt(2513)/2, where Ψ is maximum over the four even signings. Write c=1/(2 sqrt(2)). For an even regular sign word Ψ is the sum of regular magnitudes; for an odd word Ψ is sum minus twice the minimum, and hence at least max abs(r_i). Use a fixed arbitrary assignment of signs to zero coordinates.

## Directed adjacent-orthant rule
A feasible code has at most one point per sign orthant: for capped nonnegative a,b, ab >= (a+b)/2-1/4, and each row's magnitudes sum to >=2. This gives dot at least 3/4 in a shared sign orthant.

If an adjacent occupied orthant differs only on position i, then the magnitude a_i=d at that position must be at least c. To minimize the dot to any point in that adjacent orthant, first take that point's nonmatching magnitude b_i up to 1/2 and reduce the other nonnegative magnitudes to keep norm, which can only improve the dot. At remaining squared energy 3/4, the minimum of the positively weighted sum is at an extreme vertex in squared magnitudes, i.e. three 1/2 and one zero, because square root is concave. Consequently opposite-sign dot is at least half of the sum of the three smallest a_j with j != i, minus d/2. The sum of those three is at least 1+sqrt(1/4-d^2): the fourth contributes squared magnitude at most 1/4 and the squared mass of the smaller three, at least 3/4-d^2, is minimized in L1 by two capped and one partial. At d<c this lower bound for dot is >1/2.

## Occupancy at equality in the previous 24 bound
All four even (++ on u,v) orthants are forbidden: the signed score is >=21 times the sum of all magnitudes >=42. In each of the four odd regular sign words at least one of the three tail choices {++, +-, -+} is forbidden. In fact if ++ is present, at least one of u,v must be <1/4. Otherwise, using Ψ >= sqrt((1-u^2-v^2)/3), the score lower bound 25u+23v+21 sqrt((1-u^2-v^2)/3) is increasing in both arguments on [1/4,1/2]^2, and u^2+v^2>=1/4. Its minimum on the arc is >=23(1+sqrt(3))/4+21/2 > M. The <1/4 magnitude forces the corresponding adjacent mixed-tail sign choice empty. Thus for cardinality 24, each even regular sign word has all three other tail choices, and each odd regular sign word has -- and exactly two of {++, +-, -+}.

## The forbidden two-point overlap
Pick any even (+,-) row Y from an assumed 24-point code. Denote its magnitudes by U,a1,a2,a3,V. It has U>=c by adjacency to its same-regular-word (--). Some regular coordinate j must have magnitude a=a_j<c. Otherwise raise V to 1/2, reducing regular entries while keeping U fixed and staying above c (the required energy .75-U^2 is >= .5 > 3c^2). This reduces the score, and the remaining body L1 is at least .5+c+sqrt(.375-U^2) by concavity in the squared variables, with each between c^2 and 1/4. The score lower bound 25U-11.5+21(.5+c+sqrt(.375-U^2)) is concave on [c,.5], with endpoint values 9.5+46c and 11.5+42c, both > M.

In fact this selected a<1/5. Fix a and raise V to 1/2, reducing U and the other two regular entries, preserving norm and decreasing the score; in this lower bound do not impose the lower limits from adjacency. The weighted sum on the other three coordinates (weights 25,21,21) has squared energy .75-a^2, upper bounds .25. By concavity the extreme profile has two magnitudes .5 and the remaining sqrt(.25-a^2), optimally assigned to weight 25. Thus Y requires 9.5+21a+25 sqrt(.25-a^2) <= M. On [1/5,c] the lower function is concave with both endpoints > M, proving a<1/5.

Flip Y's j-th regular sign to the adjacent odd sign word. Its (+,-) orthant is absent by the directed rule. Therefore the same odd sign word must contain both (++), called X, and (-,+), by 24-point equality. For X, its positive u>=c by adjacency to (-,+). The prior equality rule therefore forces v<1/4. Also u>=sqrt(.25-v^2). The score bound 25u+23v+21 sqrt((1-u^2-v^2)/3) increases in u up to 1/2, so is at least 25 sqrt(.25-v^2)+23v+10.5. This latter function increases for v in [0,1/4], and at v=1/9 already exceeds M. Hence X has v<1/9. Unit norm and the caps imply all other four X magnitudes exceed sqrt(77)/18 and all other four Y magnitudes exceed sqrt(21)/10. Between X and Y the signs differ on precisely j and v, with three matching large coordinates. Their dot is strictly greater than
3*(sqrt(77)/18)*(sqrt(21)/10) - (1/5+1/9)/2 > 1/2,
contradiction.

Thus the 24-point chamber is impossible even with arbitrary real amplitudes and no symmetry of the code. Both chambers <=23, making the stated eight-donor/three-paid-skeleton scheme at most 201211 if all other components stay fixed. This bound is for the 2513 clipping specifically; another donor shape or new types of shared skeleton payments can escape it. All reasoning analytic; no reliance on the discrete search. A bounded search on the old T amplitude orbits and normalized integer amplitudes 0...10 was only diagnostic: 14 of 15 sign patterns fail already for adjacent variables on that finite bank; the last fails for an incompatible Hamming-two pair.
