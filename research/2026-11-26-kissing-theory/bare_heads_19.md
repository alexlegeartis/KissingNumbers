# A real-tail obstruction for unweighted Golay heads in 19D


Keep the support, tail-orientation, root geometry and block sizes of [Li’s 10,668-point 19D section](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00248v1), but allow independent sign patterns in all full-size octad blocks, as justified below. These phase switches preserve base size and base-base legality: two different octads intersect in at most 4; opposite orientations of one mixed octad contribute -2 in the tail and at most 6 in the head, and one fixed orientation uses a single parity class of length 6. Let additional vectors have one of the 2048 UNWEIGHTED projected Golay heads (entries ±1) and ANY real tail in R^3, with arbitrary length and direction, and no requirement of being a projected tail. Such a family adds at most 1792, even allowing arbitrary selections and multiplicities. The resulting total cannot exceed 12460. Li's *actual* 1792 do not consist entirely of bare heads, since he lowers one entry of every axis head; this theorem does not prove optimality of his or all 19D constructions.

Write the four T-coordinates in Li's order. A head syndrome c is the tail of a Golay preimage mod the complement word 1111, choosing representative with last bit zero. The eight syndromes each have 256 different heads. On a mixed octad meeting T in pair {i,j}, the parity of a head restricted to its six entries is the cut bit c_i⊕c_j (Golay orthogonality). The two orientations of that block have tail v and -v with v=(col_i(R)−col_j(R))/2, where R is Li's 3-by-4 Hadamard sign matrix. The six pairs yield all 12 signed two-coordinate directions. If chosen body parity equals the cut bit then a body head can fully align to give dot 6; otherwise max 4. Retaining just one representative octad per pair suffices as an outer relaxation, so at most 4096 parity patterns (two bits per pair) govern this proof, even if actual choices differ per octad. On each full-T octad, the head tetrad has parity wt(c) mod 2: its union with T is an octad in the dual; body head parity is odd. Thus its max head dot is 4 for odd c and 2 otherwise; all six ±2-axis tails occur. Any disjoint octad allows head dot 6 and each tail root tests 2v. For any t put r=||t|| and D=√(32+2r²) (half norm product with a body point). Necessarily r>=√2 and r<=4 (the latter because m=max(|t_i|+|t_j|)>=r and 2m<=D). Simpler corollary in the original mixed-even body: an omitted zero-syndrome head with unit amplitudes cannot be saved by *any* tail, since 6+m<=D implies r>=6+√40>12, conflicting with r<=4.

No multiplicities on one head: sorted abs tail u>=v>=w always obey 4+u+v<=D from the mixed blocks and 2+2u<=D from full-T blocks. With a=(D−2)/2, b=D−4, 6<=D<=8, maximizing convex u²+2v² over u<=a, u+v<=b, 0<=v<=u gives max{3b²/4,a²+2(b−a)²}. Since r²=D²/2−16, substitution forces D<=max(12−√32,14−√56)=14−√56<√(128/3), hence r²<16/3. Two variants of one head have dot≥16−rr'>32/3, while their half product of norms is <32/3.

Each syndrome has four weight-4 same-syndrome differences (the four non-T tetrads). Even any *one* of them gives a perfect matching on its 256 heads. If there is no legal pair of real tails at head distance 4 in a syndrome, at most 128 of its heads may be retained. A singleton tail impossible gives 0. Otherwise the trivial upper bound is 256. For a bare-head d=4 pair, dot=8+t·t', half product=√((16+||t||²)(16+||t'||²))/2. The integer box calculation simultaneously upper-bounds all three capacities after relaxing to one block per orientation and checking all 2^18 parity patterns. Highest sum is 14 half-classes = 1,792. Inter-syndrome constraints are not used at all.


## Arbitrary full sign-block patterns


The relaxation can include arbitrary parity choices in the four octads containing T as well. For each of their six oriented axial tails ±2f_i keep just one representative block. If the tetrad head parity wt(c) mod 2 matches its selected parity, its max head dot is 4; otherwise 2. The unsigned lower bound 2+2u used in the no-multiplicity proof is always valid. Disjoint octads, regardless of their parity, have max head dot at least 6, which is all the outer certificate uses. Thus allow any signs on all the octad blocks as long as their geometric supports/tail orientations and full body cardinality are retained. Indeed the allowed h-bit heads with one fixed octad and orientation must be independent in Q_h since two at Hamming distance 1 are too close; their full block size is 2^(h−1) for h=8,6,4 respectively. A connected regular bipartite graph of this form has only its two bipartition sides as maximum independent sets: a maximum independent set touches all edges, so its complement is independent as well. Switching parity is therefore the most general same-size sign redesign here. Base-root geometry remains fixed. If a body with a different set of supports or fewer points is used, the statement need not apply.

The attached general replay adds six bits for oriented full-T tails (a cut requirement becomes a function of wt(c) mod 2). The all-real upper bound still maxes at 14: among all 262144 masks the half-class-sum histogram is {2:512,4:9728,5:12288,6:70656,7:35328,8:99240,9:24048,10:8664,11:800,12:600,13:240,14:40}. This supersedes restrictions on other body signs stated earlier. No new numerical lower bound.


## Replay

Run `node verify_bare19.js` (standard library); `node verify_bare19.js 16` gives the independent finer outer grid. Each cube has radius 1/q and centres spaced 2/q. The code uses the upper norm from the componentwise extrema and a lower bound on the dot, so it only removes a cube if ALL its points violate a necessary condition. For a pair of cube centres k,l, the scaled dot lower bound is 8q²+k·l−||k||₁−||l||₁−3. A retained compatible box or pair can be a false positive, never a false negative. Therefore an upper bound on this finite relaxation is a bound on arbitrary real tails. This is not an optimization on grid points and does not assert that 1,792 bare-head points are geometrically achievable.
