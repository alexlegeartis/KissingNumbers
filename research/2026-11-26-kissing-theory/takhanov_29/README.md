# Two exact constraints on improving Takhanov's 29D construction

No new lower bound. The authentic comparison is R. Takhanov's [v0.2 source construction](https://github.com/k-nic/KissingNumbers/tree/77f0860bf93a745140e6e25481d3f60773b11441/version2) at **232,230**. The dated repository stock used below has 232,157. The theorems concern explicitly different scopes: one a discrete union of three pinned stocks, one a continuous real tail over a finite root-system orbit.

## A win from the simultaneous three-stock union needs at least 36 exceptional donors

The three real stocks are Takhanov v0.2 at 232,230, the dated repository stock at 232,157, and Takhanov principal v1 at 232,153. The replay constructs all true conflicts directly on their normalized integer coordinates and checks exact hashes. Norm squared is 128; a strict blocker has integer dot >64. Integer dot evaluation by float32 BLAS is exact because Cauchy–Schwarz bounds every partial sum of absolute terms by 128.

Prune the donor–stock conflict graph by alternating reachability from the unmatched donors of a maximum matching. Outside donors have disjoint assigned blockers outside the reachable neighborhood; dropping those donors and restoring these outside blockers cannot lower the gain, even when actual donor–donor conflicts are imposed. The resulting kernel has 62,907 donors and 60,306 incumbent columns. Its 188 expensive donors have degree 65–68; all are present in both alternative stocks.

Consider the giant component of the cheap-donor graph (degrees at most 64) with conflict edges to incumbent rows and to other donors. The verified cheap partition is into singleton rows and pairs that conflict. A pair may be assigned a blocker only if both its rows hit that blocker. The 2544 pairs are supplied as a compact certificate; all such edges are read back from real codes. The resulting groups admit a full matching to incumbent rows. The other cheap components independently have no possible net gain; ignore any further restrictions imposed by expensive donors on them for this upper bound.

For any required expensive donor h, let T be its incumbent blockers in the giant component. Find a matching of the cheap groups outside T with d unmatched groups. A selection involving h must already pay all |T| old rows. Every selected cheap row from a matched group pays an additional, distinct row outside T; the unmatched groups can contribute at most d rows. Thus cheap selected rows plus retained old rows number at most |B|−(|T|−d). The true graph gives |T|−d = 35 for every one of the 188 expensive rows. Adding a set H of expensive rows can contribute at most |H|, so |H|≥36 is necessary for a strict gain. This is not a certificate excluding larger H. The union of all 4534 old rows hit by the expensive donors has the same rank 35 on this particular partition. Thus prices are not additive. The previously verified scoped interval [232230,232307] for this union is not improved numerically here.

## The exceptional norm-(80+48) head cannot split into two real-tail sites

Remove the unique row of this head/tail type and retain the other 232,229 rows. The head occupies an octad, with the complementary 16 coordinates zero. Enlarge its W(E8) orbit to both D8 sign parities and every sign-permutation of the resulting magnitude profiles. Apply each half-root reflection x↦x−s(s·x)/4 for an even sign vector s, closing the D8 quotient; together with D8 these reflections generate W(E8). There are 12 classes before forgetting parity, 11 magnitude profiles, and 401,184 different heads in the enlarged bank.

Every true Leech projection of squared norm ≤51 is automatically safe because √(80·51)<64. The remaining 1448 projections leave 3528 candidates. For fixed head h the retained added rows give 3441 projected (head,tail) pairs and produce exact linear upper slabs on a proposed real tail s. Of the 3527 distinct alternatives to the original head, 3435 violate two opposite slabs and the remaining 92 have squared norm strictly below 48 from their slab intervals on one of 96 explicitly checked orthogonal bases. This closes arbitrary real tails, not just an integer grid.

For the original head, opposite slabs give s₂=s₅=0. Five actual inequalities then give s₁≥−4, s₃≤4, s₃−s₁≥4 and −4≤s₄≤4. The result is a bounded triangular prism and all six vertices satisfy every other slab, so this is the full allowed polytope. Exactly (−4,0,4,±4,0) attain squared norm 48, and no nonvertex does by strict convexity. These two tails have dot 16, whereas the common head requires a compatible pair of tails to have dot ≤−16. Thus at most one site of the whole orbit can replace the removed point, and either of the two old-head tails attains one.
