# Lower55: two genuinely different 17D apertures, 26 November 2026

**Source and benchmark.** Cohn–Li, [Improved kissing numbers in seventeen through twenty-one dimensions, v3 §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04916v3#S3), owns 5,730 in dimension 17. The maintained 18D best is 8,358 on the project and 19D external best is Kevin Li's 12,460; no 18D or 19D result is claimed in this round. Recent 17D fixed-pole and low-overlap saturation was used as a stop rule, not rerun. The target here is the first verified 5,731, even if the gain is one.

We use squared norm 8, pair dot threshold 4. Place 16 head coordinates on F₂⁴. Its 30 affine hyperplanes are equatorial octads. The usual equator consists of 480 signed D16 roots (±2,±2) plus 128 odd signings on each octad. For a bent coset of RM(1,4) inside RM(2,4), its 16 weight-six supports make a 2-(16,6,2) biplane. On each choose one complete odd bank of size 32 at tail +√2. A 192-word dense code consisting of six complete affine cosets contributes (2/3)·(±1)^16 at tail √8/3, with each coset difference bent and each dense word of even parity on every chosen sextad. The other side is a signed translate with tail signs reversed, and both poles stay. This constructive variant at 5,730 has been exactly checked, not declared a record. The geometry checker does not need the published data file: it reproduces the coordinates from these finite-field definitions and checks all 16,413,585 pairs.

## Independent first escape: remove the positive pole and use a fifth-coordinate shelf

At tail √3, take five head entries ±1. These vectors are admissible against the equator iff the five positions form an affine basis of F₂⁴: otherwise an octad contains all five and can match all signs (repair its parity on an unused position), whereas distinct affine bases meet any octad in ≤4. There are 2,688 supports, 86,016 signed candidates, each at the required norm. All are across-side safe with the intact negative half of the actual two-sided seed. Two selected shelf rows need mutual head dot≤1. The positive pole must leave: its dot to them is √24>4.

This entire direct shelf exchange is rigorously unprofitable if the only added vectors are these new five-coordinate rows and all other retained rows come from the stated old 5,730 stock:

- Against the old 192 dense upper rows, exactly matching the signs on the five positions causes an illegal dot and one mismatch suffices. Each high row meets exactly six such blockers, since each full 32-word affine coset restricts bijectively to five bits on an affine basis. Each old dense blocker can be charged by at most seven high rays in a mutually compatible collection: their supports intersect at most one at a time because their old signs agree on each intersection. For b such five-sets, coordinate-degree squares give 25b²/16−5b ≤ b(b−1), hence b≤7.
- Against a whole 32-row sparse bank overlapping in k, the threshold is head dot >4−√6, i.e. integer dot≥2. Its blocker contributions at k=0,1,2,3,4,5 are 0,0,8,4,10,6. This uses uniform sign projection onto any proper subset of the six support. Since the biplane contains every pair twice, sum over its blocks of binom(k,2) is 20. The above costs are at least (3/5)·binom(k,2), so every high ray hits at least 12 old sparse rows, analytically. In fact the complete support replay gives 1,152 choices with 60 sparse blockers, 1,440 with 82, and 96 with 86.
- Fix one old sparse row. Each new blocker agrees in sign on at least two shared positions. Charge to such a pair; there are 15 possible pairs. For each pair, delete its two signed common coordinates from the high heads. Their residual heads have squared norm 3 and pair dot≤−1, so at most four fit by the nonnegative squared norm of their sum. Thus an old sparse row is hit by at most 60 selected high rows.

For a compatible collection of k new rows the two disjoint old layers cost at least (12/60+6/7)k=(37/35)k >k, before paying the pole. This is an exact continuous-independent count for the stated integer high shelf, not an exclusion of altered heights/amplitudes or any simultaneous addition of other kinds of cap/dense rays. The alternative high axis shelf (±2eᵢ, tail 2) has 32 mutually compatible rows with the equator, old flats and negative half; its obstacle is the old upper sparse layer, not the poles once the top pole is removed.

## Second escape: cut one signed root and preserve both old poles

Take hot coordinates 0 and 1. Delete exactly the equatorial root (2,2) on them. Add two explicit rays (√3,√3,0,…; ±√2). They have norm 8 and mutual dot 4; all remaining equatorial roots and signed octads and both poles stay. The largest possible old flat dot at its original latitude is (4/3)(√3+1)<4, regardless of signs. Against a unit-magnitude sextad on the same side, the new ray is illegal precisely when both hot coordinates occur with positive signs. The norm-six dual head (3/2 at one mark, 1/2 elsewhere) is likewise forbidden precisely when its mark is one of the hot positions and both hot signs are positive. The other hemisphere's cap and all opposite-polynomial cases contribute a negative tail product and are safe. A construction maintaining 704 tier+dense points on each side after these changes yields **4320−1+2+2+2·704=5731**. The shared root cost is one for both new points, not two.

**A rigorous reason this cannot be ordinary low-overlap rephasing.** Keep only unit-amplitude sextads with pairwise support overlap≤2 and arbitrary flat rays in each side. There are at most 16 supports by the biplane counting inequality; at equality their pair intersection matrix is 4I+2J, every column sum is six, and MᵀM=4I+2J by conjugation through the invertible incidence M. Thus exactly two supports contain the hot pair. Each such bank has at most 24 allowed rows (at most 8 per remaining hot sign pair, by the Q4 matching); others have at most 32. With f flat rows, restriction to six coordinates gives at least ceil(f/6) sign patterns by the length-10 Plotkin bound (flat differences have distance≥6). Each forbids its exact matching sparse sign. For f≤192 the total is at most 496+192=688. For f>192, the bound with 16 supports is f+48+14(64−ceil(f/6))≤688, and with at most 15 supports is at most f+15(64−ceil(f/6))≤672. Thus the modified low-overlap, flat/ordinary sextad architecture reaches exactly 688 on one side, even with arbitrary permitted flat heights. This does NOT cover dual marks, overlapping partial banks or other head amplitudes. The 688 benchmark is attained by removing exactly 16 sparse rows from the 704 side.

**An exact larger, still finite construction problem.** Use L={linear/affine sign word fixing both hot coordinates(=0)} of order 8. The signed two-hot donor is invariant under this group. Allow individually selected size-eight orbits: ordinary six-support rows (unit amplitudes at tail ±√2), dual-mark rows (1/2 except one 3/2 at same height), and flat rows (2/3, tail ±√8/3). The original equator/poles protect these by exact sign code RM(2,4), except the single known removed root. Same-side compatibility is tested by all 8 possible relative sign actions; in integer numerators scaled by 6, each sum of body products plus twice the product of the tail numerators must be ≤144.

Use a common graph on the plus side, then copy it with a sign translate s in RM(2,4) vanishing at the hot pair and tail sign reversed. Cross cap/cap is always safe by Cauchy (head norms squared 6 and tail contribution −2). Cross ordinary sextad/flat is always safe. Cross flat/flat or dual-mark/flat is bad iff their sign words coincide, because distinct RM(2,4) words differ in at least four positions; this criterion is checked between the orbit sets after the translate. There are 64 distinct admissible shift classes modulo L. Any independent family of **88 orbits with at least one admissible mirror shift** gives an exact witness, with no global optimization assumption. The precise pool is 7,696 orbits (3,472 ordinary sextad, 3,968 dual marks, 256 flats); the explicit base is 86 orbits (shift 34952), giving a newly checked 5,699 under the root/donor trade. The all-pairs geometry check for this 5,699 seed covers 16,236,451 unordered pairs.

**An exact stop rule within 11 removals of that particular 86-orbit seed.** Give every outside orbit its exact blocker mask of old seed rows from the same-side graph; this ignores all mirror conflicts, so the conclusion is valid for every choice of shift. All outside orbits pay at least five old rows. Any positive exchange with at most eleven removed old orbits has a minimal actual removed set equal to a union of the selected outside blocker masks. Reinsert any unnecessary old row conceptually for the same-side relaxation; it cannot lower its size. Starting from all 2,014 distinct individual masks of size≤11, closure under unions of size≤11 tests 27,363 groups. In each mask U count every possible new orbit with blocker mask contained in U, ignoring all new-new incompatibilities as an upper bound. No group has enough new options even to break even (best raw deficit is −4). In particular any path to 88 requires a real exchange involving ≥12 old orbits relative to this seed. This is not a ceiling for the whole pool and does not assume that 86 is optimal.

## Exact replay and status

Optional candidate file contains count and shift on line 1, followed by orbit indices. A candidate with at least 88 verified orbits gives exactly 5731 by checking the first 88.
- `verify_frontier17.mjs`: reproduces the exact high-shelf counts and the orbit-neighbourhood proof.

**Stronger full-flat-coset exclusion, allowing arbitrary individual dual-mark rows.** Suppose one hemisphere keeps 192 flat points as six full RM(1,4) cosets in RM(2,4), not necessarily the chosen six. Global sign translation makes one coset zero. In the six-dimensional quadratic quotient let q be the bent indicator and B its polar form. For the other five, q(w_i)=1 and B(w_i,w_j)=1 for distinct i,j. Their Gram matrix has only the all-ones relation, but z=Σw_i satisfies q(z)=5+10=1; hence the five are linearly independent and their annihilator is generated by the bent z. Any allowable sextad must belong to z's biplane, and its compatible sign pattern is odd. Thus at most 496 sparse rows remain after the two-hot restriction.

A dual-mark word is compatible with the full flat coset φ only if their difference in the quotient is bent: its integer condition is d≥4+σ, where d is bit distance and σ=+1 if the marked bit agrees. A zero quotient fails, and in any rank-two nonzero coset the four weight-four differences partition all 16 positions, so one fails without flipping the mark. Bent differences all have distance ≥6 and pass. The possible dual cosets therefore satisfy B(θ,w_i)=1 for all five. They are at most two and B(θ,z)=1, so every such dual word has odd parity on each of the sixteen biplane supports. It conflicts with the exact matching ordinary sparse sign row on each of the 14 unaffected supports (head dot at least 3 plus tail dot 2). No selected set uses two different dual marks on the same sign word (head dot 5). Any prescribed sparse sign row can be hit by at most two selected dual words, one in each affine coset, because affine restrictions on the six support are bijective to the 32 even-relative words. Hence d selected dual rows pay at least 7d lost sparse rows. Total tier+dense size ≤496−7d+d+192≤688. Thus a winning root-cut design with 192 flat rows cannot keep them as six whole affine cosets even after freeing all sparse/dual rows within these specific amplitudes. Not a result for a nonlinear set of 192 words, differing dense or other magnitudes, or copying over a different equator.

**Mirroring is a sumset condition, not a numerically chosen phase.** In RM(2,4)/L let F and D be the flat and dual sign-class sets. A t-flipped mirror shift can be any member of the 64-element hot-zero subgroup outside (F+F)∪(F+D). For example, with 24 flat orbits and no dual, if the four hot-sign fibers have six flat orbits each then there are at most 1+4·binom(6,2)=61 forbidden shifts in that subgroup. So any balanced nonlinear solution of the single-side problem would admit at least three exact mirror shifts. The numerical driver checks the full condition without assumptions about balance or dual marks.

**Analytic stop rule for the natural two-level amplitude transfer.** With the same equator, two poles and at most the specified one removed signed root, take a possible additional dense ray with positive magnitudes a on one bent sextad S and b on its complement, and an arbitrary positive tail t. This is distinct from a flat head and permits every continuous ratio. The D16 roots still give a,b≤1: each amplitude occurs on more than two coordinates, so there is at least one retained root within either class. Every affine octad meets S in 2 or 4 positions; parity can lower the maximum aligned expression by at most twice its smallest amplitude. For a≥b this implies 4a+2b≤4; since the poles require head squared norm≥6, the bound 6a²+10b² ≤6−6b+(23/2)b² then forces b≥12/23 (the excluded b=0 case is an ordinary unit sextad). For a≤b the same argument with a 2-intersection octad gives 6b≤4, and the poles force a≥√(7/27). Thus every magnitude is >1/2. Matching the equator's odd parity on any octad would already exceed dot 4, so every possible sign word is in RM(2,4).

This continuously reweighted ray still conflicts with any fixed original flat ray on its side at sign distance at most four. A weight-four difference in RM(2,4) is an affine plane. A bent sextad has each nonzero difference on exactly one unordered pair (the 2-(16,6,2) difference-set property), hence cannot contain an affine plane. Therefore at a≥b, at most three of the four mismatches can have high amplitude; the signed head sum for inner product against the flat ray is at least 8b. The full dot is at least (16/3)b+(√8/3)√(8−6(1−b/2)²−10b²). This is concave on [12/23,2/3] and the two endpoint values are 64/23+4/3 and 40/9, both >4. For a≤b the signed head sum is at least 6a+2b, and the dot lower bound is the corresponding (2/3)(6a+2b)+(√8/3)√(8−6a²−10b²). It is concave on √(7/27)≤a≤b≤2/3, so check the three triangle vertices. Each exceeds 4: at the first vertex the head exceeds 8/3 and tail exceeds 4/3, at the second d≥4√(7/27)+8/9+4/3>4, and at the flat vertex d=40/9. Distance zero is easier; no nonzero RM(2,4) difference has lower distance. Moreover in both branches the head squared norm is at most 64/9, so t≥√8/3; exact alignment on any old six-support, with all amplitudes >1/2, is a forbidden dot. Thus this tempting family cannot open an old Hamming-four flat seam by changing a single head's amplitudes, nor match unfavourable intact old sextad signs. It does not preclude two independently reweighted heads coexisting, other masks, three amplitudes or a coupled scaffold change. Not part of the numerical driver's finite pool.

**Even stronger: the benchmark shift mirrors every admissible half automatically.** The chosen w=34952 has two disjoint weight-four words in w+L, namely 34952 and 17476, and its other six words have weight eight. Any cross-side coincident flat/flat sign orbit would differ on the plus side by a word in w+L, thus violates the plus-side flat distance-six rule using a weight-four relative difference. For a flat/dual coincidence, at least one of the two weight-four words avoids the mark; its plus-side threshold is d≥5 when the marked bit agrees, so it too was already forbidden. This shows **every** valid independent set in the one-sided 7,696-vertex geometry mirrors with w=34952, not just the seed and not merely by approximate balancing. The complete native graph counted 21,644,624 compatible unordered pairs both with and without explicitly imposing cross-side w, in agreement with this short argument. The two heuristics differ only in how their warm banks are traversed; there is no omitted geometric mirror cost for this w.

**A genuine nonlinear dense control, not covered by the six-full-coset argument.** Extend the benchmark's six affine classes to an eight-class distance-six Nordstrom–Robinson-type carrier W=[0,13,33,46,50,60,23,27] in the checker's quadratic indexing (each pair is verified to differ by a bent class). Each of the eight classes comprises four L-orbits, one per hot-sign label. Consider retaining exactly three per class, omitting one per class with equal 2-per-label counts, giving 192 flat points but not six whole affine cosets. All 8!/(2!)⁴=2520 choices are exhausted. Each cap/dual candidate gets its exact blocker mask relative to all 32 dense orbits; only masks contained in the 8-omission set can enter. After filtering there are 118 ordinary sextad options in 2,184 cases and 122 in 336 cases, before cap-cap compatibility. For each case a direct integer clique cover of the allowed cap/dual graph (pairwise incompatibility within each colour) uses at most 38 cliques, so at most 38 such orbits can accompany the 24 dense ones. This is well below the target 88. The largest 38-clique cover is an upper bound, not an example at 38. The new alternative specifically rules out this balanced partial-eight-coset pattern, not arbitrary choices of 24 slices, multiple uneven vacancies, different dense carrier supports or continuously marked heads. Exact replay: `verify_nr_split17.mjs`; it recomputes each clique and checks every assigned pair via its incompatibility matrix. Future positive work must change the dense orbit counts more drastically (or change the physical head shapes), not merely omitting one block from every complete NR coset.
