# Exact local barrier for Li's 21D line-hole construction

Benchmark (Kevin Li, https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00248v1, §3): the full sign code has 4096 words, four independent connected blocks of size 1024, and the known hole subcode H of size 256 leaves 3840 sign vectors and 31,560 total. Li Remark 3.6 already states connected components and the four-block 3908-sign counting ceiling; don't count these as new. The independent-hole condition is sufficient for arbitrary choice of a neighbor's line, not necessary for every valid labeling.

In one component of 1024 words, translate Li's 64 holes to H=ker η on the line span. On the 21 generators, the quotient has multiplicities 3 on tags 0001, 0010 and 0011, and 1 on all other nonzero tags (Table 2). Each original hole h therefore has exactly twelve private neighbors: the generator translations of singleton tags. These twelve-sets are disjoint for different h. Two distinct vertices share at most two neighbors by uniqueness of pair sums of projective lines. Adjacent vertices share no neighbors because no three generator lines have zero sum. Both properties follow from pair intersections of size one.

Suppose a different independent dominating set removes k original holes and adds j new ones, remaining in this component. All removed holes must be adjacent to some new hole (there are no edges between old holes). To cover the twelve neighbors that were private to any removed h, one new hole adjacent to h can cover at most itself, and every other new hole covers at most two. Hence j≥7. If k≤8 and j<k the only possibility is k=8,j=7.

Call an added hole triple if its tag is one of the three repeated tags; otherwise singleton. A triple is adjacent to exactly three removed old holes (it cannot be adjacent to a retained old hole by independence); a singleton is adjacent to exactly one. At least one triple is necessary to cover the eight removed holes. Any added triple can cover at most ten of the 96 private neighbors of the eight removed holes, because it covers none in the three adjacent holes' private sets and at most two at each of the other five. A singleton covers at most fifteen of those 96 (at most one for its adjacent old hole, two for each other). Thus two or more new triples would cover at most 2·10+5·15=95 private neighbors, impossible.

There is exactly one triple u. Each of its three adjacent old holes needs the other six added holes to cover its twelve private neighbors, so EACH added singleton must cover exactly two private neighbors in all three of those old holes' sets. By translating by H, it suffices to choose one representative u from each of the three triple-tag cosets. The companion exact script constructs the actual 21 lines from Table 2 and exhausts the 768 singleton-tag words in the component for each choice. It finds zero that cover two in each of the three sets simultaneously. Thus there is no improvement from replacing at most eight original holes in a component by fewer, among independent dominating sets. Same result for the full code with at most eight deletions from the original hole set (before any additions): any overall improvement must reduce at least one component, since components have no cross edges. General nonindependent hole sets or larger trades are not excluded.
