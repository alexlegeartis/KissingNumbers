# 17D: Latin-cube equators, full-bank rigidity and an exact 512-sparse construction of a second profile

2026-11-27, Lower84. No improved lower bound: target 5731, incumbent 5730 by Cohn–Li, [arXiv:2411.04916v3, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04916v3#S3). Takhanov's [arXiv:2611.00927v1, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00927v1#S2) gives 4320-point 16D equators based on six matching permutations and three order-four Latin cubes, not a new 17D count. We tested exact mathematical mechanisms and identified two independently sharp no-gain principles, plus a second-profile construction attaining the old count. None is an upper bound for the unrestricted 17D kissing problem.

## Normalization and exact interfaces

Work with norm squared 8 in 17 dimensions. Let a sparse head be z in {0,±1}^16 of weight 6 at tail +sqrt(2) or −sqrt(2), and a dense head be (2/3)y, y in {±1}^16, at tail +sqrt(8)/3 or −sqrt(8)/3. Two points in the same cap are compatible iff:
- sparse/sparse: z·z' ≤ 2;
- sparse/dense: there is at least one mismatch on the six live coordinates, since the dot is (2/3)(6−2m)+4/3;
- dense/dense: Hamming distance ≥5, strengthened to ≥6 if the dense signs have a common parity.
Cross opposite caps are automatically safe for sparse/sparse and sparse/dense. Opposite dense rows with sign words in a common affine coset of RM(2,4) are compatible if and only if their words are different: any distinct pair has distance at least 4, and a duplicated sign word has dot (64−8)/9>4. Work on the two caps can be separated for the upper estimates below.

In Takhanov's construction rotate every quaternion block by tau inverse. The axial E vectors now become D4 roots (raw two ±2 entries in one block); the two-block Y families match C_0, C_1 and C_2 across blocks, where C_0 contains signed coordinate axes and C_1,C_2 are the even and odd half-sign frames. Their raw entries are doubled. The four-block M_r families use the three frames of signed D4 roots, each block with two ±1 entries. Label each level by (b,c) in F2², b the support pair from its coordinate matching, c the relative sign. Both overall signs are free in every block.

For a (3,1,1,1) sparse head, a mixed pair permutation not fixing C_0 disallows any high triple at either endpoint: a half-sign frame can align all three prescribed signs in that block; a matched axis in the other block contributes 2, giving 5>4. Otherwise E and Y pose no sparse obstruction. For every r, a four-block M_r point conflicts iff its b-vector selects the complete pair on the high triple and the pair containing the singleton on each other block, and its relative c-value on the high block agrees with the prescribed product of its two signs. Thus arbitrary changes of Takhanov's Latin cubes reduce to checking literal two-bit fibres; a fibre empty gives no restriction, one constant c excludes that c, a fibre with both c-values excludes the profile. In the odd Barnes–Wall equator (product cubes), the restriction is just XOR=0 on the missing high coordinate and three singleton coordinate labels. This gives all 256 (3,1,1,1) supports and all 64 sign possibilities individually, before within-cap packing.

If all Y matchings fix C_0, dense compatibility against both half-sign matches forces the block parities p_u to obey p_u+p_v=constant for every pair. A nonempty bank therefore has a common overall sign parity: each p_u equals a common free bit plus a fixed shift, and four times the free bit vanishes. This holds before applying the Latin-cube constraints. If a mixed Y exists, both endpoints are unavailable as high blocks, so at most 256 (3,1,1,1) sparse points are allowed by the next argument. Even without common parity, even-extend the dense sign code to length 17 and distance 6; the published A(17,6)≤340 (Brouwer's [disclosed constraints](https://aeb.win.tue.nl/codes/lpdetail.html), or Schrijver) is more than enough to give at most 596 points in that cap. The simple distance-6 bound A(16,6)=256 used below is the classical Nordstrom–Robinson optimality bound, e.g. [Bierbrauer](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=6972ce239d794ab45484b02b17ebf353f31d36e4&repid=rep1&type=pdf).

## Arbitrary signed triple fibres and punctured Nordstrom–Robinson

For a fixed high block and actual signed triple on it, the remaining three singleton spikes of sparse points have squared norm 3 and pair dot at most −1: the common triple consumed 3 of the raw dot budget 2. If n spikes are selected, the squared norm of their sum is at most 3n−n(n−1), hence n≤4. There are 4 possible missing coordinates ×8 signed triples in each high block, hence 4×32×4=512 sparse (3,1,1,1) points per cap, for any equator and any overlap/sign choices.

At equality each four-row fibre sums to zero componentwise and has pair dot −1. In each low block, its 4 entries either use one unsigned axis twice in each sign, or two unsigned axes once in each sign. A one-axis block contributes +1 on one perfect matching of the six row-pairs and −1 on its complement; a two-axis block contributes −1 on one perfect matching and 0 otherwise. Thus zero one-axis blocks means three different matching pairings, one means the other two pairings coincide with the first block's +1 matching, two is impossible, and three means the +1 matchings exhaust the edge colors. These are the only tetrahedron types. In the zero-one-axis case, the six low-coordinate sign bits orient the six edges of K4 to their matching endpoints. A dense word matching the high triple avoids all four sparse conflicts iff that tournament has no sink (reverse the orientation convention if desired). In the one-one-axis case the fixed block sign chooses which of two independent sign-pair parity checks is applicable. In the three-one-axis case this reduces to the usual parity bank. Thus nonlinear switches exist locally; none has yet beaten the geometry below.

Stronger: if the chosen dense code D lies in any 256-word Nordstrom–Robinson carrier N, then the following is valid for *all* six-support sparse shapes, not just the triple fibres:
```
|S| ≤ 8 (256 − |D|).
```
Indeed each six-coordinate signature occurs at least twice in N. Our V8 replay generates N as eight affine RM(1,4) cosets and checks its d=6 and all 8008 six-projections: 448 have multiplicities 2/6, 7560 uniform 4. Snover's uniqueness (see [Gillespie–Praeger §2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.3878)) makes this independent of the representation, even after arbitrary coordinate sign/position changes. For each chosen sparse row all matching dense N-words must have been omitted, so charge at least 2 omitted words. For any omitted dense word, the compatible chosen sparse rows matching it have common signs on every shared coordinate, hence pair support intersection at most 2. There can be at most 16 such six-subsets: for b supports with column degrees r_i, sum binom(r_i,2)≥(36b²/16−6b)/2, while pairwise overlap ≤2 bounds it by b(b−1). Therefore each deletion pays at most 16 charges. For (3,1,1,1) sparse heads this and |S|≤512 imply |S|+|D|≤704. More broadly, even allowing all six-site sparse profiles, any D⊂N of size ≥193 forces |S|+|D|≤697. If |D|≤192, a record via these profiles requires at least 513 sparse points on one side.

## Full low-overlap banks: biplane gives sharp cap

For a fixed six-mask, the sign sets must avoid Hamming distance 1. At most 32 rows are possible, by a coordinate-edge matching in Q6; equality gives one whole parity class (equality in the edge bound of a connected regular bipartite graph). If there are ≤14 support banks and dense signs have common parity, the cap is bounded by 14×32+256=704. At 15 or 16 entire banks with pair overlap≤2, the geometry has stronger rigidity independent of the block shape. For 16 supports the previous counting bound is equality, so their pair intersection is identically 2 and every column has degree 6. Incidence A satisfies AA^T=4I+2J, and by conjugating with A, also A^TA=4I+2J. For 15 supports, minimizing sum binom(r_i,2) for sum r_i=90 gives 210 (ten degrees 6, six degrees 5), which already equals 2 binom(15,2); equality fixes every pair overlap to 2. The six degree-5 coordinates meet each old row twice (sum of their within-row contributions r_i−1 is 28), supply the missing row, and that row is also the XOR of the 15 old rows. Thus 15 or 16 whole banks lead to the same incidence span.

These are symmetric (16,6,2) biplanes; exactly three classes have binary rank 6, 7, 8. See [the rank-six uniqueness discussion in the 1992 Trans. AMS paper](https://community.ams.org/journals/tran/1992-330-01/S0002-9947-1992-1055565-6/S0002-9947-1992-1055565-6.pdf) and [Biplanes with flag-transitive automorphism groups](https://doiserbia.nb.rs/ft.aspx?id=1452-86301600021Y) for the classification. The exact portable replay constructs representatives of all three via permutations on the four high-triple groups. Normalize group 0 so each of its four coordinate labels is the same; index every other group by its coordinate-0 value. For each group u≠0 the two remaining label permutations split the cycles of the high-coordinate permutation, so there are 53 options; comparing three such groups uses only literal equality of the two matching multisets. There are 16,72,60 normalized families of the three ranks. Rank 6 is therefore the Cohn–Li Kummer cross system. A dense bank avoiding full sparse banks lies in an affine translate of the incidence dual (a dense word must have opposite parity on every mask). It has common parity because 1 belongs to each incidence span. In rank 6 Cohn–Li Lemma 3.1 bounds its distance-six size by 192. For ranks 7,8 the checker exhibits two independent dual words of weight 4 whose sum also has weight 4. Their four-element translation group limits the dense code to one per coset, giving 128 and 64 respectively. This completely rules out replacing the 16 full low-overlap banks alone. The 15 case with these hypotheses is in fact at most 672. A partial 15/16-support system does not impose the same parity equalities and is not covered by that part of the result.

## A distinct exact 512-stock using (2,2,2,0), and its Latin-square ceiling

For the standard odd 16D equator use the four blocks as the four rows of the usual F2²×F2² array. E/Y/M together are all 480 signed two-coordinate roots and all 30 affine hyperplane masks with all odd sign rows at amplitude 1. A six-support sparse row is equator-admissible iff no five of its support points lie in an affine hyperplane: otherwise we can fill leftover signs to give a dot at least 5. This holds exactly when its six coordinates sum to zero in F2⁴. Indeed if no five lie in a hyperplane, any five are affinely independent and the unique affine dependence on six points uses all six; in reverse, if five were in one hyperplane, their sum (the sixth) would be there too, whereas six distinct points in an eight-point affine hyperplane never have zero sum. This says exactly the 448 weight-six words in RM(2,4)=RM(1,4)^perp. The block shapes are 256 of (3,1,1,1) and 192 of (2,2,2,0): even row weights force (2,2,2,0), (4,2,0,0) is incompatible with zero sum; odd row weights force (3,1,1,1). For (2,2,2,0), the sum of each marked pair is its nonzero difference r, so the three occupied blocks must use the three different matching colors.

Fix the missing block v in F2² and assign each active u the matching r=u+v. At each of the three occupied blocks take one signed root axis from that level. On unsigned axis labels impose an arbitrary Latin square of order 4 (16 triples, distinct triples agree in at most one coordinate), and take all eight overall block signs. This gives 128 points per omitted block, 512 in total. In the same family distinct unsigned triples give dot≤2; when the unsigned triples agree, one reversed sign gives dot at most 2. Between different omissions, only two blocks overlap, and their colors differ in both; each local raw inner product is ±1, giving ≤2. All these supports satisfy the equator gate because the colors are distinct.

For the dense flat head, compatibility against every old odd signed octad is exactly even parity on each of the 30 affine hyperplane masks (if not, the old mask matches all 8 signs; if yes, max 6). Thus the equator-only dense bank is precisely RM(2,4), *not* its Cohn–Li subcode C10. Write the common row parity as p, and take the matching-r pair with coordinate 0 to have bit b=0. A given dense word matches exactly one of the two relative signs at each of the two support-pair choices, so the axis labels it can match in that block have the form c=c_0+p b, c_0=y_{u,0}+y_{u,r} in binary sign notation. For one omission and fixed p, these are one of two disjoint 2-sets in each of the three label spaces. To avoid matching any selected sparse word is equivalent to a 2×2 quadrant of the Latin square outputting only the other 2-set, necessarily as an intercalate. If one such quadrant exists, every quadrant is an intercalate: the remaining entries in each of those rows and columns are forced to use the complementary pair. Thus for each p the omission either admits no dense row, or imposes exactly one affine parity condition on the three c_0 bits. When each Latin square is additive a_1+a_2+a_3=t, this condition is sum c_0 ≠ c(t)+p b(t).

The functional for omission v has mask M_v={(u,0),(u,u+v):u≠v}, namely the four integer masks 38192, 22787, 12437, 857 in row bit order. Every pair of masks differs by a word of RM(1,4), so the check is the same modulo RM(2,4). For arbitrary Latin squares, a viable parity p therefore imposes one check with some RHS depending on p, or is empty. In the union of two parities the RHS is affine in p. Absorb its linear part using the parity mask of the omitted row v (the test can be written using any row); if added, its disjointness from M_v gives weight 10, whose complement has weight 6. Thus the resulting dense bank always lies in a coset of the dual hyperplane inside RM(2,4) defined by a bent mask. Every weight-six RM(2,4) mask is affine-coordinate equivalent to a standard cross: its distinguishing quadratic polar form is nondegenerate, and after an invertible linear map identifies the polar forms, a translation removes their residual linear difference; the constant is then determined by the common weight. So this hyperplane is C10 up to coordinate permutation, and there are at most 192 dense points. If only one p survives, extending its RHS to the other p likewise gives a containing C10 coset.

Taking the additive squares with t=(0,1) ensures the dense bank is exactly the zero-syndrome hyperplane. Apply Cohn–Li's two disjoint 192 words in C10 under this affine relabeling to the two caps; both contain the same 512 sparse head collection at opposite tails. Their opposite-cap checks were established above, and both meet the pure poles at dot at most 4. This gives exactly 4320+2+2(512+192)=5730 using a (2,2,2,0) latitude decomposition. No claim of inequivalence of the entire 5730-point codes is made.

## Transferable proof gates and reproducibility

The portable standalone verifier is `lower84_17d_quaternionic_biplane_nr_verify_20261127.mjs`; run with Node, or evaluate in V8 replacing both console.log calls by text. The replay also builds every point in the alternative 5730 construction (dense quadratic labels [0,12,23,37,43,57] and disjoint translate label 2) and directly checks all 16,413,585 unordered pairs in exact integer numerators with norm 72, contact threshold 36 and observed maximum 36. It certifies all small finite assertions including ranks, NR projection counts and sparse support census. It also verifies two further observations for future construction:
1. In the quadratic quotient RM(2,4)/RM(1,4), two full affine cosets have minimum cross-distance 6 iff their difference is bent. On 64 vertices normalize a clique to contain 0; exact clique traversal shows all maximal clique sizes are 4 (56) or 8 (8), and every extendible 4-clique extends to exactly one 8-clique (280 such cliques =8 binom(7,3)). Therefore a dense d6 code containing four full affine cosets is either of size at most128 (no exterior word), or contained in a unique NR carrier: any exterior single word must be bent-adjacent to each full coset. A potentially winning flat code at ≥193 outside NR must have at most three full affine cosets; this is not the same as doing another balanced puncture of eight.
2. As a route-independent check, arbitrary six-site sparse heads give a spherical code in R^16 with dots in {-6,...,2}/6. A Delsarte polynomial with coefficients [55,704,3201,8520,13464,11016] in normalized Gegenbauer basis of dimension 16 is nonpositive across all those integers and yields 36960/55=672. It is a coarse outer bound, not a closed proof of a 512 ceiling across mixed head shapes.

The next logically independent positive target is **513 or more** sparse points using a genuinely coupled mixture of the (3,1,1,1) tetrahedral switches and (2,2,2,0) Latin fibres, rather than separately saturated parallel banks. Another lane is at least 193 flat dense words not contained in any NR, with at most three full affine cosets and enough matching sparse. Changing tail/physical geometry remains open.
