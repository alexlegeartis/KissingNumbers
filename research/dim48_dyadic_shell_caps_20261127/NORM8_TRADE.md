# 48D: a fixed-lattice norm-8 exchange needs 10,469 additions

No new lower bound. The ambient is any extremal even unimodular rank-48 lattice L, whose 52,416,000 norm-six vectors are the incumbent. Keep any subset of these unit-normalized old vectors and add any pairwise-compatible subset of unit-normalized norm-eight lattice vectors. If at most **10,468** new vectors are used, the net count cannot increase. This is stronger than the previous scoped 6,857 cutoff; neither limits off-lattice donors or arbitrary large trades. The full affine periodic obstruction is separate.

For a candidate y of norm eight, an old vector x of norm six conflicts exactly when (x,y)=4. Integrality plus \|x±y\|²≥6 give |(x,y)|≤4, and the normalized threshold is √48/2 between 3 and 4. The blocker x maps bijectively to a norm-sixteen representative y−2x in the same residue as y mod 2L: conversely given such representative v, the two nonzero vectors (y±v)/2 have norm sum twelve so both are old roots. The s=8 zero-cusp series in the companion modular proof gives m16=2,256. This independently reproduces the earlier [Boyvalenkov–Cherkashin/Venkov design](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05121) moment calculation.

## Pair intersections

Let y,z be distinct compatible norm-eight donors, t=(y,z)≤4. For any common blocker x, \|x−y−z\|²=6+2t≥6, unless x=y+z. The exceptional case is impossible by dotting with y: it would give 4=8+t, hence t=−4, but then norm(y+z)=8 rather than 6. Thus t≥0, an integer. For two distinct common blockers x,x', let p=(x,x')≤3. Applying minimum six to x+x'−y−z gives 2(p+t−2)≥6, or p≥5−t, except for exact equality x+x'=y+z. Dotting this equality with y forces t=0, and then for fixed x there is at most one such x', so the entire common blocker list has ≤2. At t=1 nonzero difference is forced and p≥4 impossible, so ≤1.

For t≥2, all common blockers share the same projection onto span(y,z), of squared magnitude 32/(8+t). Their residuals lie in rank 46 and have squared norm R=6−32/(8+t). At t=2, p=3 for all distinct, so their normalized residual mutual dot is −1/14; a sum-of-vectors norm bound gives at most fifteen. At t=3 the normalized residual dots are −5/17,1/34. The degree-two polynomial 1+(1173/58)G1+(4335/58)G2 vanishes at both, with normalized Gegenbauer G_k for dimension 46; positivity gives size ≤2783/29<96. At t=4 the normalized residual dots belong to −1/2,−1/5,1/10. Use 1+(65/4)G1+(1875/4)G3, nonpositive at all three, to obtain ≤486.

Consequently the full intersection cap is 486, and more precisely the caps at t=0,1,2,3,4 are 2,1,15,95,486. A further geometric feature at t=4: a p=1 blocker pair x,x' completes uniquely to a third blocker y+z−x−x', of norm six by the same lattice identity. The residuals form a 120° triangle. Two p=1 neighbors of a fixed point force the third p=1 edge, since applying lattice minimum to the sum of the three blockers minus y+z gives p12+p13+p23≥6 unless the sum is zero, and under two p=1 edges the largest possible sum is five. So these are disjoint triangles. Relative to any other blocker, each triangle has exactly one residual dot −1/5 and two 1/10, by zero residual sum. At t=3 the same three-blocker lattice identity forbids triangles of all p=2, since its squared norm would be 4 (and the sum cannot vanish as its y-dot is 1).

## Global arbitrary deletions

For k additions there are exactly 2,256k blocker incidences; second moment of blocker degrees is at most 2,256k+486k(k−1). Cauchy–Schwarz gives at least 2,256² k/(2256+486(k−1)) distinct old blockers which have to be deleted. This is at least k for k≤1+floor(2256·2255/486)=10,468. No shielding or choice of unrelated old deletions evades the conclusion. The [companion exact verifier](verify.mjs) checks the two Gegenbauer polynomial inequalities and threshold; there was no numerical geometry, timed enumeration or native computation.
