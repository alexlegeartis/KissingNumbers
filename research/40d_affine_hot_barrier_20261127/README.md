# Lower89: whole affine one-hot barrier in 40D

**No new lower bound.** The [current 40D construction](https://github.com/alexlegeartis/KissingNumbers/tree/a304d9f04756c67f07c7df9fc846b989c1bb0826/verifications/improved/dim40-qr-central-split) remains **1,139,159**, 39 over the [B. Smart and Edel–Rains–Sloane external construction](https://github.com/alexlegeartis/KissingNumbers/tree/main/verifications/recovered/dim40-smart-ers) of 1,139,120. The octads are Smart [A(40,8,8)≥4267](https://aeb.win.tue.nl/codes/cwc/d8/a40.8.8.4267H), and the 589,824 flat signed rows are tied to [Edel–Rains–Sloane](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0207291). They are attributed externally, not a priority claim. Archived Lower51 already excluded arbitrary selections with ≤4 multi-child parents per hot even allowing arbitrary sign recoding, and all unpaid selections regardless of parity; Those earlier bounds are not assumed in the new proof. The two moment/finite-field estimates below are new.

## Precise scope and result

Keep all signed D40 roots, the original 0/unit-prefix QR dense scaffold except deleted parents, and signed ±1 rows of each original Smart octad (which may be reselected). All daughters are original sharp one-hot daughters: on a central parent v from the zero-prefix shortened binary QR C=[40,16,12], put multiplier −a with a=√41−2 at its selected hot i and preserve the other 39 signs. Changes of geometric motif, sparse carrier, or QR parent code are not covered. Let V_i⊆C be the selected sets of parents for hot i. Same-hot legality forbids all steps of Hamming weight 12 with bit i zero, because shared hot sign at distance12 raises the raw angle above half; all higher weights give the old bound. A daughter with hot i on octad S forbids its exactly matching restriction v|_S+e_i, and forbids no other sign on S (the next raw possibility is a+5 instead of a+7).

The net gain in this model is at most **+39** whenever every nonempty V_i is either an arbitrary set of size at most **85**, or an arbitrary affine subspace of C (with a possibly different subspace, dimension and translation for each i). This result allows **mixed parity** of the surviving sign rows on all octads; the choices need not be complete parity classes. Under the stronger assumption that each old octad retains a subset of one freely chosen parity class, replace 85 by **212** for arbitrary V_i (per-hot cutoffs reach 305). Both results are exact mathematical ceilings only for these scopes, not global 40D upper bounds or a proof for the unrestricted one-hot model.

## Exact small-set combinatorics

For every fixed hot i, between any two legal distinct parents there are at least t_i≥340 octads S incident on i where their restricted parities differ. At most r_i≤33 incident octads have exactly the same restricted sign for that pair. In the latter case the pair differs on no site of S, so i agrees; its weight12 case is illegal, and all counted collisions can be bounded from QR differences of weight >12 only. The actual incidence degree D_i is 852–855. These exact statements are verified by reconstructing the QR mother by translates of quadratic residues, reducing to the rank16 zero-prefix subcode, and evaluating parity disagreements by a Walsh transform. Full restriction coincidences are counted from each octad kernel. The marked physical convention matches the original first eight binary QR coordinates; octad hexadecimal masks use bit 0 as physical coordinate 0.

An octad's sign graph is Q8: Hamming distance one is the forbidden edge. Maximum independent sets of size 128 are precisely whole parity classes: Q8 is regular and bipartite, and an independent half-cube saturates the edge count, so its complement is independent too; connectedness makes the bipartition unique. Thus if V_i forces a forbidden sign of each parity on S, that octad must lose at least one row even if it mixes surviving signs. For m=|V_i|≥2, at most floor(m²/4) oppositely-parity pairs are accounted for by a single contested S. There are therefore at least t_i·binom(m,2)/floor(m²/4) contested S. For m≤85 this is ≥8(m−1).

The stronger second-moment calculation counts distinct signs instead. On incident S let x_S count parents projecting to the parity with fewer distinct forbidden signs, and f_S count those distinct signs. The product x_S(m−x_S) counts all parent pairs of opposite projected parity independently of which side was chosen. If A=Σ_S x_S, summing gives
m A−A²/D_i ≥ t_i m(m−1)/2.
Let A0 be the least integer satisfying this on the increasing half of the quadratic (if the true A is above its midpoint, certainly A≥A0). Group repeated projections as multiplicities. Their total pair collisions over all S, even before restricting to the cheaper sign parity, are at most r_i·binom(m,2). Cauchy twice gives
Σ_S f_S ≥ A²/(A+r_i m(m−1)) ≥ A0²/(A0+r_i m(m−1)).
First apply Cauchy to each S's distinct image multiplicities, then across S; ignore f_S=0, x_S=0. For m≤212 this is ≥8(m−1) on all hots, by the fully integral quadratic and ratio checks in the replay. It bounds actual sign loss if the octad uses one parity; it also does so for affine V_i using the following lemma.

## Affine forbidden sets cost exactly half even against mixed signs

Let the forbidden sign set F in Q8 be affine with direction U and containing equally many signings of either parity, equivalently U contains an odd-weight vector u. Choose an odd vector of minimum weight, with support I. Then U∩F₂^I={0,u}: a proper odd subvector violates minimality, and a nonzero even vector supported on I makes u+v a smaller odd vector. Each coordinate I-subcube therefore meets F in zero points or in two antipodes. In the latter case the antipodes have opposite parity (|I| is odd); deleting two opposite-parity vertices from a Hamiltonian cycle of the cube leaves two even-cardinality paths and hence a perfect matching. The trivial one-dimensional case is immediate. Match every I-subcube separately. Consequently Q8\F has a perfect matching, and any surviving independent set is at most 128−|F|/2. One parity class after deleting its forbidden elements attains this. If F has only one parity, choose the complementary class and pay nothing. This lemma concerns a subset of forbidden signs from a single hot; additional forbidden signs only strengthen the loss.

When V_i is affine of dimension k≤7, |V_i|≤128, and this lemma justifies the second-moment payment even when mixed surviving signs are allowed. At k≥8, the signed projection dimension estimate is simpler. For each S the rank16 C restriction kernel K_S has a finite Cayley graph with edges for all weight12 differences. Each S through i contains i, so any chosen V_i fibre is independent in this graph. Its Hoffman bound is at most 53 across all 4,267 S by integral Walsh spectra: for regular adjacency of degree d and least eigenvalue λ, the zero inner adjacency of the independent-set indicator gives size ≤|K_S|(−λ)/(d−λ). An affine intersection has power-of-two size, thus ≤32. On any S whose parity character is nontrivial on the direction of V_i, both parity classes of its forbidden image consequently have size at least |V_i|/64. All parity characters on octads incident to that hot are nonzero, and among their D_i occurrences the repeat excess over distinct characters is at most 11. At most (2^(16−k)−1)+11≤266 occurrences can annihilate the dimension-k direction. At least 586 S therefore pay |V_i|/64 each, exceeding 8|V_i| in sum.

Finally, if L_S is the actual total count lost from octad S after imposing all daughters, L_S bounds each applicable individual hot charge. It therefore bounds their sum divided by eight, since S contains eight coordinates. Summing across S pays at least Σ_i(|V_i|−1) over nonempty banks. After subtracting also at least \(|∪_i V_i|\) removed parents, the net gain is bounded by (number of used hots) minus (number of removed parents), hence at most 39. The existing all-plus split attains it.

**Next choice:** A positive result cannot come from trying bigger linear/affine codimension choices in this model: all such hot-dependent choices are now closed. Non-affine banks larger than the explicit thresholds remain mathematically open, but the cleaner escape is a different daughter body/contact geometry. In particular a second light sign flip changes the octad label without changing the root norm; it is not free against the actual dense scaffold since QR d=11 neighbors differing at both altered coordinates then fail. Any two-coordinate escape therefore needs to redesign or jointly pay that dense layer, not blindly append another per-hot affine code.
