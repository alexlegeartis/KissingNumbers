# 20D: five tetrads, D₄ triality and a sharp 19,448 interface

## Result and its scope

Let the twenty coordinates be divided into five four-coordinate tetrads, chosen as the complements of a fixed 4-set in the five octads of the extended binary Golay code through it. Use the 15 support-class profiles below. Allow all of the following, with no fixed signs, no global triality requirement, and no linear assumption on the transversal arrays:

- 24 in-block roots, each supported on one tetrad, in each of the five blocks;
- a maximum compatible subfamily of the complete D₄ triality product on each unordered tetrad pair;
- a maximum compatible subfamily of signed roots in each of the 15 transversal support-class profiles;
- dense points made from one of the 24 D₄ triality directions in each of the five tetrads. The directions can be axes or either sign parity; the block magnitudes may vary independently for each dense point, as detailed below.

Here the first three (sparse) contributions have norm squared 8 before normalization; the equal-magnitude dense model below uses norm squared 20 before normalization. Under the **maximum sparse-stock hypothesis for the fixed 15 profiles**, the sharp total is 120 + 1,920 + 15,360 + 2,048 = **19,448**. This neither improves τ(20) nor bounds the unrestricted problem or trades in which sparse subsets are intentionally deficient. It covers arbitrary nonlinear Latin hypercubes at full stock and arbitrary local triality choices on all ten edges. It also gives a strict 3.5-to-1 deletion barrier for adding dense axis heads to the old transversal stock, even when all pair edges and arbitrary old-sparse deletions are allowed.

Reference incumbent: Henry Cohn and Anqi Li, *Improved kissing numbers in seventeen through twenty-one dimensions*, [arXiv:2411.04916v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04916v3); Henry Cohn's [kissing-number table](https://cohn.mit.edu/kissing-numbers/). This is a larger interface than either their fixed dense Golay code or the earlier two-amplitude calculation. Vince Gonzalez's [analysis of the fixed added-vector code](https://f-keys.com/papers/maximality-added-vector-codes-cohn-li/) already gives its 20D decomposition into 128 5-cubes. The work below concerns variable sparse stocks, triality, and dense points not necessarily flat. Standalone exact data and geometry replay: [`verify.mjs`](verify.mjs), replay with `node verify.mjs`.

## Tetrads, support classes, and separation of the geometry

Puncture the four coordinates T₀. The five octads containing T₀ give disjoint suffix tetrads T₁,...,T₅. Distinct such octads meet exactly in T₀, because an intersection of size greater than 4 would contradict the Golay minimum distance, and five disjoint tetrads exhaust the other coordinates. Take A as the 130 octad supports disjoint from T₀; take B as the 120 eight-coordinate suffixes of dodecads containing T₀. The ten pair unions Tᵢ∪Tⱼ are in A. Each other A support and every B support occupies exactly two coordinates in each of four tetrads. Two supports from the same bank intersect in at most four coordinates. A and B meet in six exactly when their symmetric difference is one tetrad: if they meet in six, their full Golay words have difference an octad through T₀. Conversely toggling an occupied Tᵢ changes an A octad to a B dodecad suffix, or back. These identities, including support counts, are replayed directly from the extended Golay generator.

A position-pair on four coordinates and its complementary pair form one of three matchings. Thus a transversal support has a profile of four matching labels (one absent tetrad). There are exactly 15 such profiles, three per omitted tetrad; each contains all 16 complementary-pair choices, eight A and eight B. The 90 receipts (two occupied tetrads, their two matching labels) occur exactly once over the 15 profiles. Each profile consumes six receipts; 90/6=15 is consequently a sharp upper on any family with no repeated receipt. Within the same omitted tetrad its three profiles differ in all four labels. Across different omissions they have at most one equal shared label. All finite incidence claims are replayed exhaustively.

Before normalization, pair products are sums of two norm-4 vectors; transversals are sums of four norm-2 signed roots. For code points of equal squared norm 8 the allowed raw dot is ≤4. In one tetrad, each matching supplies eight roots ±eᵢ±eⱼ (two complementary pairs); they are an orthogonal four-axis alphabet. Across different matching classes the root dots are ±1. Thus profiles sharing all four tetrads have raw dot ≤4 after differing in all four matching labels. Profiles with different omissions share three tetrads, at most one equal matching label, giving dot ≤2+1+1=4. Pair edges and transversals are automatically compatible: a pair edge meets at most two occupied tetrads, with each contribution ≤2. Two different edges meet at most one tetrad, where the dot is ≤4. An in-block root is 2(±eᵢ±eⱼ); it is automatically compatible with pair products, transversals, and dense heads, by the same one-block dot calculation.

## The full two-block product: all six triality pairings

Write the 24-point single-block alphabet Q as three mutually orthogonal-axis alphabets of eight vectors each: Aₓ = {±2eᵢ}, E = even-sign ±1 quadruples, and O = odd-sign ±1 quadruples. All have norm squared 4. Cross-type dot products are ±2. On a two-tetrad edge allow the complete product Q×Q, 576 possible points of norm squared 8. Fix the first vector. The selected second vectors must have mutual dots ≤0, so there are at most eight by the elementary orthoplex bound in dimension four; equality is an orthogonal cross-polytope and has only one type because cross-type vectors are never orthogonal. Therefore there are at most 24×8=192 selected points. Equality for columns as well as rows forces an entire type to map to an entire type: if x in type i has all eight neighbors of type j, every such neighbor must have all eight predecessors of type i. Hence a maximum product is exactly a permutation matching of the three types. Conversely all six matchings are feasible: distinct matched types differ at both ends and give ≤2+2=4; within a matched type pair, at most one of the vectors can coincide at the two ends.

Using Aₓ→Aₓ and E↔O recovers 64 cross-roots plus the 128 old odd pair-support octads. A permutation not fixing Aₓ has two pointed banks (one concentrated axis at one end, a signed quadruple at the other) and one flat-flat bank. Block triality is the normalized symplectic Walsh matrix indexed by F₂²:
$
  H_{a,b}=\tfrac12(-1)^{[a,b]}.
$
It interchanges Aₓ and E, fixes O, and preserves all three root-matching classes: a root on \{b,b+v\} maps to a root on a coset of \ker[a,v]=\langle v\rangle. A one-coordinate sign flip interchanges E and O and preserves the class of every root. Together they realize all six permutations of Q types without changing any of the 15 admitted transversal matching profiles.

A dense head from Q⁵ conflicts with a pair-edge vector exactly when the latter reproduces the dense head's two block symbols. A shared norm-4 symbol contributes 4; a different one contributes at most 2. The cutoff is \sqrt{8\cdot20}/2, strictly between 6 and 8. Thus the edge permits all dense heads except the type matches specified by its permutation. No edge relaxation is needed below: the dense obstruction already holds if all edge constraints are ignored.

## Saturated transversal banks and the missing-box rigidity

Fix one matching profile. Transversal roots $r_j$ in each coordinate alphabet are identified by an antipodal axis q_j in F₂² and a sign. Fix any three full roots. Because they contribute 6 to a pairwise raw dot and only ≤4 is allowed, only an antipodal pair of choices can share that three-root restriction. This proves a cap of 8³×2=1,024. At equality every such fibre has exactly the antipodal pair, hence membership is invariant under changing the sign in any one block. The selected points are all 16 signings of 64 axis tuples; each triple of axes occurs exactly once. In particular this is an arbitrary quaternary Latin hypercube, not assumed affine.

A dense head in Q⁵ hits a transversal exactly when all four overlapping blocks contribute +2: the contributions are in {-2,0,2} and the raw cutoff is again between 6 and 8. In each block precisely two axes each offer one root sign giving +2. The two axis labels form one affine line in F₂²; the three Q types correspond one-to-one to the three line directions. For a candidate dense head to be admitted, a product of four affine lines must be missing from the 64 axis tuples.

Project those tuples onto four bits by the nonzero linear functionals perpendicular to the four line directions. Each fixed triple of bits occurs eight times. Denote the counts on the four-bit patterns by f. Then f(b)+f(b+eⱼ)=8 for every b and j. If one forbidden pattern is absent, f is exactly 0 on its parity class and 8 on the other. Equivalently the head imposes a linear parity identity on **all selected** axis tuples, with right side equal to one plus the sum of the four affine-line constants. This argument only uses the triple marginal property. If two admitted heads have distinct type profiles that agree in one occupied block and disagree in another, subtracting their parity identities gives a nonzero constraint on at most three blocks, a contradiction. Since some transversal profile contains any designated pair of blocks, globally two different admitted profiles differ in every block. There can be at most three profiles. Two distinct profiles already force each max Latin array to be affine: at each omitted coordinate, their equations use two independent functionals, hence uniquely determine that coordinate from the remaining triple.

## No third dense profile: the Golay simplex

If three different type profiles exist, apply in each block a triality that sends them to uniform Aₓ, E and O respectively. Those transformations keep the full class of admissible sparse stocks. Choose one admitted head from each profile. In a fixed matching profile take its reference octad c∈A. Use line-axis coordinates (a,p) at every occupied tetrad: a selects the reference position-pair versus its complement and p is the sign parity on that chosen pair. The three affine-line equations for a head of type Aₓ, E, O are
$
    a=\beta_{A},\qquad p=\beta_{E},\qquad a+p=\beta_{O}.
$
For Aₓ, \beta_A is zero or one according to whether the chosen coordinate is in the ref pair or its complement. For E and O, \beta is the parity of minus signs of the head on the reference pair. If v is the binary indicator of the five Aₓ coordinates and e,o are the minus-sign masks of the other two heads, the sums of the \beta constants on the four occupied tetrads are c·v, c·e, c·o over F₂, respectively (four 1's cancel for the axis convention). The three missing-box identities sum to
$
  c\cdot (v+e+o)=1.
$
But v+e+o has even parity in each tetrad (both v and o odd, e even). The span E₀ of all A octads has rank 8. Its intersection with the span H₀ of the tetrads has rank 4: the even tetrad unions are in E₀ while adding any odd union would imply a Golay word of weight four. The 15 reference octads project to the fifteen distinct nonzero elements of the four-dimensional quotient E₀/(E₀∩H₀). They are nonzero because transversals are not tetrad unions and distinct since different matching labels cannot differ by whole complementary tetrads. A binary word even on each tetrad induces a linear functional on that quotient. It takes value 1 either 0 or 8 times, not all 15. Thus the third profile is impossible.

Within a single profile, map it to uniform E by class-preserving block triality. Any two admitted heads in it give the same parity identity on each Latin bank. Their difference as binary words is therefore orthogonal to all 15 reference octads and all 5 tetrads. Those masks span a rank-9 space, so the permitted sign words are contained in a single affine coset of size 2¹¹. Adding any full tetrad mask preserves that coset but changes exactly four signs. For two fully flat heads, inner product ≤1/2 requires Hamming distance ≥5. A perfect tetrad-flip matching bounds the selected set by 1,024. This is per-profile, and at most two profiles give ≤2,048, as claimed.

For equality use the old A transversals with odd signs on each support, the in-block roots and old pair-edge transposition stock. Let K be the 4-punctured extended Golay binary code, dimension 12. Its only nonzero weights ≤4 are the five tetrads, because a full Golay word of weight ≥8 can lose at most four positions. Expurgate K by one parity functional taking value 1 on each tetrad, yielding 2,048 words of distance at least 5. Every word of K is orthogonal to every A support by Golay self-duality; hence no dense word fully matches an odd-signed A octad. The original exact stock is feasible and attains 19,448.

## Robust axis insertion barrier and useful escape measurements

For the original A-only transversal code, a dense five-axis head hits a given matching profile exactly when its positions define an even complementary-pair choice (equivalently c·v=0). Since the 15 reference octads map to the simplex and one can subtract a fixed transversal-axis word having even inner products with all (verified using the 10-bit location space), the axis mask evaluations are of weight 0 or 8. For a direct construction of such a fixed word, each tetrad has four locations and an axis-location word consists of five of them. The coordinatewise differences from a fixed word span all tetrad-even words. The exact generator yields 64 words at weight 0, 960 at weight 8; the checker verifies the zero row, so no unproven affine offset is involved. A head therefore hits at least seven matching profiles. For each such profile its support is prescribed. Fix the signs at its four marked axes; half of the remaining four free signs meet the odd parity, giving exactly eight distinct old transversal conflicts per profile. Conversely one old transversal conflicts with at most 16 mutually compatible axis heads: two allowed choices of marked coordinate, each with prescribed sign, in each of four occupied blocks. Heads making the same four choices have raw dot ≥16−4=12>10, irrespective of the fifth block. Double count to get at least $\lceil 56 |D_{\rm axis}|/16\rceil$ old transversal deletions. This argument does not charge any pair-edge deletion.

The basic fixed-parity A/B signed graph has an alternate clean proof useful for exact phase moves. Within each of the 15 matching profiles record the four complementary-pair choices a and the four selected-pair sign parities p. Cross-edge conflicts preserve all six shared signs and toggle one a-bit. Odd A signs and even B signs make η=p+a invariant, sum η odd. There are eight such 256-vertex connected 8-regular bipartite graphs per profile; equality 128 forces an entire side in each component by saturation of Hall. Hence 120 independent neutral signed switches. For any flat dense head whose tetrad sign parities are mixed, at least one of those modules is hit in both choices. For h=1,2,3,4 odd tetrad parities, the exact minimum number of such double-hit modules is 48,42,24,12, respectively, even before charging pair edges. With both B signs, the sharp sparse cap still follows: for one fixed pair toggle and fixed six shared signs, the two A extensions join to a four-cycle of B extensions, with independence number 2. The full Latin-array argument above goes further and does not fix any A or B sign parity.

A genuine positive construction must move beyond this equality interface: (i) controlled sparse deficiency, not another optimizer over maximal Latin banks; (ii) repeated two-tetrad matching receipts with explicit signed overlap repair; or (iii) different block amplitudes not all in Q. The second option has quantitative room: across ALL matching labels, the fibre fixing any two block roots has at most 24 residual vectors by the orthoplex bound in the remaining 12 coordinates, so complete transversal stock has upper bound
$
  \frac{\binom52\,24^2\cdot24}{\binom42}=23{,}040
$
rather than 15,360. A residual fibre can attain 24 locally by a perfect pairing between coordinate axes of the three residual tetrads, using each edge with both relative signs and both global antipodes. Compatibility among many fibres, not per-fibre density, is the mathematical target. No candidate above 19,448 was found this round. No native or external-write process was started.

## Other low targets considered

12D requires ≥842 versus the 841-point Takhanov–Assylbekov–Yun construction ([arXiv:2606.18984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18984)), and 21D requires ≥31,561 versus Kevin Li's 31,560 ([primary source](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00248v1)). The previous 12D near contacts and the fresh 21D one-label parity-flat obstruction were not re-run. For 20D an independent bare \sqrt2 D₄ torus screening of the length-4/6 Leech shells returned a sharp universal-interface count 18,728, below the incumbent; this is a separate conservative construction route, not an upper bound on physically compatible fibres.


## Stability: six deletions round to a full Latin bank, and three charts cost ≥35

These statements do not assume the original A orientations. Fix one of the 15 matching profiles and split a selected transversal bank into 16 sign slices F_s with s∈F₂⁴, choosing once an orientation of every axis. Each F_s is an axis-word code of minimum Hamming distance two, so |F_s|≤64. If s and t differ just at coordinate j, every row of F_t sharing the other three axis values with a row of F_s must be the same axis row: the pair would otherwise contribute 6 from the other three blocks and zero from the fourth, which exceeds the allowed 4. Thus if either slice is full, the other is a subset of its Latin array.

Suppose total deficiency $\delta=\sum_s(64-|F_s|)\le7$. Adjacent full slices have the same Latin array. Two full slices at sign distance two have one common intermediate slice with at least 57 rows, lying in both arrays; two distinct Latin arrays overlap in at most 56 rows. To see that, the difference of their 0/1 incidence functions has every three-coordinate marginal zero. Any nonzero n-dimensional integer array with every (n−1)-coordinate marginal zero has ≥2ⁿ nonzero entries: slice along one coordinate and use induction, with at least two nonzero slices required by cancellation in that coordinate. Thus for n=4 the indicator difference has at least 16 nonzero entries, half positive and half negative. The full sign slices form a connected graph when sign distances one and two are allowed: each vertex has only five other vertices at distance ≥3, a closed ordinary radius-one ball about its antipode. The intersection of any two such radius-one balls has at most two vertices. Therefore a disconnected induced set has at most six vertices (one singleton plus at most five; if both sides have at least two, both sides have size at most two). There are at least nine full slices here, so all their Latin arrays agree, call the common array L.

Any selected row outside L must belong to an incomplete sign slice whose four neighbors are also incomplete, for one of those neighbors being full would force membership in L. Two such central sign slices have a union of their closed radius-one neighborhoods of size at least eight, impossible here. Hence if there are extra axis rows they all lie in one slice. Its four neighbors are subsets of L and each must omit at least k tuples if the central slice contains k outside rows, since the k rows project injectively onto any selected triple. Thus k≥2 already costs deficiency ≥8. For k=1 the central slice must omit the four distinct L rows matching the extra on three coordinates (one in each direction); it has deficiency at least three, and each neighbor loses another point. Consequently a bank of size ≥1018 (defect ≤6) is always a subset of a complete max Latin bank. The threshold is sharp: start with all 16 sign slices equal to L; put one row q outside L in one slice, delete its four old one-coordinate neighbors in that slice, and in each adjacent sign slice delete the corresponding old row sharing three fixed axes. This produces a legal 1017-point bank which cannot extend to a 1024-point bank.

For any collection of three dense heads that differ in Q type at every coordinate (each pair), triality makes their profiles uniform Aₓ,E,O. The previous simplex gives at least seven profiles where the associated four-box parity conditions sum inconsistently. On such a cube, if its deficiency is already ≥7, it costs ≥5. Otherwise complete it by the preceding result. For each of the three heads define B_t as the parity of the four direction bits measured relative to its forbidden box. In a bad cube their three bits sum to zero on each axis tuple, so every tuple has at least one B_t=0. Among the 64 rows, denote by n_t the frequency of the head's specific forbidden box. Triple marginals give exactly 8n_t rows of parity B_t=0. Hence $n_A+n_E+n_O\ge8$. The axes of any two heads use different affine-line directions in every block, so those boxes intersect in at most one axis tuple and at most one signed root; each contributing row of one box gives exactly one signed conflict. The forbidden union therefore has at least 8−3=5 signed roots. This proves at least 7×5=35 missing transversals in any such triple-chart arrangement. A pair of type profiles which agree on one block and disagree on another, on the other hand, forces all nine banks containing those two blocks to be non-maximal (choose one of three remaining omitted blocks, and three profiles each).

For reference, any one dense Q⁵ type profile has cap 1024 even without sparse walls. Fix three full symbols; residual admissible vectors must pairwise have raw dot ≤−2, hence ≤−4 since dot is a multiple of 4. Their first residual symbols are all distinct and so are their second symbols: a duplicate contributes +4 and the other can contribute at worst −4. Therefore antipodal edges in each residual block form a matching. Three residual rows would need all three pairs covered by two matchings, impossible. The cap is 8³×2. It follows in particular that no improvement using this equal-magnitude Q⁵ family can have fewer than nine total missing transversal points, even if every other sparse choice changes; an improvement using only pairwise coordinatewise distinct type profiles needs ≥35 missing, hence at least 2084 dense points.

### Amplitude rigidity of transversals under full edge stocks

With all pair-edge products saturated (each an arbitrary full triality permutation), an orthogonal four-root transversal cannot vary its positive block magnitudes at all, irrespective of the in-block roots. For a fixed D₄ root in one block, each of the three Q types has exactly two vectors with dot +2 against it. On every edge between two occupied blocks a pair point can attain +2 in both blocks simultaneously. For raw transversal $(a_i r_i)$, $\|r_i\|²=2$, the normalized condition forces $a_i+a_j\le \sqrt{\sum a_k²}$ on every such pair. For the largest two magnitudes $\alpha\ge\beta>0$ among four, this implies $2\alpha\beta\le\gamma²+\zeta²\le2\beta²$; equality throughout forces all four equal. With two or three positive block weights it is impossible. A nonflat weighted transversal must therefore remove or redesign pair-edge products too, not just change its own local coordinates. For each edge where $a_i+a_j>\|a\|_2$, at least 12 edge vectors attain the offending double maximum (three matched types with two maximizing block symbols each).


### Variable magnitudes of dense Q blocks still do not help at full edge stock

The sharp retained-stock theorem also allows each dense vector its own arbitrary nonnegative block weights: its raw vector is $(a_i q_i)_{i=1}^5$, with $q_i\in Q$ and $\sum_i a_i²=1$ (hence its raw norm is 2). This is distinct from the optional discrete small-deficiency count above, which uses the unweighted single-profile cap.

For any ordered pair $i,j$, a full pair-edge product has a vector whose $i$-symbol is exactly $q_i$ and whose $j$-symbol has dot at least 2 with $q_j$. Its dot with the dense head is therefore at least $4a_i+2a_j$. The cutoff $\sqrt{8\cdot4}/2=2\sqrt2$ implies
$
 2a_i+a_j\le\sqrt2.
$
Write α≥β for the two largest weights. Since $1\leα²+4β²\leα²+4(\sqrt2-2α)²$, and $0\leα\le\sqrt2/2$, the smaller quadratic root gives $α\le A=(8\sqrt2-3)/17<0.4891$ (the larger root is above $\sqrt2/2$). Set $u=\sqrt2/3$. The squared sum of any four weights is at most $8/9$: if α≤u this is immediate; if α≥u use $α²+3(\sqrt2-2α)²$, which decreases on $[u,A]$ and equals $8/9$ at $u$. So every weight is at least $1/3$. On any transversal, if all four local Q-root dots are +2 then its weighted raw dot is $2\sum_{i \text{ occupied}}a_i$, and the sum is at least $(1-A²)/A>1.5>\sqrt2$. Thus the exact same forbidden product boxes and parity restrictions are necessary for weighted heads. In particular there are at most two charts and each is still a subset of a single sign coset of size 2048 after triality.

It remains to justify the tetrad flip matching with arbitrary different magnitudes. Same-sign heads cannot be repeated, as their normalized mutual dot is $\sum a_i b_i\ge5/9$. Heads differing by exactly one entire tetrad j have mutual dot
$
 1-\tfrac12\big((a_j+b_j)^2 + \|a_{\ne j}-b_{\ne j}\|_2^2\big).
$
The bracket is strictly below 1. Here are elementary bounds for all cases. Put $L(x)=\sqrt{1-x²-3(\sqrt2-2x)²}$, $U(x)=\sqrt2-2x$ for $x\in[u,A]$. When a head has $a_j=x\ge u$, its other entries lie in $[L(x),U(x)]$: the upper bound is the ordered edge constraint, and each lower follows after bounding its other three entries by $U(x)$. The intervals are nested, $L$ increasing and concave and $U$ decreasing and affine on $[u,A]$; at $u$ they are $[1/3,u]$, and at $A$ both endpoints equal $U(A)$.

- If $x=a_j\ge y=b_j\ge u$, the bracket is ≤$(A+y)^2+4(U(y)-L(y))²$. Its upper expression is convex in $y$, by convexity and nonnegativity of $U-L$, so its maximum is an endpoint. At $y=u$ it is $<0.9606²+4(0.4715-1/3)²<0.99912$, and at $y=A$ it is $4A²<0.957$.
- If $x\ge u\ge y$, the bracket is at most $(x+u)^2+4\max\{U(x)-1/3,A-L(x)\}²$. This is again convex in $x$. At $x=u$ it is $8/9+4(A-1/3)²<0.987$; at $x=A$, using $U(A)-1/3<0.103$ and $A-U(A)<0.054$, it is $<0.9606²+4(0.103)²<0.966$.
- If both $x,y\le u$, bound the bracket by $8/9+4(A-1/3)²<0.987$.

The elementary rational endpoint inequalities use $1.4142<\sqrt2<1.4143$ and their direct squares; the tighter upper estimates for $u<0.4715,\ A<0.4891,\ U(A)<1/3+0.103$ follow immediately. Thus a full-tetrad sign flip always gives normalized dot $>1/2$, and there are at most 1024 heads per chart despite arbitrary independent continuous weights. No change to the sharp 19,448 bound. A magnitude escape must also change the single-tetrad directions $Q$, introduce sparse deficits including pair-edge cuts, or change the selected matching quotient.
