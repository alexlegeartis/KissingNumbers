# Three scoped structural constraints on the Zinoviev–Ericson 13D cap

**Baseline still 1154, not claimed optimal.** Primary: V. Zinoviev and T. Ericson, *New Lower Bounds for Contact Numbers in Small Dimensions* (1999), Theorem 1, https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/ppi457. Their proof gives an 816-point signed quartet equator plus 168 cap points at each height ±1/2, plus 2 poles. Each cap is built from a 144-word binary code of length 12/distance 4 and 24 signed unit axial directions, all scaled by sqrt(3)/2 in the ordinary coordinates. The named source supplies the geometry. The exact 51 quartet masks and 144 binary masks used in the replay come from Henry Cohn's MIT archive https://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/153312 (the read-only transparent 13D extract at Git blob `88e648045a3ac1d02092bb71355318420ee40488` in Rafael Amichis Luengo's reproduction repository; extract is not attributed as a new construction). The exact external binary bound A(12,4)=144 is by Östergård–Baicheva–Kolev (IEEE TIT 1999): https://users.aalto.fi/~pat/72.html; A(9,4)=20 is tabulated in Brouwer's exact small binary codes https://aeb.win.tue.nl/codes/binary-1.html.

For the 13D ambient point write (x,t) with ordinary body x, squared norm 1. The two hemispherical caps at height ±1/2 are mutually compatible for *any* unit body code, since (3/4)−1/4=1/2. Thus improving just one hemisphere suffices, and any pole replacement at t≥1/2 also automatically clears the entire opposite fixed cap.

## A. One pole cannot split while the fixed axial and maximal binary cap remain

Remove the positive pole but keep the same equatorial 51 full signed quartet blocks and all 24 northern axial points plus any fixed valid size-144 ordinary binary sign code at height 1/2. Suppose a new point has height t≥1/2. From the northern axes, |x_i|≤(1−t)/sqrt(3). The case t=1 is simply the removed pole. For t<1 put z=sqrt(3)*x/(1−t), w_i=|z_i|. Then w lies in the unit cube, N=||w||²=3(1+t)/(1−t)≥9. The binary cap requires max_c c·z≤2sqrt(3), with sign vectors c in {±1}^12.

Any (12,144,4) code has radius at most 3 (otherwise adjoining an uncovered sign word would break A(12,4)=144). A nearest word with at most 3 mismatch positions gives L=sum w_i≤6+2sqrt(3). Consequently D=L−N≤2sqrt(3)−3<0.465. With at most eight w_i≥1/2, N≤8 + D<9, since w_i²≤w_i(1−w_i) on [0,1/2]. Thus there is a nine-set of high positions. The exact 51 quartets have two disjoint blocks inside each nine-set (all 220 checked).

Each equatorial block Q gives sum_{i in Q} w_i≤sqrt(3)/(1−t) since its old signed ordinary entries are ±1/2. Using N≤L we obtain 1/(1−t)=(N+3)/6≤(9+2sqrt(3))/6, so every quartet sum is bounded by 1+(3/2)sqrt(3)<3.6. A quartet in the high nine-set therefore has deficit sum_{i in Q}(1−w_i)>0.4, with individual deficits in [0,0.5]. Its contribution to D is sum d_i(1−d_i)≥0.4−0.4²=0.24: trim deficits to total 0.4, use monotonicity, and then bound the squared sum by 0.4². Two disjoint high quartets cost ≥0.48, contradicting D<0.465. Thus not even a *single* additional point at heights [0.5,1) can be added under these retained walls. No whole cap or global claim.

## B. The actual code blocks even safe pair-rotations of all northern axial vectors

Independently rotate the northern ordinary axial basis in disjoint coordinate 2-planes, leaving its height and every other old family unchanged. Any angle with |cos|+|sin|≤2/sqrt(3) is safe for these axes versus all fixed cap and equatorial points (ordinary L1 bound and existing hemisphere independence). One e.g. is (cos,sin)=(143,24)/145. This class cannot open a high hole, however. Retain the actual old 144 sign rows for this claim.

After coordinate permutations/signs choose each rotated-square block with c=cos≥s=sin≥0; c+s≤2/sqrt(3) implies c>49/50. The candidate z of part A now lies in the rotated cube. Every coordinate |z_i|≤M=2/sqrt(3), and each pair has squared norm ≤(L1/c): in each orthant, the convex quantity ||z||²−L1/c has maximum at corners of the rotated square intersected with the orthant. Square vertices have squared norm 2 and L1=2c, and axis intersections are at radius 1/c; all give zero (or zero at the origin). Singleton blocks obey the same inequality. Thus N≤(50/49)L.

For a sign pattern of Hamming distance at most 2 to the actual code, L≤2sqrt(3)+4M=14/sqrt(3). For the 320 distance-three orthants, the 15 minimum mismatch triples have an exact fractional perfect matching: give all triples weights by their sorted incidence-degree type. For the 256 holes of the first type, weights on (2,2,5),(4,4,4),(4,4,5),(4,5,5) are 1/2,3/8,5/24,1/24; for the 64 of the second type weights on (2,2,5),(4,4,4),(4,4,5),(5,5,5) are 1/2,1/2,1/6,0. Each coordinate receives total weight one, exactly checked, so total edge weight four. Some mismatch triple therefore costs ≤L/4. Hence max_c c·z≥L−2(L/4)=L/2 and L≤4sqrt(3), stronger. In both branches N≤(50/49)max(14/sqrt(3),4sqrt(3))<9, ruling out t≥1/2. This does not cover larger rotations exploiting special sign cancellations, or relative cap/equator motion.

## C. Equal-amplitude support classification at height 1/2

At t=1/2, a body with w equally sized nonzero magnitudes has each magnitude sqrt(3/(4w)). Its support S is admitted by every full signed equatorial quartet Q iff 3|Q∩S|²≤4w. Exact incidence shows only support sizes 1,3,12 occur, with 12 singles, 16 triples and one full support. The 16 permitted triples are two separate octahedra of triples: on each of two disjoint six-coordinate groups, choose one coordinate from each of three fixed pairs (eight triples per group). The two octahedra groupings are embedded in the portable replay. This is a geometric restriction, not an assumed ternary alphabet.

In one octahedron encode a signed triple by three symbols, each of the form (one of two physical coordinates in its pair, sign). With two matching symbols, the third must be the opposite sign on the same physical coordinate: if it instead uses a different coordinate the resulting cap dot is 3/4, while exactly one sign reversal on the same support gives dot 1/2 and is genuinely legal. We account for this exception in the following proof. Let F be the full-length flat binary code count, U the number of retained signed axes, and T the number of signed triples. T triangles use M distinct signed physical coordinate atoms, so U<=24-M.

* If no unsigned triple support is repeated, within each octahedron the signed symbol code has true distance >=2. With q_1<=q_2<=q_3 the three numbers of signed symbols used in the pair positions, T_block<=min(8,q_1 q_2)<=q_1+q_2+q_3 (if there is no triangle this also holds). Thus T<=M and F+U+T<=144+24=168.
* If some unsigned support is repeated twice and none occurs three times, F<=6 A(9,4)=120. Indeed the one sign pattern agreeing with all three signs of a chosen triangle is forbidden for a flat sign word; on each of the other six projection classes the remaining nine signs have binary distance at least four. Each octahedron then has T_block<=min(16,2 q_1 q_2): each of eight physical supports carries at most two signings, and any two fixed signed symbols allow at most two possibilities for the third (which must be opposite signs at one physical site). Over positive q_i<=4, T_block-(q_1+q_2+q_3)<=7. Hence F+U+T<=120+24+14=158.
* If three or more signs occur on one unsigned triple support, F<=5 A(9,4)=100 by the same projection bound. To bound axes plus triangles, choose a uniform global sign assignment on a single six-site octahedron; a triangle whose three signs agree survives with probability 1/8, a signed axis survives with probability 1/2. In the surviving unsigned pool, triangles are binary length-3 words of distance >=2 (distinct supports must share at most one coordinate). There are at most four, and three or more require both coordinate choices in each pair, leaving no surviving axes: fixing any one pair choice would leave a distance-two binary code on two bits, of size at most two. For two triangles at least five sites are used, leaving at most one axial site. For one triangle at most three axial sites remain, and otherwise at most six. In every case the number of surviving triangles plus one quarter the number of surviving axes is at most four. Taking expectations gives T_block+U_block<=32. Summing the two blocks, F+U+T<=100+64=164.

Thus the claimed bound 168 holds for arbitrary choices from all three allowed equal-amplitude families, including any support phases, with equality from the classical cap. It does not generalize by itself to variable amplitudes; part D is one real-amplitude result that keeps all signed axes.

## D. Real body magnitudes at a cap against only the actual equator and full axes

Retain all 51 full-signed equatorial quartets and all 24 northern axial cap points, but assume **nothing about any binary cap**. Then there is no real point with height t in [12/25,1/2), and at t=1/2 any other point must have all 12 ordinary magnitudes exactly 1/4. Thus an attempt to replace the rest of the northern cap by arbitrary real body points at the standard height, still keeping its axes and the actual equator, gives at most A(12,4)=144 such other points: 168 total at that height is sharp.

To prove it, use z=sqrt(3)*x/(1-t), taking absolute coordinate magnitudes, and write C=sqrt(3)/(1-t), N=||z||²=2 sqrt(3) C−3. All z_i are between 0 and 1 by the axial interfaces. Every quartet Q has sum at most C. In this range C is between C0=25 sqrt(3)/13 and C1=2 sqrt(3). Write r=C/4, delta=z-r*1, R_Q=-sum_{i in Q} delta_i>=0, and Delta=-sum delta_i. The incidence matrix B of the 51 quartets has column sum 17, so sum R_Q=17 Delta and Delta>=0. Pair codegrees are 4 or 5, and each position belongs to four codegree-four pairs. Thus B^T B = 12 I + 5 J - A, where A is a 4-regular simple adjacency matrix, and delta^T(B^T B)delta >=8||delta||² + 5 Delta² via 2|ab|<=a²+b².

Each quartet Q belongs to a parallel class of three disjoint quartets. On either of the other quartets, a vector of four coordinates in [0,1] with sum at most C has squared norm at most 3+(C−3)², by concentration at three ones and one fractional remainder. Consequently the squared mass in Q is at least 2 sqrt(3) C−9−2(C−3)². This is strictly greater than 2+((13/17)C−2)² throughout [C0,C1]. Indeed their difference F(C)=2 sqrt(3) C−11−2(C−3)²−((13/17)C−2)² is concave, and positive at both endpoints: for C0 the first two terms equal 7/13, and the subtractions are smaller than 2(0.331)²+(0.55)²; for C1 these terms equal 1 and the subtractions are smaller than 2(0.465)²+(0.65)². Since (13/17)C lies in [2,3], the same concentration inequality shows sum_{i in Q} z_i > (13/17)C. Hence R_Q<4C/17 for every Q.

The uniform vector r*1 has squared norm N0=3C²/4. Here N>=N0, with equality only at t=1/2, as follows directly from N=3(1+t)/(1-t). Thus ||delta||²>=2r Delta. If Delta>0, the quadratic sum of quartet deficits is strictly below (4C/17)(17 Delta)=4C Delta, but the Gram lower bound makes it at least 8||delta||²>=16r Delta=4C Delta, a contradiction. If Delta=0 then every R_Q is zero; positive definiteness of B^T B forces delta=0. The required norm now forces t=1/2 and z_i=sqrt(3)/2, i.e. |x_i|=1/4 in the original coordinates.
