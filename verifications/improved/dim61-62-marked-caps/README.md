# Distinguished-amplitude capsule refill in 61D and 62D

The construction replaces a small portion of the earlier [nine-coordinate layer](../dim61-66-nonary-affine-caps/), not any of the dense, axis, four-support or weight-16 layers. These are lower bounds for the ordinary kissing number; the search is not an optimality result.

A new shape has one coordinate of absolute value 4 and twelve of absolute value 2, all in the 60-coordinate body. The squared norm is 64. Within a shape, use both signs of its distinguished coordinate and the 144 sign words of the canonical binary length-12 Julin code on the other coordinates. The code is constructed by Theorem 4 in [Conway and Sloane, 1994](https://neilsloane.com/doc/Me185.pdf), and this replay explicitly checks its version used here. A change of the mark sign or at least four low-coordinate sign changes reduces a norm-64 inner product by at least 32. Different shapes have maximum absolute-coordinate dot at most 32 by the finite certificate.

For compatibility with the inherited weight-16 layer, the checker requires the sum of absolute entries on every weight-16 support to be at most 16. Those old points have amplitude 2, so no old sign information is necessary. For compatibility with the inherited four-support layer, the analogous cap is 8. A four-support block has zero coordinate XOR. Without the mark the sum is automatically at most 8; with the mark only three occupied low positions could cause a violation, which the checker excludes by looking at all low triples. Axis products are at most 8 × 4 = 32. The body has absolute-coordinate sum 28, so the 62D dense comparison is at most 28 and the 61D dense comparison at most 28 sqrt(17)/4 < 32. The added points use zero tail coordinates.

The old nine-coordinate support radius uses amplitude 8/3. For each marked shape, retain every old nine-support block whose sum of absolute entries on that support is at most 12 and remove the union of violating blocks. Every such block has 40 signed points. Then refill with exact nine-support templates compatible with both the retained nine-support blocks and the new marked shapes, using the same 40-sign pentacode phase. No duplicate or removed point is counted twice. The replay constructs both small phase codes, checks every old/core support interaction using actual pinned input support lists, and derives the final counts.

Run the verifier with OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python verify.py, with NumPy installed. The earlier construction's underlying geometry is inherited, and its files are pinned by the checker. No search heuristic is needed to replay the result. The discovery used local support searches followed by a search across the previously enumerated nine-support pools for compatible refills.

The local discovery audit also exhaustively tried one replacement of a low coordinate, one replacement of the mark, or swapping the mark with a low coordinate, around each selected marked template. None passed the same four- and weight-16-support absolute caps, even with every earlier nine-support block ignored. This does not rule out two-coordinate moves, different templates, or phase-dependent compatibility.
