A KISSING CONFIGURATION OF 610394 POINTS IN DIMENSION 38
25 October 2026

THE RESULT

The accompanying explicit data establish tau_38 >= 610394. This is
5072 more than Qihong Lin's public 605322-point benchmark (22 October
2026), and 5048 more than our preceding 605346-point certificate.

The new idea is to assign Leech body lines jointly with head directions.
When two heads have a large inner product, orthogonal bodies can compensate.
This permits far more new points than a construction that independently
bounds all body products by 16 and all doubled-head products by 12.

The final data use 1352 different head lines and 215 distinct Leech body
lines of the form 4e_a +/- 4e_b. Body lines can be reused. The proof below
does not assume their special form: the verifier checks the joint products.

THE EXISTING CAP AND EQUATOR DATA

Use the integer model of the Leech lattice with minimum squared norm 32.
Its 196560 minimal vectors give 98280 antipodal lines. Choose each line's
representative u with first nonzero coordinate positive, and order these
representatives lexicographically.

The supplied class_labels partition these lines into 644 classes. Within
each class, distinct representatives obey |u.u'| <= 8. The supplied set W
contains 1932 distinct vectors in R^14 of squared norm 8 and pairwise inner
product at most 4. It is partitioned into 644 triples of sum zero, so the
three vectors in each triple have pairwise inner product -4.

For each body line u in class c and each w in the corresponding triple,
include

                   (+/-u, sqrt(2) w) / sqrt(48).

There are 6*98280=589680 such caps. Distinct body lines have absolute
inner product at most 16. In the same class the stronger bound 8 applies.
These bounds and the head bounds prove cap-cap inner products <=1/2:
different classes give at most 16+2*4=24 before division by 48;
the same class gives at most 8+2*8=24. The same body and different heads
give 32+2*(-4)=24. Opposite body signs are also compatible.

Lin's 6855 equatorial line representatives q have squared norm 48,
belong to the same Leech lattice, and obey |q.q'|<=24. Include both signs
of (q,0)/sqrt(48), for 13710 equatorial points. For a minimal vector u,
q +/- u are nonzero lattice vectors, hence have squared norm at least 32.
Consequently |q.u|<=24, proving equator-cap compatibility.

THE NEW LATITUDE FAMILIES

The certificate supplies 1352 pairs (u_i,z_i), where u_i is a canonical
Leech minimal vector, indexed in the lexicographic ordering above, and
z_i is an integer vector in R^14. The following finite conditions are
verified exactly:

  |u_i|^2 = 32,  |z_i|^2 = 24;
  |u_i.u_j| + 4|z_i.z_j| <= 64 for every i != j;
  |z_i.w| <= 8 for every w in W;
  |z_i.w| <= 2 for the three w in u_i's assigned cap class.

For each pair include four points, with independent signs:

                    (+/-u_i, +/-2z_i)/sqrt(128).

Their norms are one, since 32+4*24=128. Distinct families have product
at most 64/128=1/2 by the joint condition. Within each family the three
possible products between distinct points are 64/128, -64/128, and -1.

For an old cap with a different body line, the unnormalized numerator
is at most 16+16sqrt(2). For the same body line, the stronger class
condition gives at most 32+4sqrt(2). The common denominator is
sqrt(128*48)=32sqrt(6), and

             16+16sqrt(2) <= 16sqrt(6),
              32+4sqrt(2) <= 16sqrt(6).

The verifier proves these inequalities by squaring nonnegative integer
expressions. This covers every old cap, including both body signs.

The new body's squared norm fraction is 1/4. Its product with any unit
equatorial vector is therefore at most 1/2 by Cauchy-Schwarz. This
establishes compatibility with all 13710 retained equatorial points.

WHY JOINT ASSIGNMENT HELPS

Here z_i=2y_i, where y_i has six coordinates equal to +/-1 and the rest
zero. If |y_i.y_j|=4, the joint condition asks for u_i.u_j=0. If that
head product is 3, any distinct Leech body lines suffice, since their
absolute product is at most 16. If the head product is at most 2,
even reusing the same body line is permitted. These exact rules allow
1352 head/body pairs to coexist, instead of restricting the heads alone
to |y_i.y_j|<=3.

THE AXIS CONFIGURATION

Set Q=diag(-1,-1,-1,1,...,1) in dimension 14. This is exactly orthogonal.
Retain the 1568 vectors w in W whose eight nonzero entries are +/-1,
and include (0,Qw)/sqrt(8). The other 364 axis points are omitted.

The verifier checks, by integer multiplication,

       |w'.Qw| <= 6 for every w' in W and every retained w,
       |z_i.Qw| <= 8 for every i and every retained w.

The first inequality implies axis-cap compatibility: their product is
w'.Qw/sqrt(192), and 6^2<=48=192/4. The second implies new-axis
compatibility: their product is +/-z_i.Qw/16, at most 1/2.
Axis-axis compatibility follows from orthogonality of Q and the
verified pair bounds for W. Axis-equator products are zero.

Add another 28 axis points, namely (0,+/-e_j) for j=1,...,14. These are
mutually compatible and orthogonal to the equator. Against caps their
products have magnitude |w_j|/sqrt(24), at most 1/2 since w_j^2<=6.
Against retained flat axes the products are |(Qw)_j|/sqrt(8), at most
1/2 since (Qw)_j^2<=2. Against new latitude points they are
|z_ij|/sqrt(32), at most 1/2 since z_ij^2<=8. All three coordinate-square
bounds are checked directly. There are 1596 axis points in total.

COUNT AND DISTINCTNESS

The four families have different body squared norm fractions:
caps 2/3, equator 1, axis 0, and new points 1/4. Hence they are disjoint.
Distinctness within the original families is verified. Within the new
families it follows from the norms and the checked joint products:
coinciding points from different families would have product 1.

             589680 + 13710 + 1596 + 4*1352 = 610394.

Equivalently, the change from 605322 is to replace 364 axis points
with 5408 points at the new latitude and 28 coordinate-axis points,
for a net gain of 5072.

THE LEECH MINIMUM USED IN THE PROOF

The verifier reconstructs the binary Golay code C from the supplied
basis. Its weight enumerator is 1+759t^8+2576t^12+759t^16+t^24.
The integer lattice consists of vectors x with common coordinate parity
e, with ((x-e)/2) mod 2 in C and sum(x) congruent to 4e modulo 8.
These conditions are closed under addition, since C is linear and
contains the all-one word.

If e=0 and the Golay residue is nonzero, at least eight coordinates have
magnitude at least 2, giving squared norm >=32. If that residue is zero,
coordinates are multiples of 4; the sum condition forbids a single
coordinate +/-4, again giving minimum >=32. If e=1, all coordinates
are odd. Norm below 32 would force all magnitudes to be one, but then
the Golay weight is divisible by four and sum(x) is 0 modulo 8,
contradicting sum(x)=4 modulo 8. Hence every nonzero vector has norm >=32.

REPRODUCTION AND SCOPE

Run python verify.py. Python and NumPy are required. The program
reconstructs the Golay code and Leech minimal lines; checks all source
hashes, cap classes, head triples, equator pairs, and axis products;
then checks every pair of new families and all cross-family conditions.
Every mathematical check uses integers, with no floating-point tolerance.
Norm bounds keep all NumPy integer calculations well inside int64 range.
The elapsed time is the only floating-point value in verification.json.
