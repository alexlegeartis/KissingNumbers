PARTIAL-FAMILY SEARCH -- 23 OCTOBER 2026

New retained 43D construction: 2,919,743, improving 2,919,686 by 57.
The gain is modest; this pass does not achieve the proposed 3-million target.

The construction keeps all 118 complete families with r3 >= 12 and all five
pure heads. It replaces the 3,351-point tiny layer by 3,408 explicit points.
The first free-insertion screen supplied 15 points; continuous point-head
search raised this to 18. Small shared-deletion searches found 96 exact
profitable trades. Selecting compatible trades and retaining compatible free
insertions gives the final gain of 57. A separate finite packing of the
union of the successful trade points gives the same total.

Exact replay: results_43/verify.py checks 6,232,333 angles with integer
arithmetic. It reconstructs the parent embedding, rank-40 complement, gluing
map, and all 30,240 shortest body vectors. The verifier independently derives
21,825 sharpened body bounds using absent shortest differences. Complete
large-family populations retain the previous modular/population proof and
published parent minimum/design assumptions; they were not freshly enumerated.

This is a constructive lower bound, not an optimality claim or a fresh audit
of every external publication. Preferred local package directory: results_43.

OTHER COMPLETED EXPERIMENTS

43D tiny insertion pool: 1,835 shared head positions plus class-specific
positions, 30,065 class/head choices, 316,677 point/head choices after the
large-family screen. Longer heads than the whole-family radius restriction
were allowed; the retained gain does not depend on a claimed successful
long-head insertion.

43D continuous tiny heads: 10,080 distinct constraint signatures, 145,497 LP
calls, 21 feasible point/head samples. The lifted-polytope search samples
vertices and is not an exhaustive feasibility proof.

43D joint motion of small blocks of individual tiny points: 150 attempts,
no further insertion. No rigidity conclusion follows.

Norm-four body recovery: 1,095,312 explicit vectors obtained from differences
of shortest bodies. Exactly 488 discriminant classes reach their full
495-point population; 1,752 classes remain incomplete in this recovered pool.
All sixty retained 43D medium classes were separately enumerated completely.
Replacing their conservative bounds by actual maxima sharpens 3,408 entries
of the tiny-point bound matrix. Another 18,270 LP calls over the affected
signatures found no further free insertion in the sampled search.

43D partial-medium screen: 620,739 point/head choices survive the large
families; 275,289 also survive the old tiny layer. None is a free insertion
against the retained medium layer. Small exchanges from 191,352 candidates
with at most three old-medium blockers found no profitable sampled trade.

42D: completed medium and tiny layers for 67 alternative hexagon cores whose
saved large-layer incumbents have eighteen large families. The best result
reproduces 1,991,445. This tests those fixed large-layer choices and finite
head pools; it is not a global obstruction for 42D.

45D: 3,108 shared head positions were screened, giving 58,968 point/head
choices after the big-layer check and no free additions. A continuous
eight-minute screen visited 3,358 of 10,080 constraint signatures with
44,774 LP calls and found no insertion. A small-trade screen of 24,477
candidates with at most three blockers also found no profitable trade.

Discriminant reflection audit: reflections outside the E8 Weyl group can
preserve the 43D outer core, but none of these single reflections preserves
all retained large-family inequalities. A finite auxiliary screen for one
such core supplied no improvement. Coalescing cores by their pair-distance
matrix is only a search heuristic, not a proof of equivalence of their full
auxiliary extension problems.
