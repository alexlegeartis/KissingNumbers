A CLEAN DESCRIPTION OF THE NEW POINTS

Let a retained family have body squared norm r/3, head z/(D sqrt(3)), and
population w. Its normalized head contribution is z/sqrt(r D^2 + ||z||^2).
For each integer vector a in pure_directions, insert the point with zero body
coordinates and normalized head a/||a||. Its cross-angle condition is exactly

  2 a.z <= sqrt(||a||^2 (r D^2 + ||z||^2)).

When the left side is positive, the verifier squares this inequality using
integers. It handles pure/pure angles the same way. The eight-coordinate head
vectors lie in the original head span, so they do not increase the dimension.

The preferred 45D extension has 16 directions, all of squared length 80.
Their distinct normalized inner products are -3/5 and 1/5. The maximum
positive squared inner product with the retained configuration is 8/45.

The preferred 46D extension has 27 directions, all of squared length 48.
Their distinct normalized inner products are -1/2 and 1/4. The maximum
positive squared inner product with the retained configuration is 1/6.

Both cross bounds are strictly below 1/4. These extensions have comfortable
angular clearance; they are not floating-point near-misses.

HOW THE PURE EXTENSIONS WERE FOUND

pure_heads.py searches normalized rational directions in the existing head
space. The sampled feasible pools contain 176 directions in 45D and 891 in
46D. Finite packing solves attain 16 and 27 points. canonical_pure_heads.py
then selects the deepest feasible directions and produces the simpler codes
above. The finite optima are not continuous or global upper bounds.

A separate LP-vertex sampler explores nonlattice directions and checks a
rational projection back into the exact head span. It returned the same counts.
Trading some complete tiny families for additional pure points also returned
net gains of 16 and 27, with no further increase. With the larger retained
44D layers fixed, that sampled pure-direction pool was empty.

PARTIAL FAMILIES: ACTUAL POINT EXCHANGES

The next departure was to split tiny families into individual points.
For a candidate tiny point x, the known 30,240 shortest dual-body vectors
identify whether a sharp short difference to a retained larger family actually
exists. If it does not, the relevant doubled-inner-product bound improves by
six integer units. Contacts against retained tiny families are calculated
from their actual coordinates, not the classwise gluing bound.

A refined independent head grid produced these pools of points compatible
with every retained family of body-radius index r3 >= 12:
  44D: 50,433 points, including retained tiny points.
  45D: 157,161 points, including retained tiny points.
  46D: 438,942 points, including retained tiny points.

None can be added individually while all retained tiny points stay fixed.
That fact alone does not exclude exchanges. Actual point-conflict searches
therefore released and reoptimized neighborhoods:
  44D: 9,463 local solves; net gain 0.
  45D: 17,518 local solves; net gain 0.

The refined 46D point pool has been built and screened for free additions,
but its full joint exchange problem has not been optimized. The 45D search
was time-limited to part of its available neighborhood list. The earlier
bipartite matching screen is not a global upper bound when mutually
conflicting new points outnumber old points; the later local solves explicitly
include new/new conflicts. A small matching deficiency is never reported as
a construction on its own.

Files: tiny_point_additions.py, tiny_point_exchange.py, tiny_point_lns.py,
point pools and results. These go beyond the earlier whole-family searches.

NEW 41D BODY MODEL

K41 is the complement of sqrt(3) E7. Its discriminant has 4374 classes.
A finite convolution of the inherited K40 populations gives every body shell
through squared norm six. An independent regrouping of the parent minimal
shell checks the first populations.

There are 126 empty short shells: the gluing estimate 17/6 improves to an
actual squared body minimum of 29/6. This is a stronger construction metric,
not itself a kissing-number improvement.

Unions of independently normalized shells, with no additional head, were
optimized for all 729 norm-six centres. The best reconstructs 1,460,036 in
41D. The one-head 42D experiments reached 1,989,960, below our retained bound.
The strongest sampled pool was closed by clique-compressed optimization.
Pentagonal, square and triangular two-head experiments in 43D were also tried;
they did not improve the retained 43D figure. Some larger planar pools were
skipped rather than treated as solved.

Files: body41_geometry.py, body41_spherical.py, body41_line.py,
body41_line_opt.py, body41_planar.py and their data/results.

NEW 45D BODY MODEL FOR 47–48D

K45 is the complement of sqrt(3) A3. It has 108 discriminant classes. All body
populations through squared norm six were obtained by enumerating a sufficient
finite E8 lift ball, then convolving with the K40 table. The ball contains
2,131,921 vectors; it is reproducible from e8_ball.cpp and is omitted from the
archive. No empty first shells occur in this model.

For 47D, four-largest-family rhombi and independent placements of the other
families were explored. Eleven sampled structural models were solved. The
best body arrangement, followed by a pure-head completion, reconstructs
23,766,960. It does not improve that number.

The full table also exposes second-shell families: there are 18 possible
largest-radius classes, rather than just the eight visible in the parent
minimal shell. However, the necessary pairwise compatibility graph has maximum
clique size eight, with two maximizing class sets. Thus simply counting all
18 as simultaneous families is invalid. This check is a necessary obstruction,
not a full optimization of the new body model.

Files: body45_geometry.py, body45_planar.py, repair_planar_pure.py,
body45_large_cliques.py and their tables/candidates.

A GLOBAL CEILING FOR ONE OLD 48D SEARCH MODEL

The old K46-plus-two-head complete-family model cannot beat 52,416,000 under
its squared-norm-at-most-six and gluing-distance rules. This is broader than
the earlier tests which kept particular largest families fixed.

For any repeated body shell of squared radius R > 3, each normalized copy
has projection at least sqrt(R/6) on the direction of a fixed body point.
The pair-angle inequality and the squared norm of the sum give

  multiplicity <= floor(6 / (2R - 6)).

The three available norm-six body shells can contribute at most one family:
heads must vanish and different centre classes conflict under the model's
body-distance rule. The largest centre population is 12,309,600. Applying the
multiplicity bound to all remaining body classes and adding at most six pure
head points gives exactly 52,416,000, attained by the parent construction.

This is NOT an upper bound on the 48D kissing number. It does not cover partial
families, different body lattices, larger norms with independently checked
internal contacts, or arbitrary point configurations. Its practical value is
to avoid spending more search time on an already bounded model.

File: a2_capacity_ceiling.py / a2_capacity_ceiling.json.

WHAT REMAINS OPEN AND WHERE TO PUSH NEXT

The new +16 and +27 extensions show that the previous searches had not even
exhausted useful additions to the retained 45D/46D configurations. They are
modest improvements, not the large jump we are seeking.

The most concrete next structural attack is to split the 9180-point and
roughly 116,000-point bodies by one extra body coordinate, then allow those
pieces to move separately. This changes which points must be sacrificed in a
trade, rather than repeatedly paying for a complete large family. The current
actual-point machinery implements this only for the tiny bodies.

For 47D/48D, useful further departures include other parent lattices or normal
embeddings, and cross-shell clusters with actual shared deletion costs. Such
candidates must be constructed explicitly; neither a class-capacity gap nor a
matching deficiency is evidence of a larger kissing configuration.
