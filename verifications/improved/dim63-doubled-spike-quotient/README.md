## Tiles and actual geometry

Order the six binary coordinates by their ordinary numeric bit labels. Cubic monomials are indexed by increasing weight-3 masks; quadratic coefficients by increasing weight-2 masks. The unchanged two marks are sites **0 and 20**. Use the same omitted mark bits **(0,1)** as the old spike family (and therefore the same old sparse rephasing). For a retained source sign word delete both marks, give each ordinary coordinate amplitude one except at its assigned spike site, where the signed amplitude is four; its physical head is zero. Squared norm is 77, and the original sign at each site is unchanged.

The 1,024 alternating quadratic charts DG are rebuilt from the same GF(32) generator as before, not presumed to form a linear subspace. In the coefficient group F₂¹⁵, the **translation stabilizer K** of this nonlinear 1,024-set is a 32-element linear subspace. The incremental checker explicitly reconstructs all stabilizers: because 0 belongs to DG, every stabilizer belongs to DG, so testing all 1,024 possibilities is complete. Work modulo K, leaving 10 binary dimensions. Choosing one cubic and one quadratic coset of K defines a tile of **32×32=1,024 sign words**: 32 quadratic parts times the 32 affine shifts fixing both marks to (0,1). Thus every tile has the same count and no two distinct tiles can contain the same word.

The 80 literal mother cubics are ten disjoint translates of a subgroup T of order 8. Use one group per site. Contracting the quadratic stabilizer and the affine choices reduces the per-site problem to **F₂¹³ = T × (F₂¹⁵ / K)**. This is an exact graph contraction, not an estimate of degrees. For two distinct codewords at the same spike site j, the only potentially dangerous full Hamming distance is **16 with agreement at j**: with disagreement its raw dot is 62−32−15=15, and with distance ≥20 the worst raw dot is 62−40+15=37, below 77/2. When marked bits both agree, an offending binary difference is therefore exactly a weight-16 word with zero at 0,20,j. For every cubic in T, the verifier enumerates all 2¹⁵ quadratics and the 32 affine completions vanishing at both marks, and keeps the literal weight-16 words. Including zero cubic, the exact counts are [1457,248,272,248,272,248,272,248] in the source subgroup order. Project their quadratic labels modulo K and retain the ones with zero at j. This gives a forbidden set of 1,546–1,627 13-bit masks for each site, never including zero.

At every ordinary site the witness gives a 6-dimensional subgroup of F₂¹³ avoiding its forbidden set. Its 64 members are translated jointly into its indicated macro-group. The translate does not change any intra-site differences, and all **3,968 actual owner tiles are disjoint** across the 62 sites. The checker expands the 62 subgroup bases and verifies all these facts independently. A 64-tile selector therefore gives exactly 65,536 weighted sign words per site.

## Pair angles and ledger

At different sites the true full distance is ≥16 because the owner tiles are disjoint; marked bits agree, so body distance is ≥16 as well. The two changed amplitudes add at most 3+3 to the unweighted bound 30, giving 36≤77/2. The previous proof's identical physical profile (same (0,1) prefix, spike amplitude four, zero tail) applies unchanged to every other retained family, since those bounds use only the mother distance, the actual sparse parity guard and the coordinates' absolute amplitudes, not the old quadratic subset. Explicit bounds:
* old dense (mark bits equal): squared norm 64, body distance ≥15, raw dot ≤35;
* old unmarked 16-site sparse: norm squared 16, body parity opposite to (0,1) sign words ⇒ raw dot ≤17; old one-mark sparse similarly gives ≤16;
* old affine planes, norm squared 4: raw dot ≤7 if ordinary-only, and ≤5 for two ordinary pins plus tail;
* old ordinary and tail poles: raw dot ≤4 and zero, respectively.
All gates are squared and checked with integers. Distinct normalized points are automatic from the strict separation upper threshold of 1/2. No new/old spike check is needed: old spikes are **replaced**, not retained. The ledger is `232191236 − 2031616 + 62×64×1024 = 234222852`, with no deletions outside the superseded spike layer.
