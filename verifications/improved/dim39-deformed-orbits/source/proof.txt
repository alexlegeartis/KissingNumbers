A KISSING CONFIGURATION OF 769180 POINTS IN DIMENSION 39
26 October 2026

RESULT AND STATUS

The explicit data and exact verifier in this directory establish

                            tau_39 >= 769180.

The count is 264 above Qihong Lin's public bound 768916, and
248 above our preceding local bound 768932. This is a computationally
certified construction; external review or acceptance is not asserted.
All geometric comparisons in the verifier are integer inequalities.
Floating-point optimization was used only to discover the coordinates.

CONSTRUCTION

Take the following sparse vectors, each divided by sqrt(32):

1. On each of 3424 eight-coordinate supports, take all 128 vectors with
   coordinates +/-2 and an even number of negative signs: 438272 points.
   The supplied source/supports_3424.txt lists the initial supports.
   Apply the four replacements in base_certificate.json.
2. Take +/-4 e_i +/-4 e_j on every coordinate pair except the fourteen
   center-leaf pairs excluded in base_certificate.json: 2908 points.
3. For each of the four heavy stars (a,B) in base_certificate.json, take
   +/-4 e_a + 2 sum_(b in B) s_b e_b with even negative leaf parity:
   64 points, with the center sign independent.

There are 441244 sparse points altogether. The retained sparse layer is
the one from our previous 768924-point construction. Its separate proof
is in base_proof.txt, and verify_base.py checks it from the source data.
The old binary dense layer is completely replaced in the present result.

Let G be the explicitly listed group_words in orbit_certificate.json.
Addition in G is bitwise XOR; bit j specifies coordinate j, starting at 0.
The verifier proves that G is a binary linear group of order 256.
For each of the 1281 integer rows V in representatives and every g in G,
include the exact unit vector

                  x(V,g)_j = (-1)^g_j V_j / sqrt(sum_k V_k^2).

Thus the new dense layer has 1281 * 256 = 327936 points, once distinctness
is established below. These normalized integer vectors are the exact
construction, not decimal approximations to unit vectors.

SPARSE-SPARSE PAIRS

Every sparse vector has squared norm 32. The verifier checks that distinct
eight-supports intersect in at most four places, by ruling out repeated
five-subsets. This gives products at most 16 between different supports.
Even sign parity gives the same bound within one support. Root-root and
root-middle products are at most 16 directly from their shapes.
The 64 heavy vectors are generated explicitly and checked against each
other, every retained root pair and every middle support. For a middle
support, the maximum over its 128 even sign patterns is evaluated exactly.
All these products are at most 16. Dividing by 32 proves the angle bound.
Their different support and magnitude patterns also prove distinctness.

DENSE-SPARSE PAIRS

For every supplied row V the verifier proves the integer norm bounds

                   99996^2 <= sum_j V_j^2 <= 100004^2.

Write L=99996. For each actual middle support S, it verifies

                       (sum_(j in S) |V_j|)^2 <= 2 L^2.

The absolute unnormalized product with any signed middle vector is at
most 2 sum_(j in S)|V_j|. After division by sqrt(32)||V||, it is at most
1/2. Coordinate sign changes from G do not affect this bound.

For the sum R of the two largest coordinate magnitudes, it checks
2 R^2 <= L^2. This similarly proves compatibility with all signed roots,
using unnormalized product bound 4R and root norm sqrt(32).

For each actual heavy star (a,B), it checks

                 (2|V_a| + sum_(j in B)|V_j|)^2 <= 2 L^2.

The unnormalized product bound is twice the expression in parentheses,
so every heavy vector is also compatible with every dense vector.
For certificates using the stronger top-eight-coordinate bound, that
bound implies every actual-support inequality above.

DENSE-DENSE PAIRS: COMPLETE GROUP ENUMERATION

For two representatives V,W and group elements g,h, the unnormalized
product of their signed versions equals

                       sum_j (-1)^(g_j+h_j) V_j W_j.

Since g+h ranges over G, it suffices to test every relative element
t in G for each pair of representatives V,W. When V=W only t=0 is
excluded, as that denotes the same indexed point. All other cases are
checked, including possible duplicate or stabilized orbit points.

The verifier obtains a binary basis b_0,...,b_(m-1) of G and enumerates
the entire span. This is checked against the supplied group, so no
closure or group-size claim is assumed. At coordinate j define the
character c_j whose k-th bit is (b_k)_j. Form the integer array

                       F[c] = sum_(j: c_j=c) V_j W_j.

Its Walsh transform at index u is

                 sum_c (-1)^<u,c> F[c]
                    = sum_j (-1)^<u,c_j> V_j W_j,

which is exactly the desired product for t=sum_k u_k b_k. The transform
is computed by the elementary butterfly (a,b) -> (a+b,a-b), stage by
stage. This formula follows by splitting the sum according to each bit
of c. Selected pairs are also checked by direct dot products against all
group elements, independently of the transform implementation.

The program evaluates all 210205695
required relative products. The largest is

                            4997124511.

It satisfies the exact inequality

                     2 * 4997124511 <= 99996^2.

Every normalized product is consequently at most 1/2: positive raw
products are divided by ||V|| ||W|| >= L^2, while nonpositive products
already satisfy the requirement. There is no distance cutoff or
perturbation estimate in this complete verification.

Each coefficient has absolute value at most 100000. Norm sums and every
partial Walsh sum have magnitude at most 39*100000^2. Sparse sums before
squaring are at most 8*100000. The explicit safety inequalities checked
by the program place all these operations within signed 64-bit range.
Thus NumPy integer arithmetic is exact for every mathematical check.

DISTINCTNESS, COUNT, AND KISSING SPHERES

Any repeated indexed dense point would have product 1 with itself under
two distinct indices, contradicting the checked upper bound 1/2.
The dense-sparse inequalities likewise exclude equality of those points.
Therefore all counted points are distinct, and the total is

                       441244 + 1281*256 = 769180.

For unit vectors x,y with x.y <= 1/2, ||x-y|| >= 1. Placing unit spheres
at centers 2x makes their interiors disjoint and makes every one tangent
to the unit sphere centered at the origin. This proves the kissing bound.

Run python verify.py from this directory, or pass its full path.
Python and NumPy are required. No optimizer, GPU, saved distance table,
network access or floating-point tolerance is used by the verifier.
Run without Python's -O option, because the inherited sparse verifier
uses assertions. The top-level verifier explicitly rejects -O.
