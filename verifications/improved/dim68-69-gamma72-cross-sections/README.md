# Dimensions 68 and 69 — cross-sections of Γ₇₂, with the Grams exhibited

| dim | k | Gram of the k-tuple | det | Hermite floor | certified | floor | factor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 69 | 3 | `A₃`: diagonal 8, two edges −4 | 256 | 256 ✓ | **627 822 180** | 331 737 984 | 1.89 |
| 68 | 4 | scaled `D₄`, exact vertex | 1024 | 1024 ✓ | **361 275 480** | 331 737 984 | 1.09 |

Both Grams are at the **Hermite floor** for their rank, so neither can be improved by a
different Gram — only by a larger *k*. That is the subject of the two sections below.

```
python scripts/leech_truth.py      # 2 s:  the LP is TIGHT on the Leech through k = 4
python scripts/k4_gram_min_det.py  # 2 min: no four-tuple beats D_4
python scripts/scan_k3.py          # 1 min: all 16 realizable det-256 three-point Grams
python scripts/scan_k4.py          # 50 min: all 104 realizable det-1024 four-point Grams
python scripts/verify69.py         # derives the A_3 triple and checks it exactly
python scripts/verify68.py         # derives the winning four-tuple and checks it exactly
python scripts/presentation_invariance.py   # 80 s: the basis costs nothing, measured
python scripts/recheck_cert.py     # 40 s: the 68, 69 and 70 certificates, by a second
                                   #        arithmetic path (70 lives next door, but the
                                   #        engine and the rewriting are the same)
python scripts/k4d4.py             # the four-point LP, exact integer certificate
python scripts/k4.py               # the same LP validated on the Leech, where the answers are known
```

`realize_k3.py` and `d4gamma.py` are the SEARCHES that found the original configurations. They
need a pool of Γ₇₂ minimal vectors — 488 MB — which is not shipped, so they do not run here;
what they found is shipped (`data/gamma72_triple_k3.npy`, `data/gamma72_d4_k4.npy`) and is
checked from scratch by the verifiers above, which need nothing but the Gram.

Dimension 69 no longer depends on that search at all. The winning A₃ triple is *derived*:
`verify69.py` rebuilds the 24 minimal vectors of the scaled D₄ sublattice generated by
`gamma72_d4_k4.npy` — which `verify68.py` checks independently — and exhibits the triple among
them. `data/gamma72_triple_k3.npy` is kept only as the record of the superseded det-352 Gram.

## What this fixes

**Dimension 69 was previously withdrawn** ([`../../closed/dim69-gamma72-three-point/`](../../closed/dim69-gamma72-three-point/)),
and that directory called it *"the one place in the project where a large, tempting number had
to be thrown away."* It does not have to be.

The *k*-point moment LP certifies, for a **fixed** Gram *G* of minimal vectors `u_1..u_k`, a
lower bound on

    n[0..0] = #{ v minimal : <u_i, v> = 0 for every i },

and those *v* lie in a (72−k)-dimensional subspace, pairwise at ≥ 60°, so they are a kissing
configuration of ℝ^(72−k). **The bound is valid for any actual k-tuple with that Gram.**

The obstacle was never the bound — it was realizability. `dim70-71` could only establish it
*through the LP itself* (Lemma 4 of [`../../../common/PROOF-kpoint.md`](../../../common/PROOF-kpoint.md):
a Gram is realizable if the witnessing lower-order cell has strictly positive LP minimum), and
for every three-point Gram except the pairwise-orthogonal one that cell has minimum **0**. So
the best *provable* Gram gave only 149 415 783, below the floor.

But realizability is a fact about the lattice, and Γ₇₂ is available in coordinates
(`../dim73-95-gamma72-caps/data/gamma72_gram.npy`, plus a pool of its minimal vectors).
Inner products are `x G yᵀ`, so a k-tuple with any prescribed Gram can simply be **exhibited**.
`realize_k3.py` finds one for every Gram in the table — and for the winning A₃ Gram nothing
needs to be searched at all, since A₃ ⊆ D₄ puts the triple inside the four-tuple that
`verify68.py` already checks (see *The Gram is forced* below):

| Gram | det | LP certificate | realizable | beats the floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(−4,−4,0) = A₃** | **256** | **627 822 180** | **exhibited** | **yes** |
| (4,−2,0) | 352 | 527 597 644 | exhibited | yes |
| (4,−1,0) | 376 | 500 027 708 | exhibited | yes |
| (4, 0,0) | 384 | 469 123 586 | exhibited | yes |
| (3, 0,0) | 440 | 409 062 220 | exhibited | yes |
| (2, 0,0) | 480 | 346 628 061 | exhibited | yes |
| (1, 0,0) | 504 | 245 233 175 | exhibited | no |
| (0, 0,0) | 512 | 149 415 783 | exhibited | no |

## The exact optimum, not a rounding of it

`run3` proposes a dual with a floating-point solve, rounds it to rationals and repairs it into
exact feasibility by lowering one coordinate. That is sound — an imprecise dual weakens the
bound and cannot inflate it — but it is **lossy**, and how much it loses depends on the
conditioning, which is why the 104 bases gave 98 different answers.

[`../../../common/exact_vertex.py`](../../../common/exact_vertex.py) removes both problems.

1. **Reduce.** Most rows are identifications rather than equations: `n[c] = n[-c]` from the
   antipodal symmetry, and translations reading `n[c] = n[c']`, `n[c] = 0` or `n[c] = 1`.
   Substituting them out is exact — it leaves the feasible set alone — and takes *k* = 4 from
   **3473 × 1681 to 617 × 520**. Every HiGHS method and presolve setting then returns the same
   optimum, where before they disagreed by 0.16% and returned points violating the constraints
   by several whole vectors.
2. **Take the vertex.** Complementary slackness makes the optimal dual tight exactly on the
   active columns, so it solves a rational linear system. The active set is read off the
   numeric dual, the system solved in `Fraction`s, and `Aᵀy ≤ c` then **verified over every
   column** in exact arithmetic. A wrong active set fails that check and nothing is returned.

    τ(68) ≥ 361 275 480      (1681 cells → 520 variables, 453 active columns, ~5 min)

This is the **optimum**, not merely a better witness: the numeric primal attains the same
value, so no dual improves on it, and dimension 68 cannot move again without a different
programme. Four determinant-1024 presentations were run — including the one whose repaired
certificate was the worst of the 104, 358 774 871 — and all four give exactly 361 275 480.

Validated against what is already known: τ(70) = 1 249 778 250 and τ(69) = 627 822 180 to the
unit, and the Leech's 60° two-point cross-section **49 896**, which
[`scripts/leech_truth.py`](scripts/leech_truth.py) *counts* outright rather than certifying.
`run3` is untouched; `build_system` was lifted out of it so the new certifier works on the
same rows rather than on a reimplementation of them.

## The Gram is forced

The certificate falls as the **determinant of the Gram** rises — read the *k* = 3 table above
downwards: 352 → 527 597 644, 376 → 500 027 708, …, 512 → 149 415 783. A more degenerate tuple
constrains the cells harder. So "which *k*-tuple?" is really "how small can det be?", and that
has an exact answer.

At a *fixed* determinant the basis costs nothing.
[`scripts/presentation_invariance.py`](scripts/presentation_invariance.py) measures it: the
sixteen realizable determinant-256 Grams are sixteen bases of one scaled A₃, the default
solver path certifies **thirteen different values** for them, and all sixteen collapse to
**627 822 180** once more than the first dual is kept. The spread `scan_k3.py` and
`scan_k4.py` report — 0.7% across the 104 determinant-1024 Grams — is the quality of the
numeric dual, not a property of the Gram. Each of those numbers is a valid lower bound
regardless, since the certifier repairs any dual into exact feasibility before using it.

The *k* vectors generate a rank-*k* sublattice `L ⊆ Γ₇₂`, so every nonzero vector of `L` has
norm ≥ 8, and `det(Gram) = det(L)`. Hermite's constant `γ_k` gives `min(L) ≤ γ_k·det(L)^(1/k)`,
so

    det(L) ≥ (8/γ_k)^k ,

attained exactly by the densest rank-*k* lattice. For Γ₇₂ that is

| k | densest | γ_k | det ≥ | this package uses | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | A₂ | (4/3)^(1/2) | 48 | 48 — the 60° pair, dim 70 | ✓ |
| 3 | A₃ | 2^(1/3) | 256 | 256 — dim 69 | ✓ |
| 4 | D₄ | 2^(1/2) | 1024 | 1024 — dim 68 | ✓ |
| 5 | D₅ | 8^(1/5) | 4096 | — | |

Dimension 69 sat at determinant 352 (×1.38 above the floor) until this was noticed; moving to
the attaining A₃ Gram gained **+100 224 536**. [`scripts/scan_k3.py`](scripts/scan_k3.py) runs
all sixteen realizable determinant-256 Grams — every one of them beats 527 597 644 — and it is
**exhaustive**: enumerating every integer Gram with diagonal 8, off-diagonal in `[−4,4]`,
determinant 256 and sublattice minimum 8 produces exactly those sixteen, which the script
asserts. So 627 822 180 is the best this LP can certify at *k* = 3, for the optimal lattice,
over every basis of it. Likewise
[`scripts/k4_gram_min_det.py`](scripts/k4_gram_min_det.py) confirms the *k* = 4 floor by brute
force over all 339 393 integer Grams a four-tuple of minimal lines could have, and finds exactly
104 attaining it — all 104 realizable among the 24 roots, so
[`scripts/scan_k4.py`](scripts/scan_k4.py) is exhaustive too, up to the 6 that hit the solver
budget.

The same rule is visible everywhere the project uses a cross-section, which is a good check on
it: on the Leech, A₃ at det 32 gives 27 720 = Λ₂₁ and the orthogonal triple at det 64 gives
19 530; D₄ at det 64 gives 17 400 = Λ₂₀ and A₄ at det 80 gives 15 540. On P₄₈, A₃ at det 108
gives 6 687 320 and the det-138 Gram gives 5 915 140. Lower determinant wins every time.

## Dimension 68

Once realizability is settled by exhibition, `k = 4` becomes usable. Which Gram to aim for is
decided on the Leech, where the four-point LP is **exact**:

| Leech, k = 4 | certified | true |
|---|---|---|
| `D₄` | 17 400 | 17 400 = Λ₂₀ |
| `A₄` | 15 540 | 15 540 |

Those "true" values are **counted, not quoted**:
[`scripts/leech_truth.py`](scripts/leech_truth.py) builds all 196 560 minimal vectors of the
Leech lattice from the Golay code, finds twelve independent tuples of each Gram, and counts the
minimal vectors orthogonal to them. It also reproduces the marginals `93 150 / 47 104 / 4 600`
that the LP takes as input. On all six Leech cases — `k = 2, 3, 4` — the LP certificate equals
an attained count, so the *k*-point LP with lattice-range constraints is **tight on the Leech
through k = 4**. (An earlier comment in the engine recorded 19 962 for three orthogonal
vectors; the true count is **19 530**, and the LP was right.)

`D₄` wins, and by *The Gram is forced* above it is the only candidate: `γ₄ = √2` is attained
only by D₄, so det ≥ 1024 with equality exactly there, and A₄'s 1280 loses before any LP is
run. [`scripts/k4_gram_min_det.py`](scripts/k4_gram_min_det.py) checks the floor by brute force
over all 339 393 integer Grams a four-tuple of Γ₇₂ minimal lines could have (diagonal 8,
off-diagonal in `[−4,4]`, positive definite, sublattice minimum ≥ 8): the smallest determinant
is 1024, and all 104 Grams attaining it have D₄'s vector counts. The Leech counts agree.

Scaled to Γ₇₂ the D₄ Gram is diagonal 8 with three edges −4, determinant `4⁴·4 = 1024`, and
`d4gamma.py` exhibits a four-tuple of Γ₇₂ minimal vectors realising it exactly.

The *lattice* is forced, but the **basis is not**. The certificate comes from a numeric dual and
is basis-dependent even among isometric Grams: across all 104 determinant-1024 Grams that occur
among four-tuples of the 24 roots of that sublattice, it ranges over **358 774 871 to
361 275 383** — a 0.7% spread for one and the same sublattice.
[`scripts/scan_k4.py`](scripts/scan_k4.py) runs all 104 and records every value in
`data/scan_k4.json` (3.6 KB); 98 certified and 6 returned nothing.

Those six are not an artefact of impatience. All 104 programmes have **exactly the same size** —
1681 cells, 3473 rows — so the construction cost is identical; the six are simply linear
programmes HiGHS does not solve. They failed at a 900 s per-solve limit in the scan and one was
still running at 1740 s of a 4000 s limit, returning `status = 1`, time limit reached. The engine
degrades cleanly rather than hanging (verified: a 60 s limit produces a 60 s solve and a `None`
result at 77 s), so this is a gap in coverage, not a defect. What it could be worth is bounded:
the 98 values that did certify span 358 774 871 to 361 275 383, the claim is the top of that
range, and a seventh value falling outside a 0.7% band would be surprising. A dual from a lower
design degree would certify them cheaply — any feasible dual gives a valid bound — but at
tdes = 9 this Gram family certifies around 156 million, so those numbers would be far weaker and
could not move the claim — and since the exact vertex settles the programme outright
(below), those six cost nothing at all. The best of the 98 repaired certificates is
`n[0,0,0,0] ≥ 361 275 383` (1681 cells), for the Gram

    [[8, 0,-4, 4], [0, 8,-4, 4], [-4,-4, 8,-4], [4, 4,-4, 8]]

exhibited by [`scripts/verify68.py`](scripts/verify68.py) among those 24 roots. The textbook D₄
pattern gives 361 184 461.

## What the floor 331 737 984 really is, and why dimension 68 is the most exposed claim here

The floor quoted above is Edel--Rains--Sloane's **dimension-64** total, inherited by
monotonicity. That is not the same thing as the value their construction takes **in dimension
68**, and the difference matters here because the margin is only 9%.

Their count is a sum over a chain of support sizes,

    N(n) = sum_nu  A(n, n_nu, n_nu) * A(n_nu, ceil(n_nu/4)),   n >= n_0 >= 4 n_1 >= 16 n_2 ...

and at `n = 64` the chain `(64, 16, 4, 1)` gives `2^28 + 30828*2048 + 10416*16 + 128 =
331 737 984`, reproducing their published figure to the digit. In dimension 68 the same chain
has four more coordinates to spend, so

| n | level 0 | level 1, `A(n,16,16)*2048` | level 2 | level 3 | ERS(n) >= |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 268 435 456 | 63 135 744 | 166 656 | 128 | **331 737 984** |
| 68 | 268 435 456 | 63 135 744 | 200 464 | 136 | **331 771 800** |

using `A(68,16,16) >= A(64,16,16) = 30828`, which is all that is citable, and
`A(68,4,4) = C(68,3)/4 = 12529` exactly, since `68 = 2 mod 6` so an `S(3,4,68)` exists. The
claim clears that by a factor **1.089**, so it is an improvement — but only just, and the
margin rests on one unpublished number:

> **Dimension 68 falls if anyone exhibits a constant-weight code with `A(68,16,16) >= 45 235`.**

The best in hand is ERS's own 30 828, monotone in `n` from their length-64 extended cyclic
code. A factor **1.47** would sink the claim, and that is not a comfortable factor: the
Gilbert--Varshamov and Johnson growth rates from 64 to 68 are 1.67 and 1.79, and ERS's own
code beats the generic grid map at the same parameters by 1.88. Nobody has published an
`A(68,16,16)`, which is what protects the claim; the exposure is to a referee with a better
code, not to the literature as it stands.

[`../../closed/dim96-ers-takeover/ers_exposure.py`](../../closed/dim96-ers-takeover/ers_exposure.py)
runs this computation in **every** claimed dimension and ranks them. Dimension 68 comes out
most exposed at 1.47; the next is dimension 95 at 3.9 and everything else is above 5. Nothing
about dimensions 69, 70 and 71 is affected — they clear the same floor by 1.89, 3.77 and 7.85,
and dimensions 70 and 71 additionally clear a *generous* upper bound on ERS in their own
dimension (563 125 646, handing ERS the length-80 constant-weight code they have no right to
at length 71), which dimension 68 does not.

## The engine

[`../../../common/kpoint_lp.py`](../../../common/kpoint_lp.py) is the LP with **every** exponent vector of even total degree, 161
equations at k = 3 rather than 56. The working tree's `dim69-71` had been importing an earlier
revision that imposes only the all-even-exponent moments; that is a relaxation, so its optimum
is a valid but weak lower bound — it returns 1 170 823 530 for dimension 70 and 126 630 658 for
dimension 69, which is exactly where the stale numbers in that package came from.

## How much room the programme leaves

The claim is the **minimum** the linear programme certifies. Running the same programme as a
**maximisation** says how large that cross-section could possibly be, so the difference is
everything a better method could win without changing the Gram at all:

| dim | k | claimed (LP minimum) | LP maximum | room |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 71 | 1 | 2 603 658 750 | 2 603 658 750 | **0** — the one-point system has a unique solution |
| 70 | 2 | 1 249 778 250 | 1 250 506 440 | +728 190, **0.058%** |
| 69 | 3 | 627 822 180 | 663 905 373 | +36 083 193, **5.75%** |
| 68 | 4 | 361 275 480 | 406 583 317 | +45 307 837, **12.5%** |

So dimension 71 is not a bound but a count, dimension 70 is within a sixteenth of a per cent
of one, and the room opens up with *k* exactly as the number of unpinned cells does. Closing
it needs a stronger input, not a better Gram — the natural one is the Siegel theta series of
degree 3 or 4, whose Fourier coefficients count *k*-tuples with a prescribed Gram and would
replace these brackets by exact values; see
[`../../../common/PROOF-kpoint.md`](../../../common/PROOF-kpoint.md) §8. Ozeki's Theorem 6.2
shows every degree-4 coefficient carries one global unknown bounded only within a factor
278/184, so the programme is doing real work at *k* = 3 and the brackets above are not an
artefact of laziness.

**The Gram sets are exhaustive and the maxima are taken over them.** At *k* = 3,
[`scripts/scan_k3.py`](scripts/scan_k3.py) runs all sixteen realizable determinant-256 Grams
in two seconds each and 627 822 180 is attained by five of the sixteen bases, the weakest
being 625 961 766 — a 0.30% spread for one and the same sublattice. At *k* = 4, 98 of the 104
certify and span 358 774 871 to 361 275 383, a 0.70% spread; the six HiGHS does not solve are
recorded as `null` in `data/scan_k4.json` and are the one gap in coverage.

## Where the chain stops

Dimensions 65–67 would need *k* = 5, 6, 7. With every Gram now at its Hermite floor the chain
is clean enough to extrapolate honestly:

| k | dim | det | certified | ratio to previous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 71 | 8 | 2 603 658 750 | — |
| 2 | 70 | 48 | 1 249 778 250 | 0.480 |
| 3 | 69 | 256 | 627 822 180 | 0.502 |
| 4 | 68 | 1024 | 361 275 480 | 0.575 |
| 5 | 67 | 4096 | ? | ~0.6 ⟹ ≈ 215 M |

Beating the floor of 331 737 984 at *k* = 5 would need a ratio of 0.92, against a trend running
at 0.5–0.6, so dimensions 65–67 keep their inherited values and **dimension 68 is where this
chain ends**. The cost would also be steep: the cell enumeration is `11^k` candidates against
`7^k` lattice-range vectors, so *k* = 5 is some 10³ times the *k* = 4 work and would need the
construction rewritten in array form rather than in `Fraction`s.

(The earlier version of this section extrapolated from ratios 0.48, 0.42, 0.68 — the 0.42 was
the det-352 three-point Gram, and its being out of line was the clue that the Gram, not the
LP, was what needed fixing.)
