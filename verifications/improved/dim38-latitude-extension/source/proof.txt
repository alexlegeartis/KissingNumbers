A KISSING CONFIGURATION OF 605346 POINTS IN DIMENSION 38
25 October 2026

THE RESULT

The supplied data and exact verifier establish

                         tau_38 >= 605346.

This exceeds the 605322-point configuration of Qihong Lin by 24 points.
The new points use a different latitude from the original caps. A rational
rotation of the axis configuration fixes their head vectors and allows
all 1932 axis points to remain. No original cap or equatorial point is
deleted. External publication or acceptance is not claimed.

THE EXISTING CAP AND EQUATOR DATA

Use the integer model of the Leech lattice with minimum squared norm 32.
Its 196560 minimal vectors give 98280 antipodal lines. Choose each line's
representative u with first nonzero coordinate positive, and order these
representatives lexicographically.

The supplied class_labels partition these lines into 644 classes. Within
each class, distinct representatives obey |u.u'| <= 8. The supplied set W
contains 1932 distinct vectors in R^14 of squared norm 8 and pairwise inner
product at most 4. It is partitioned into 644 triples of sum zero, so the
three vectors in each triple have pairwise inner product -4.

For each body line u in class c and each w in the corresponding triple,
include

                   (+/-u, sqrt(2) w) / sqrt(48).

There are 6*98280=589680 such caps. Distinct body lines have absolute
inner product at most 16. In the same class the stronger bound 8 applies.
These bounds and the head bounds prove cap-cap inner products <=1/2:
different classes give at most 16+2*4=24 before division by 48;
the same class gives at most 8+2*8=24. The same body and different heads
give 32+2*(-4)=24. Opposite body signs are also compatible.

Lin's 6855 equatorial line representatives q have squared norm 48,
belong to the same Leech lattice, and obey |q.q'|<=24. Include both signs
of (q,0)/sqrt(48), for 13710 equatorial points. For a minimal vector u,
q +/- u are nonzero lattice vectors, hence have squared norm at least 32.
Consequently |q.u|<=24, proving equator-cap compatibility.

THE SIX NEW HEAD LINES

certificate.json supplies six integer vectors z_i in R^14 satisfying

       |z_i|^2=24,       |z_i.z_j|<=12 for i!=j,
       |z_i.w|<=8 for every w in W.

They were found in a scaled D4 root configuration generated by four
orthogonal norm-six holes of W. This discovery description is not an
assumption in the proof: the verifier checks the actual six vectors.

Assign six distinct Leech body lines u_i, with canonical indices

                       440, 237, 1268, 600, 1343, 1628.

For each i, the three heads w in u_i's existing class additionally obey

                              |z_i.w| <= 2.

Include the four independent sign choices

                       (+/-u_i, +/-2 z_i) / sqrt(128).

These give 24 new points. Their norm is one because 32+4*24=128.
Different new families have unnormalized inner product at most
16+4*12=64. Within one family, distinct sign choices give inner products
64, -64, or -128. Thus all new-new products are at most 1/2.

Against an old cap with a different body line, the numerator is at most
16+16 sqrt(2). The denominator is sqrt(128*48)=32 sqrt(6), and

                     16+16 sqrt(2) <= 16 sqrt(6).

Against the same body line, the assigned-class condition gives instead

                      32+4 sqrt(2) <= 16 sqrt(6).

Both inequalities are checked by integer squaring in the verifier.
They establish compatibility with every old cap, including both body
signs. The new body's squared norm fraction is 1/4, so Cauchy-Schwarz
gives compatibility with every unit vector in the equator automatically.

THE RATIONAL AXIS ROTATION

The certificate gives an integer matrix N and positive integer D such
that

                       NN^T=D^2 I,       Nz_i=D z_i.

Thus Q=N/D is orthogonal and fixes all six new heads. Include all 1932
axis points (0,Qw)/sqrt(8), w in W. Axis-axis compatibility is inherited
from W. Axis-equator products vanish.

The finite certificate also proves, for every w,w' in W,

                              w.Qw' <= sqrt(48).

This is exactly the axis-cap condition. The inequality is verified
without floating point as follows. An integer matrix T and M=2^24 obey

                  2 |M N_ij - D T_ij| <= D.

Every w in W has l1 norm at most 8. Therefore

               w.Qw' <= (w.Tw' + 32)/M.

All 1932^2 integer products are checked. The largest certified upper
numerator is 115984315; its square is at most 48 M^2. The verifier checks
explicit bounds preventing overflow in all NumPy integer operations.
Orthogonality and the fixed-head identities use arbitrary-size integers.

For a new point and an axis point, the inner product is

                          +/- z_i.Qw / 16.

Since Q fixes z_i and is orthogonal, z_i.Qw=z_i.w, whose absolute value
is at most 8. This proves the remaining compatibility condition.

COUNT AND DISTINCTNESS

The four families have distinct body squared norm fractions:
caps 2/3, equator 1, axis 0, and new points 1/4. They are disjoint.
Distinctness within each family is checked or follows from the distinct
body lines, head vectors and sign choices described above. The total is

                  589680 + 13710 + 1932 + 24 = 605346.

THE LEECH MINIMUM USED IN THE PROOF

The verifier reconstructs the binary Golay code C from the supplied
basis. Its weight enumerator is 1+759t^8+2576t^12+759t^16+t^24.
The integer lattice consists of vectors x with common coordinate parity
e, with ((x-e)/2) mod 2 in C and sum(x) congruent to 4e modulo 8.
These conditions are closed under addition, since C is linear and
contains the all-one word.

If e=0 and the Golay residue is nonzero, at least eight coordinates have
magnitude at least 2, giving squared norm >=32. If that residue is zero,
coordinates are multiples of 4; the sum condition forbids a single
coordinate +/-4, again giving minimum >=32. If e=1, all coordinates
are odd. Norm below 32 would force all magnitudes to be one, but then
the Golay weight is divisible by four and sum(x) is 0 modulo 8,
contradicting sum(x)=4 modulo 8. Hence every nonzero vector has norm >=32.

REPRODUCTION

Run python verify.py from this directory, or pass its full path.
NumPy is required. All mathematical checks use integers and exact
rational intervals. No numerical search, tolerances, or unverified
floating-point Gram matrix enters the proof. verification.json records
the successful run and certificate hash.
