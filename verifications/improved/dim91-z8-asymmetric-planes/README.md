# Signed-diagonal doubling of Z8 fibers in dimension 91

**25,915,131,904 kissing points**, an increase of **3,555,326,110** (about **15.90%**) over the [previous 22,359,805,794 construction](https://github.com/alexlegeartis/KissingNumbers/tree/f388441f74d4d72903eb1aba504dc08e58122824/verifications/improved/dim90-91-a2-rotated-square). This is a lower bound, not a claim of optimality.

This construction replaces the earlier 91D points. It uses 88 ordinary coordinates and three head coordinates, keeping only the independently verified sparse layer transferred from the 88/89D proof.

## Explicit 24-fiber rule

Each of three colors uses one of the three pinned Z8 row alphabets (even Walsh, odd Walsh, or axial). The first two blocks are deleted, leaving a 22-row body of squared norm 88. Fixing the two deleted symbols gives exactly (8^{10}) words. Before deletion the same-color norm-96 words have mutual dot at most 48 by the pinned homogeneous minimum 24.

Assign to each color a different coordinate plane in the three-dimensional head. Use first prefix symbol 0. For its four labels (b=0,1,2,3), use the four high signed diagonals (u_b=(pm2,pm2)) in that plane in lexicographic order. For label (b+4), use the low diagonal (-u_b/4). The full squared radii are 96 and (177/2), respectively. Normalize each fiber by its own radius.

In a same-color opposite prefix pair the ordinary dot is at most 48, and the head contribution is −2, yielding raw cap 46. The exact squared comparison is (4cdot46^2=8464<96cdot177/2=8496). Distinct nonopposite labels have body dot at most 44. Two high heads in the same color have dot ≤0, and two low heads in the same color also have dot ≤0; a high-low head pair has dot at most 2. For distinct colors, the body dot is at most 44 and the planes have only one coordinate in common. The head maxima are 4 for two high heads, 1 for high and low, or (1/4) for two low heads. These give normalized dots at most (1/2), including equality for two high or two low heads of different colors. Within a fiber the body dot is at most 40 and the head squared norm ≤8, also giving cap (1/2). Every distinct indexed pair is covered, implying injectivity and total (24cdot 8^{10}=25,769,803,776) dense points.

## Sparse transport

Place the [982 weight-11 and 11 weight-10 support-pattern fibers](../dim88-89-golay-variable-punctures/) in the ordinary 22 blocks, at zero in the new head. Each active row has raw dot at most 4 against any dense body row, so the normalized inequality follows from (4(4w)^2le8w(88+|h|^2)). The dense body uses every row; the sparse stock does not. This adds 145,004,544 points, distinct from the dense code. No earlier 91D stock is included.

## Exact replay and extension rule

The frozen `heads.json` lists twice each physical head. `python3 verify.py` exactly reconstructs the rule, checks all 276 pairs by signed squaring, binds the published Z8 parent (whose proven minimum is inherited), and reruns the sparse proof with its pinned input in the normal sibling layout. It also verifies the analogous 4D and 5D head tables generated by `heads_analytic.py`: partition all coordinate-axis edges into three bipartite classes and double each singleton class by the low-antipodal rule. The 92D example uses two disjoint singleton edges and a (K_{2,2}) class; the 93D example uses one singleton edge and two classes obtained by identifying its endpoints in a two-bit edge partition. This reproduces 32 and 44 fibers respectively; those two dimensions’ registered totals are handled separately.

## Flat hexacode appendix

An additional 323,584 points use 79 six-row supports in flat_supports.json, pairwise meeting in at most three rows, none contained in the support of any retained sparse pattern. On each support take two complementary parity patterns (three even and three odd flat rows). The four projective axes in each parity class are the sign vectors in four coordinates modulo overall sign, with raw entries of magnitude one. Choose the axis sequence with the length-six quaternary hexacode and choose an even-parity overall sign on the six axes. The 64 axis words and 32 sign words are checked directly.

The squared radius is 24. New points from different supports have dot at most 12; opposite parity assignments on the same support also have dot at most 12. Within a parity assignment distinct axis words share at most two axes, while different signs on the same axes disagree at least twice. Dense rows contribute at most 18 across the six slots by the balanced parity assignment; retained sparse patterns meet at most five rows, giving dot at most 20. These satisfy the normalized cap for all old radii. Occupied-row counts distinguish all three structures.

Run python3 verify_flat.py (Node.js for the inherited sparse proof) for the current published count in claims.json. It replays verify.py and the count in claims_base.json before checking all flat supports, code distances and layer geometry.
