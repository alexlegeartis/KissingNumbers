A 501,147-point kissing configuration in dimension 36
24 October 2026 (Moscow time; investigation started 23 October)

Statement
---------
The finite certificate in certificate.json defines 501,147 vectors in R^36,
each of squared norm 12, with distinct-pair squared distances at least 12.
Consequently their normalized inner products are at most 1/2, and

                         tau(36) >= 501147.

This exceeds the 484,760 entry retrieved from Henry Cohn's table on
23 October 2026 by 16,387 points, approximately 3.38 percent. It is a locally
verified construction; this package makes no claim of external publication
or independent third-party review.

The construction uses three Coxeter--Todd lattices and a nonlinear code in
their ternary quotient groups. It does not use the signed weight-eight
supports or the binary [32,17,8] component of the previous search.

1. Lattice and quotient
----------------------
Let K be the rank-12 lattice with Gram matrix G in certificate.json.
It is the Coxeter--Todd Gram matrix from the Nebe--Sloane catalogue:
https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/LATTICES/K12.html
Coordinates below are row vectors in this Gram basis: |x|^2 = x G x^T.

Let M be the integer matrix in the certificate and L = Z^12 M in K.
The verifier checks

                  M G M^T = 3G,       |det M| = 3^6.

It proves G positive definite by rational LDL decomposition and enumerates
ALL vectors of squared norm at most 8, using exact rational branch bounds.
The nonzero shell populations are

                  squared norm       population
                        4                 756
                        6                4032
                        8               20412.

In particular, min(K)=4. The displayed similarity proves min(L)=12 and
that L has 756 minimal vectors. These facts are checked from the supplied
matrix, rather than assumed from the catalogue.

Define rho: Z^12 -> F_3^6 by

 rho(x) = (x_0+x_3, x_1+x_4, x_2+x_5,
           x_6+x_9, x_7+x_10, x_8+x_11) mod 3.

This map is onto. Every row of M lies in its kernel. Both its kernel and L
have index 3^6, so ker(rho)=L. We encode a label (h_0,...,h_5) as the integer
sum_i h_i*3^i, with h_i in {0,1,2}.

For a label h, let w(h) be its coset's minimum squared norm, and let m(h)
be the number of vectors of this minimum norm. For h=0 these conventions
include the zero vector, so w(0)=0 and m(0)=1. Exact enumeration gives

             w(h)    m(h)    number of labels
               0       1             1
               4       3           252
               6      18           224
               8      81           252.

Every nonzero label occurs in the enumeration, so it proves every entry
and every minimum in this table. No coset lower bound is extrapolated
from sampled vectors.

2. General gluing lemma
----------------------
Let C be a set of distinct triples of labels, containing (0,0,0), such that

  (a) w(a)+w(b)+w(c)=12 for every nonzero (a,b,c) in C;
  (b) w(a-a')+w(b-b')+w(c-c') >= 12 for distinct triples in C.

All differences here are componentwise in F_3^6.

For each nonzero triple, include every vector (x,y,z) in K^3 for which
rho(x)=a, rho(y)=b, rho(z)=c and each component has its coset's minimum norm.
Also include the 3*756 minimal vectors of L^3, of squared norm 12.

Every included point has squared norm 12. Points from different cosets
have squared distance at least the sum in (b). Distinct points from the
same coset differ by a nonzero vector of L^3, whose minimum norm is 12.
Thus all distinct-pair squared distances are at least 12.

The resulting point count is exactly

             3*756 + sum_{(a,b,c) in C, (a,b,c)!=0} m(a)m(b)m(c).

Finally |v-v'|^2 = 24-2<v,v'> gives <v,v'> <= 6. Dividing all vectors by
sqrt(12) gives a unit spherical code with inner products at most 1/2.
This proves the kissing-number claim in ordinary Euclidean dimension 36:
G is positive definite and the ambient Gram matrix is diag(G,G,G).

3. The explicit outer code and count
-----------------------------------
certificate.json supplies all 5,038 triples in C, including the zero triple.
The standalone verifier checks (a), every one of the 12,688,203 distinct
coset pairs in (b), all coset multiplicities, and the final count.

The population separates as follows:

       type                          cells    points/cell       points
       minimal vectors of L^3          --          --             2268
       cyclic (0,6,6) types           672          324          217728
       cyclic (0,4,8) types           756          243          183708
       (4,4,4) types                 3609           27           97443
                                                                ------
                                                                501147

Here the type records component squared norms, and the cyclic locations
are included in the actual list. The proof depends only on the explicit
code and the verified gluing lemma, not on how the code was discovered.

4. How the code was found
------------------------
This section explains the structure; it is not an extra assumption in
the verification.

The quotient has a quadratic form q(h)=|x|^2/2 mod 3 for rho(x)=h.
Its values 2, 0, and 1 correspond to nonzero coset weights 4, 6, and 8.
An explicit linear map A, recorded in discovery.json, satisfies

          A Q A^T = -Q,       A^2 = -I       over F_3,

where Q represents q in row-vector convention. Thus A exchanges coset
weights 4 and 8 and preserves nonzero weight 6. Pairing a coset h with
hA gives the familiar 196,560-point 24D Leech-shell count:

          2*756 + 224*18^2 + 2*252*3*81 = 196560.

Take these pairs in each of the three coordinate planes of K^3. Keep
all balanced (6,6) cells and one cyclic orientation of the (8,4) cells.
Together with the L^3 minima, these give 403,704 compatible points.

For a triple of weight-four labels (a,b,c), compatibility with this fixed
part is equivalent to

              w(a-bA)=w(b-cA)=w(c-aA)=8.

There are 729,000 such triples. An unstructured search found only 2,643
mutually compatible ones, yielding 475,065 points.

The useful change was imposing the algebraic relation

                        a + bA + c = 0.

There are 8,100 admissible triples in this slice. If two triples share a
or c, the other two differences are related by A, so their weights sum
to 12. Only pairs sharing b can conflict. For each fixed b, select a
subset of possible a-values whose distinct differences have weight at
least 6. The problem therefore separates into 252 small graphs.

The selected independent-set sizes are

                 90*10 + 90*18 + 72*14 = 3528.

These choices give the structured 498,960-point configuration. A greedy
extension in the full pool adds 81 further cosets, each worth 27 points,
giving 501,147. The 81 additional triples are also listed in discovery.json.

5. Reproduction and coordinate conventions
------------------------------------------
Run

                         python verify.py

with Python 3.9 or newer. No third-party libraries, web access, search,
optimization solver, or randomness are required. verification.json is
the machine-readable output and includes a SHA-256 of certificate.json.

vectors.npz is an optional explicit coefficient list, with 501,147 rows
and 36 columns, and its exact 36-by-36 Gram matrix. It is provided for
inspection and further work; verify.py reconstructs the certificate's
meaning without trusting this auxiliary file. These integer coefficients
are in a Gram basis, not orthonormal Cartesian coordinates. If G36=L L^T
is a Cholesky factorization, the corresponding ordinary unit vectors are

                         coefficients @ L / sqrt(12).

6. Immediate consequence in dimension 37
----------------------------------------
Embed the unit configuration in the equator of S^36 and add the two poles.
The poles have inner product zero with every equatorial point and inner
product -1 with each other. Therefore

                          tau(37) >= 501149.

The retrieved Cohn table lists 500,008 in 37D, so this also exceeds that
snapshot by 1,141. The construction itself is the 36D result above.
