Exact improvements in dimensions 36 and 37, and an obstruction to sections
24 October 2026

Results
-------
The attached finite certificate proves

                       tau(36) >= 501633,
                       tau(37) >= 505359.

All vectors have squared norm 12 and distinct-pair squared distance at
least 12. Normalization therefore gives inner products at most 1/2.
These are locally verified constructions, with no claim of publication
or independent third-party review.

The comparison is with Henry Cohn's table downloaded on 23 October 2026:
36D: 484760, giving a gain of 16873;
37D: 500008, giving a gain of 5351.
The live page returned HTTP 502 during the follow-up investigation.
https://cohn.mit.edu/kissing-numbers/

1. Exactly enumerated quotient geometry
--------------------------------------
Let K have the positive definite, rank-12 integer Gram matrix G recorded
in certificate.json. Coefficients are row vectors, with squared norm
x G x^T. The verifier proves positive definiteness using rational LDL
decomposition and exhaustively enumerates every vector of norm at most 8.
The shell populations are 756, 4032, and 20412 at norms 4, 6, and 8.

The integer matrix M satisfies M G M^T = 3G and |det M| = 729. Thus the
sublattice L with coefficient rows Z^12 M has minimum 12 and 756 minimal
vectors. The label map rho: Z^12 -> F_3^6 is the coordinate-pair sum map
specified in the certificate. Its kernel is L: it is onto, its kernel
contains all rows of M, and both indices are 729.

Labels are encoded in little-endian base 3. Exact enumeration proves the
following minimum squared norms w and multiplicities m in K/L:

    w      m      number of cosets
    0      1             1
    4      3           252
    6     18           224
    8     81           252.

The zero coset's entry here refers to its zero vector; its nonzero minimum
is 12. Coset subtraction is coordinatewise over F_3.

For any two body vectors with coset triples c and d, their squared distance
is at least D(c,d) = sum_{j=1}^3 w(c_j-d_j). Distinct vectors in one triple
of cosets differ by a nonzero vector of L^3, so their distance is at least
12. These two facts are the main certification shortcuts.

2. Fixed part: 403704 points
---------------------------
The certificate lists 1429 base cosets, including zero. The 1428 nonzero
ones have total minimum norm 12; all their pair distances D are at least
12. Include all products of component minimum vectors in these cosets,
and the 2268 minimal vectors of L^3 in the zero coset. The count is

             2268 + 672*324 + 756*243 = 403704.

The nonzero types are (0,6,6) and cyclic (0,4,8). This is the same fixed
part used in the earlier 501147-point construction.

3. Finer phase choices: 97929 additional points
---------------------------------------------
Set Phi = I-M. The verifier checks Phi^3=I and Phi G Phi^T=G.
Every weight-four coset consists of a triangle

                        x_a, x_a Phi, x_a Phi^2.

The certificate supplies x_a for each of the 252 labels, in increasing
label order, and checks each triangle against the exhaustive enumeration.

An entry (a,b,c,p,q) in phase_orbits represents the three points

       (x_a Phi^t, x_b Phi^(p+t), x_c Phi^(q+t)),    t=0,1,2.

Each component has norm 4, so these points have total norm 12. Each
27-point product cell has nine such three-point orbits. Allowing some
orbits of a cell without requiring all nine is the new freedom.

The certificate contains 32643 distinct orbits, contributing 97929 points.
Different orbit entries represent disjoint points: the first component's
phase determines t and hence the other two relative phases p and q.

Compatibility with the fixed part is checked by coset distances for every
one of the 3979 outer triples used. For two triples with all three labels
different, D is automatically at least 4+4+4=12. For every remaining pair
of orbits, the verifier computes all three relative phase inner products
using the exact Gram matrix, and verifies that each is at most 6.

This gives

                       403704 + 3*32643 = 501633.

The search started from the previous 3609 complete product cells. Its net
gain is 486 points. Removing neutral exchanges leaves an alternative
description with 18 independent beneficial exchanges: 683 old three-point
orbits are replaced by 845 new ones. The packaged 37D extension uses the
original search output, rather than this simplified alternative.

4. Two latitude layers in dimension 37
-------------------------------------
The certificate additionally lists 207 body cosets with component norms
(4,4,0), in any coordinate order. Each gives 3*3=9 body vectors of norm 8.
The verifier checks D >= 12 between these body cosets, and D >= 8 between
each of them and every equatorial coset used above, including zero.

For every body vector v, include the two 37D vectors (v,2) and (v,-2).
Their squared norms are 8+4=12. At the same height, distances are at least
12 by the coset checks and the minimum of L^3. Opposite heights alone
contribute 16 to squared distance.

For any equatorial vector x of norm 12, the body distance bound gives

                    <v,x> <= (8+12-8)/2 = 6.

Its last coordinate is zero, so this proves compatibility with the
equator. The total is

                       501633 + 2*207*9 = 505359.

This construction uses actual latitude layers, not just the two poles.

5. All sections in dimensions 33--35 fall short
----------------------------------------------
The fixed base B of 403704 points satisfies exact moment identities. Write
an ordinary Euclidean normal as u=(u_1,u_2,u_3), with 12D components. Then

 sum_{x in B} <u,x>^2 = 134568 |u|^2,

 sum_{x in B} <u,x>^4 = 95256 |u|^4 + 87480 sum_j |u_j|^4.

verify_sections.py checks these identities with integer moment sums. It
checks all 1365 symmetric fourth-order coefficients in a block and all
20736 coefficients for each pair of blocks. Means of individual nonzero
cosets vanish, eliminating the odd mixed terms.

Let P be an orthogonal projection of rank r onto the orthogonal complement
of a proposed section. For y_x = x^T P x >= 0, tensor contraction gives

 sum y_x = 134568*r,

 sum y_x^2 = 31752*(r^2+2r)
             +29160*sum_j [(tr P_jj)^2+2 tr(P_jj^2)]
           <= 60912*r*(r+2).

Here P_jj are the three diagonal 12-by-12 blocks. The inequality uses
sum_j tr P_jj=r, 0<=P_jj<=I, sum_j (tr P_jj)^2<=r^2, and
sum_j tr(P_jj^2)<=r.

Cauchy--Schwarz forces at least

             ceil((134568*r)^2 / (60912*r*(r+2)))

base points outside the section. Even allowing every one of the remaining
97929 points to lie in it yields these section cardinality upper bounds:

    dimension    section at most    Cohn snapshot lower bound
       35            402536                  415308
       34            352987                  390980
       33            323258                  370240.

The base is antipodal, also checked exactly. An affine section avoiding
the origin can contain at most half of B, hence at most
201852+97929=299781 total points. Thus affine sections do not circumvent
this obstruction.

These are restrictions on sections of this particular configuration.
They are not upper bounds for kissing numbers, or obstructions to
projection with subsequent deformation or entirely new constructions.

6. Reproduction
---------------
Run python verify.py using Python 3.9 or newer, with no third-party
dependencies. It verifies both lower bounds without a solver or a search.
The recorded run checked 1020306 base coset pairs, 5685991 phase/base
coset pairs, 6563399 phase-orbit pairs, 21321 lift/lift coset pairs, and
1119456 lift/equator coset pairs, together with exact lattice enumeration.

Run python verify_sections.py to reproduce the section obstruction;
this optional check additionally requires NumPy for integer contractions.

vectors36.npz and vectors37.npz contain the explicit distinct integer
coefficient vectors and their Gram matrices. They are not Cartesian
coordinates. To obtain ordinary unit vectors, use

          coordinates @ numpy.linalg.cholesky(gram) / sqrt(12).

The certificate, rather than these auxiliary coordinate files, is the
input to the standalone lower-bound verifier.
