# A parity-coordinated twelve-head construction in dimension 35

All coordinate indices below start at zero. Coordinates 0, 1, and 2 are the
extra coordinates; the binary dense code occupies coordinates 3 through 34.

## Starting layers

Use the 2,202 weight-eight supports of Qihong Lin's October 2026 code from
[Brouwer's code table](https://aeb.win.tue.nl/codes/Andw.html), supplied in
[`a35.8.8.2202H`](https://aeb.win.tue.nl/codes/cwc/d8/a35.8.8.2202H).
Every two distinct supports intersect in at most four coordinates.

For each support S, take all 128 vectors with entries ±2 on S and zero
elsewhere, prescribing the parity p(S) of the number of negative entries.
This parity may be chosen separately for each support. Every such vector has
squared norm 32. Same-support vectors differ in at least two signs and have
dot product at most 16. Different-support vectors have dot product at most
16 from their support intersection bound.

Take the 131,072 sign vectors from the supplied binary [32,17,8] linear code
C, shifted by r = 1,605,954 and embedded in coordinates 3 through 34. Exact
enumeration verifies that C has minimum nonzero weight eight and C+r has
weights from six through 26. These dense vectors have squared norm 32 and
mutual dot products at most 16. Their dot products with the middle layer are
at most 16, independently of the chosen middle parities.

The root layer initially consists of all 4(±e_i ± e_j), i < j, with squared
norm 32. Root/root and root/middle dot products are at most 16; root/dense
dot products are at most eight.

## Coordinating the parities

Associate the following binary codewords with pairs of extra coordinates:

| Pair | Body codeword, as a 32-bit mask | Weight |
|---|---:|---:|
| (0,1) | 0 | 0 |
| (0,2) | 2,939,611,009 | 18 |
| (1,2) | 2,529,791,442 | 16 |

All three words belong to C. Write c_ab for the word belonging to pair (a,b).
If S contains a pair (a,b) of extra coordinates, set

`p(S) = popcount((S >> 3) & c_ab) mod 2`.

If S contains fewer than two extra coordinates, set p(S)=0. There are 13
supports containing all three extra coordinates. On each of their core
five-subsets, the inner product with both nonzero body codewords is zero.
Thus all applicable pair definitions agree. Exactly 59 support parities are
odd; the other 2,143 are even.

The constructor finds these codewords by solving the 13 binary orthogonality
equations inside C. The solution space has dimension four.

## The twelve heads and six deleted roots

For each pair (a,b), and each δ in {−1,+1}, form q_ab,δ as follows:

- In dense coordinate j+3, use (−1) raised to bit j of c_ab.
- At a and b, use 3δ and −3δ, respectively.
- At the remaining extra coordinate, use +1.

Include both signs of each of these six vectors: twelve heads in total.
Each has squared norm 32+9+9+1=51. Delete the six roots
±4(e_a−e_b), one antipodal pair for each of the three extra-coordinate pairs.

Every head has absolute dot product at most 20 with every dense vector:
adding c_ab to C+r leaves that coset unchanged, and its weights range from
six to 26.

For a middle support containing both heavy coordinates, the prescribed
parity differs from the parity of the head's negative coordinates on that
support. Consequently at least one sign must disagree when maximizing the
dot product. The unsigned sum of head magnitudes on the support is 12;
the parity condition lowers it to at most 10. Multiplying by the middle
coordinate magnitude two gives 20. Supports containing one or zero heavy
coordinates give bounds 20 and 16 directly.

Every retained root has absolute dot product at most 16 with every head.
The only roots with larger products are precisely the six deleted roots.
For two distinct non-antipodal heads, direct integer calculation gives
absolute dot product at most 17.

After normalization, all these products are at most one half:

```text
4 * 20^2 = 1600 <= 32 * 51 = 1632
4 * 16^2 = 1024 <= 32 * 51
4 * 17^2 = 1156 <= 51 * 51 = 2601
```

The number of points is therefore

```text
131072 + 2202*128 + (2*35*34 - 6) + 12 = 415314.
```

The certificate and both verifiers establish this lower bound using exact
integer comparisons. They do not establish optimality of the construction.
